# ENDO01A
## Final FLAGS

- ENDO-03: Riedel thyroiditis is not a recognised cause of destructive thyrotoxicosis. "Secondary hypothyroid: TSH low" is better written "low or inappropriately normal".
- ENDO-04: "1% of hospitalised hyperthyroid patients" is an older figure; US NIS 2016–2020 gives 2.5%, with about 72% women. Mortality of 10% matches Japan (10.7–11%); recent US in-hospital mortality is 3–5%. Not wrong for the exam.
- ENDO-07: Burch–Wartofsky "from 90" does not match ENDO-06 (99–109). Both versions circulate (MDCalc prints 90–109), so the deck is inconsistent rather than one card being wrong.
- ENDO-08: Methimazole "10–50 mg 6-hourly" is unclear and probably low (ATA 60–80 mg/day, JTA 60 mg/day). Lithium: some sources give 8-hourly. "PTU preferred" and "iodine ≥ 1 h after" are ATA 2016 positions; JTA 2016 accepts methimazole and gives iodide with the thionamide. JTA does not recommend propranolol for the tachycardia. The GT5 key stands, with esmolol equally supported. The card-layer "Killip ≥ III" wording is a simplification.
- ENDO-09: The card calls iodine "second line"; ATA and JTA both make it part of the initial regimen. The GT2o key (iodine) is defended over the qpack note's methimazole.
- ENDO-10 (new): The prevalence of 0.01% could not be traced (published incidence is about 0.22 per million/year in the West and 1.08 per million/year in Japan). Hypothermia "under 33 °C" is not a standard cut-off (reviews use below 35–35.5 °C). "TSH usually over 100" is not a defining criterion.
- ENDO-11: The ECG row leaves out T-wave flattening or inversion (DocT's key). "Hyponatraemia (high ADH)" gives only the secondary mechanism.
- T_2b8a9a37 (new): "Cuts type 1 and 2 deiodinase": the firm changes are type 1 down and type 3 up; type 2 changes are inconsistent. The "10–20" TSH overshoot is not a fixed range (usually below 20).
- T_38ffd8d5 (new): "Usually EUTHYROID" is overstated; most reported cases have subclinical or overt hypothyroidism. Methylprednisolone regimens run 3–7 days.

Could not open: the ATA 2016 and ATA 2014 full texts (PMC blocked by a browser check). Their doses were confirmed through secondary sources that cite them (Endotext, EMCrit). The exact dose of the ATA 2014 hydrocortisone recommendation was not seen; "about 100 mg 8-hourly" rests on Endotext and patient.info. The original 1993 Burch–Wartofsky paper was not seen, so the 90 vs 99 band cannot be settled.

# ENDO02A
## Final FLAGS
- GLY-01: the card gives hyperglycaemia as >180–200, but ADA's inpatient definition is >140 (which triggers HbA1c), and 180 is the insulin start. "Below 110 not recommended" is too low: SCCM 2024 advises against 80–139, so the floor is 140. ADA still allows 110–140 in selected patients.
- GLY-06: the card routes IL-6 only to the pituitary. IL-6 also stimulates the adrenal cortex directly, and TNF-α blunts the adrenal response to ACTH.
- GLY-11: "check HbA1c when >180" is wrong; ADA says when glucose is >140 or diabetes is known, if none was done in the past 3 months. No guideline uses HbA1c-tiered targets; LUCID and CONTROLING (stopped early, benefit unlikely and harm possible) showed no benefit.
- GLY-12: "then 4-hourly once stable" does not fit an IV infusion. ADA says every 30 min to 2 h; SCCM says ≤1-hourly during instability.
- GLY-18: "samples every 15 min" describes older devices; current sensors read every 1–5 min. SCCM 2024 makes no CGM recommendation. The AACE/ACE 2016 statement was not verified.
- GLY-21: the DocT GT (GrandT1) key of oral glucose for a confused ICU patient at 48 mg/dL is disputed; IV dextrose 25 g is the safer answer unless a safe swallow is explicit. Card a's "steroids" applies only to adrenal insufficiency.
- Top-up T_a674f960 (comment only, not a card error): the FDA also advises stopping metformin at the time of contrast for eGFR 30–60, liver disease, alcoholism, heart failure or intra-arterial contrast. The UKPDS late macrovascular benefit is fair.

# HY5
## Final FLAGS
- HEO-08: a "K below 3.3" is the ADA 2009 figure; the 2024 consensus delays insulin until K is above 3.5. d: 2024 gives bicarbonate only below pH 7.0. e: 2024 resolution is ketones <0.6 with pH ≥7.3 or bicarbonate ≥18, and the anion gap is no longer used.
- HEO-04: the table's "TACO within 6–12 h" and "Fever: No" should read: onset up to 12 h, ≥3 ISBT criteria, fever does not exclude TACO. Card layer: the GrandT1 Q43 type II key is wrong (type I is right); the AIM2 Q105 TRALI key needs an assumed transfusion.
- HEO-11: wording only. ACOG gives the load over 20–30 min and 2–4 g over 5 min for a recurrent seizure. The DocT "magnesium first" key with pulmonary oedema is the least-wrong single answer.
- HEO-02: the card agrees with AABB 2023 and AABB/ICTMG 2025. The neurosurgery figure of 100 is BSH. The GT7 6 g/dL key is the old ASA wording. The GT8 aplastic-anaemia prophylaxis key rests on the stem's "chemotherapy".
- HEO-03: card correct. DocT's "within 8 hours" is CRASH-2's enrolment window; the answer is 3 h. In the GT1o ratio question options B and C are printed identically.
- HEO-13: the card folds part d into line c. Tennessee is ≥600 / ≥70 / ≤100. ACOG still allows a 48-h steroid delay for stable HELLP before 34 weeks.
- HEO-14: card correct, and the DocT expectant-care key is outdated. The card-layer cross line misattributes "34+0–35+6 with recurrent bleeding" to ACOG/SMFM (that is their accreta timing; RCOG gives 34+0–36+6 for praevia with bleeding).
- HEO-15: card correct. Two keys are disputed: carboprost where ergometrine is equally valid, and misoprostol where methylergonovine is safe in a normotensive asthmatic.
- HEO-17: c "Anti-D 50 µg" for a 7-week ectopic is the traditional and exam answer but is now contested (NICE NG126: none before 12 weeks; ACOG 2024: may be forgone after abortion or loss before 12 weeks, ectopic not mentioned in its summaries). a: D-dimer is unreliable alone, but ESC 2019 accepts pregnancy-adapted algorithms. d: IDSA 2019 says 4–7 days; ACOG avoids nitrofurantoin in the first trimester only when an alternative exists.
- HEO-07: the card follows ATA 2016. JTA 2016 differs: either thionamide, iodide at the same time as the thionamide, β1-selective blockers rather than propranolol. The card's "why" misuses the term Jod-Basedow (strictly, that is iodine-induced hyperthyroidism).
