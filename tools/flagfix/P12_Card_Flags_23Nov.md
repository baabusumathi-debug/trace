# NEET cardiology packets — card flags (23 Nov)

The writers and fact-checkers found these problems in the card text itself while building P12. Nothing on the cards has been changed. Mark each ✅ fix / ❌ keep, and I will apply the ✅ ones as card corrections.

## Shock I (5)

- **CVS03|CS-05**: Card gives the ESC 2016 definition (SBP <90 mmHg with adequate volume). ESC 2021 (and the 2020 HFA statement) defines cardiogenic shock by hypoperfusion from primary cardiac dysfunction and no longer requires hypotension. The sign lists are still current. Lesson teaches the card and names the update.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS03|CS-08**: Arrest (A) modifier is the SCAI 2019 wording (CPR, including defibrillation). The 2022 update narrows it to cardiac arrest with concern for anoxic brain injury (coma), and its table gives typical lactate ≥2 for stage C and ≥8 (pH <7.2, base deficit >10) for stage E, which the card omits. Lesson teaches both.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS05B|ECHO-19**: Card lists RV hypertrophy among the secondary indices of raised RAP. The ASE 2025 right heart guideline lists RA enlargement, interatrial septum bulging into the LA, restrictive right-sided filling, tricuspid E/e′ >6 and hepatic vein systolic filling fraction <55% (diastolic predominance), not RVH; it uses them to move an indeterminate (8 mmHg) estimate to 3 or 15, and adds that 20 mmHg for an IVC >2.5 cm with <50% collapse plus dilated hepatic veins (table; the text says markedly enlarged). The three bands and the 2.1 cm / 50% cut-offs are unchanged. (Checked against the ASE 2025 guideline PDF.)  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS05B|ECHO-40**: Not wrong but mismatched with other cards: normal IVC given as <20 mm (FATE figure) while the RAP table uses 2.1 cm; E/e′ 'normal <8–10' is older teaching, current guidelines use average E/e′ >14 for raised filling pressure; 'TI <2.5 m/s' sits beside the ESC/ERS 2022 TR threshold of >2.8 m/s. 'TVI' on the card means tissue velocity imaging, easily misread as velocity–time integral.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS03|CS-11**: Question part b asks for 'the four non-ischaemic myocardial causes' but the answer list begins with chronic ischaemia. Wording of the question, not the facts, is inconsistent.  — ✅ / ❌

## Shock II (5)

- **CVS03|CS-26**: Card is the ESC 2016 bedside-profile algorithm (wet and cold with SBP <90: inotrope first, vasopressor if refractory). AHA 2017/SCAI and ACC/AHA 2025 practice starts norepinephrine first in hypotensive cardiogenic shock (as CS-31 on this deck says), and ESC 2021 reorganised acute HF into four presentations. Lesson teaches the card, reconciles it with CS-31 and gives a stem rule.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS03|CS-35**: Two unlabelled or rounded figures. '50.3% vs 63.1%' is the 6-month mortality (30-day was 46.7% vs 56.0%, p 0.11) but the card does not say so. 'Successful vs unsuccessful PCI 35% vs 80%' is rounded: the original SHOCK report (Hochman 1999) gives 38% vs 79%; the later PCI analysis (Webb 2003) gives 39% vs 85% at one year. Lesson teaches the card figure and names the original.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS04|CS-45**: 'Catheter diameter 9 Fr, peak flow up to 4.3 L/min' are Impella CP figures and the card does not name the model. The pump head is 14 Fr, so the sheath is 14 Fr; a reader can take 9 Fr as the sheath size, which contradicts the IABP (7–8 Fr) vs Impella (13–14 Fr) question on CS-42.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS08E|ACLS-85**: 'IABP needs a native rhythm' is the basis of GT7 Q95 (device in AF, key Impella CP). The key is narrower than the truth: TandemHeart and VA ECMO are also rhythm-independent. Card could say 'only the IABP needs a regular rhythm; Impella, TandemHeart and VA ECMO do not'.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS03|CS-31**: RV shock row still lists dopamine among the pressors (AHA 2017 table) although the classic row on the same card explains why dopamine is no longer an equal choice (SOAP II). Minor inconsistency; lesson names norepinephrine or vasopressin.  — ✅ / ❌

## ACS I (11)

- **CVS10B|MI-29**: Rows 1 and 3 are reversed. Troponin T binds tropomyosin and troponin I is the inhibitory subunit (card has them swapped). hs-cTnT (single manufacturer) has the single reference level, 14 ng/L; cTnI has multiple assay-dependent levels (card has them swapped). Lesson teaches the biochemistry and says the card is reversed.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS10B|MI-30**: 'CK-MB used for re-infarction' is older teaching. UDMI 2018 and the Marrow key use troponin: a >20% rise from a stable or falling value (symptom onset and 3-6 h later); CK-MB only where troponin is unavailable. The card does not mention cMyC or copeptin (DocT GT, 57%).  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS10B|MI-25**: 'ST depression ≥1 mm in 8 leads' is the ESC 2017 STEMI guideline wording. UDMI 2018 and ESC 2023 (and ESC 2020 NSTE-ACS) say ≥6 surface leads with ST elevation in aVR and/or V1. Lesson teaches both (6 current, 8 on the card). Verified against the UDMI 2018 and ESC 2023 full texts by the checker.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS10B|MI-27**: Stress (Takotsubo) cardiomyopathy appears only in the ST depression column; it classically presents with ST elevation mimicking STEMI (the Marrow false statement is 'ECG never shows ST elevation'). The ST depression column also repeats LVH and LBBB.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS10A|MI-19**: Free wall rupture is listed under 'new systolic murmur'; it usually presents as tamponade/PEA, not a murmur. 'Most common peri-infarct arrhythmia: NSVT' follows the notes; counted strictly, ventricular premature beats are commoner. Lesson gives a stem rule (VPBs if offered, otherwise NSVT).  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS10A|MI-09**: Card is UDMI 2018 and correct for it, but the shared 'evidence of new ischaemia' line overstates type 5: for CABG the 2018 list is new Q waves, new graft or native-vessel occlusion, or imaging (new ST-T change alone does not count). Under the Fifth UDMI (2026) procedure-related MI (any cardiac procedure, within 30 days) has unified criteria for PCI and surgery and fixed troponin multiples are no longer diagnostic; definitive diagnosis needs an angiographic coronary complication and/or a new RWMA or loss of viable myocardium (both if during the procedure or for acute MI). Checked against secondary reports (TCTMD, EMJ Reviews Oct 2026), not the full JACC/EHJ text.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS10A|MI-05**: Type 2 'usually without significant CAD' is loose: many type 2 patients have fixed CAD; the defining feature is no acute plaque disruption or thrombus. Types 1-5 were replaced by primary/secondary/procedure-related in the Fifth UDMI (ESC, August 2026); bank keys still use the numbers. Fifth UDMI confirmed (ACC, ESC, Healio, TCTMD; published 28 Aug 2026, JACC and EHJ).  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS10A|MI-08**: Type 3 is retired in the Fifth UDMI (2026): cardiac death before testing is classified by setting or post-mortem findings as primary, secondary or procedure-related (TCTMD; EMJ Reviews). GT8 Q21 still keys type 3. The 2026 section on MI after sudden death was not read in full text, so the lesson does not state its criteria.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS10B|MI-22**: Thresholds differ slightly from UDMI 2018: V7-V9 ≥0.5 mm (≥1 mm in men <40); V3R/V4R ≥0.5 mm (≥1 mm in men <30). The card's 1 mm in V4R is the classic, more specific cut. Lesson gives both.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS10B|MI-32**: The 0 h/3 h pathway (ESC 2015; IIa in 2020) is only a fallback in ESC 2023, which recommends 0 h/1 h or 0 h/2 h (I, B). Lesson teaches the card's logic and its current standing.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS10A|MI-16**: Not a card error: the linked DocT QBank key (ibuprofen and reassurance for exertional pain with sternal tenderness and a family history, 45% right) is unsafe practice. Lesson teaches the safe answer (ECG + troponin if offered) and names the bank's key.  — ✅ / ❌

## Rhythm (7)

- **HY2|CAR-03**: Part b's banned list for pre-excited AF (adenosine, verapamil, diltiazem, β-blockers, digoxin) omits IV amiodarone, which ACC/AHA 2023 and AHA 2025 class as harm (COR 3) and which ACLS-33 already lists. Suggest adding it.  — ✅ / ❌
- **HY2|CAR-06**: Part d says 'ICD for survivors'. Suggest making it explicit: ICD is class I after a survived arrest (GT7 Q130, missed) and is also indicated after arrhythmic syncope (Marrow); quinidine only if the ICD is refused or for recurrent shocks. Part a calls magnesium the drug of choice; AHA 2025 grades it COR 2b (may be considered) for recurrent torsades. The NEET answer is unchanged.  — ✅ / ❌
- **HY2|CAR-05**: Part b uses the 48-hour line (exam answer; ACC/AHA 2023). ESC 2024 moved it to 24 hours and replaced CHA₂DS₂-VASc with CHA₂DS₂-VA. Card is not wrong for NEET; consider a one-line note. The score itself was on no card (now V12-015).  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS08B|ACLS-26**: The card lists 100 J for narrow-complex tachycardia and monomorphic VT as '2025 energies'. The AHA 2025 guideline text gives a figure only for AF (≥200 J, 2a) and flutter (200 J, 2b); the 100 J values come from the algorithm chart. Consider wording 'AF ≥200 J' (the card says 200 J).  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS08B|ACLS-31**: Card has no reduced-dose line. The pack's two explanations disagree: ACLS-31 says 3 mg via a central line or after transplant (Marrow key); CAR-02 says AHA 2025 '1 mg may be adequate'. Both are true (checked against the AHA 2025 Part 9 text); taught as 3 mg for the exam, 1 mg per AHA 2025. Covered by new card V12-016.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS08B|ACLS-38**: Withdrawn by the checker. The pack's 'post-ROSC adrenaline 0.1–0.5 mcg/kg/min' matches the AHA 2015/2020 post-cardiac-arrest algorithm (epinephrine 0.1–0.5 mcg/kg/min, norepinephrine 0.1–0.5 mcg/kg/min), so it is not an error. Leaving it out of the lesson is still right: the bradycardia dose is 2–10 mcg/min.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS08B|ACLS-35**: SA exit block (type I vs type II vs sinus pause) was asked in a DocT GT (75%) and is on no card. Section P12-505 is already at 8 steps, so I taught it inside this lesson with its own takeaway and did not add a card. Suggest a V12 card or an added line if the section limit allows.  — ✅ / ❌

## Cardiac drugs (6)

- **HY2|CAR-21**: Andexanet alfa is listed first for Xa inhibitors with no caveat. AstraZeneca ended US sales of Andexxa on 22 Dec 2025 after an FDA safety communication on thromboembolic events (ANNEXA-I). Still the exam key and still licensed elsewhere; card could add 'withdrawn in the US Dec 2025: 4F-PCC in practice'. Lesson teaches the exam answer first, then the change.  — ✅ / ❌
- **NEURO04|NEURO-92**: Same andexanet point as CAR-21. Also the enoxaparin row gives only '1 mg per 1 mg' with no time rule (CAR-21 has: 1 mg/mg under 8 h, 0.5 mg/mg at 8–12 h, none after 12 h).  — ✅ / ❌
- **HY4|NEU-09**: 'Cerebellar haematoma above 3 cm' is the older (AHA 2015) criterion; AHA/ASA 2022 uses a volume of 15 mL or more, neurological deterioration, brainstem compression or hydrocephalus. Roughly the same clot; exams still key 3 cm. Lesson gives both.  — ✅ / ❌
- **TOX01|TOX-14**: Card says high-dose insulin '1 U/kg bolus, then 1–10 U/kg/h' (correct, AHA 2023) but its stored explanation heading says '0.5–1 U/kg/h': the explanation should match the card. Beta-blocker row ranks glucagon before high-dose insulin; AHA 2023 makes high-dose insulin class 1 and glucagon 2a (exam still keys glucagon as the classic answer).  — ✅ / ❌
- **HY2|CAR-14**: Line b 'lipid emulsion if refractory' (and the TOX-15 bank line on lipid for propranolol/verapamil): AHA 2023 focused update recommends against routine lipid emulsion in beta-blocker and CCB poisoning (class 3, no benefit); VA-ECMO is 2a. DocT still keys lipid 1.5 mL/kg as the rescue. Lesson teaches the exam key and states the guideline position.  — ✅ / ❌
- **TOX01|TOX-15**: Bank line keys lipid 1.5 mL/kg as the rescue for refractory propranolol; AHA 2023 says lipid is not likely beneficial in beta-blocker poisoning and routine use is not recommended in CCB poisoning (lipid is class 1 only for local anaesthetic toxicity). Lesson keeps the exam key and states the guideline.  — ✅ / ❌

## Pericardium, myocarditis, endocarditis (12)

- **CVS01|CVS-05**: Says ACS pain is the 'stabbing type'. It is the reverse: pericarditic pain is sharp/stabbing and pleuritic; ischaemic pain is pressure-like. Lesson teaches the correct contrast.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS01|CVS-10**: ESC 2015 rule (2 of 4) only. ESC 2025 (myocarditis and pericarditis, 'inflammatory myopericardial syndrome') starts from typical pain plus additional criteria (one = possible, more than one = definite) and counts raised CRP and CMR inflammation as criteria. Lesson teaches 2015 first, then the change.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS01|CVS-18**: Recurrence ladder is ESC 2015 (steroid second, anakinra third). ESC 2025 gives IL-1 blockers (anakinra, rilonacept) a Class I recommendation for steroid-dependent, colchicine-resistant recurrent pericarditis with raised CRP. Also '17% constriction with ATT' is the best figure from older series (range 17–40%).  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS01|CVS-24**: Gives prolonged RA collapse as 94% sensitive / 100% specific (Gillam 1983, single series), which conflicts with CVS-26 (RA collapse 68% / 66%) and with the DocT key that early diastolic RV collapse is the most specific sign. Lesson teaches the exam convention.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS01|CVS-27**: Lists post-MI free-wall rupture as an absolute contraindication; ESC names aortic dissection as the major contraindication and prefers surgery for rupture and traumatic haemopericardium (a small controlled drainage can bridge to theatre). Exam answer unchanged.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS02|CVS-42**: Card is fine, but the linked DocT GT explanation says LGE extent does not predict events; current evidence and ESC 2025 say it does. Lesson teaches the key (yield highest after about 7 days) and notes the dated explanation.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS02|CVS-43**: Marrow's key that 'no EF improvement after optimal HF therapy' is not an EMB indication is disputed (AHA/ACC/ESC 2007 and ESC 2025 count failure to respond as an indication).  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS02|CVS-46**: 'No recovery within 4 weeks → transplantation': no guideline gives a fixed interval; ESC defers transplant in the acute phase and considers it when the patient cannot be weaned from support. Marrow keys LVAD for refractory fulminant shock; the first device in practice is temporary (VA-ECMO ± microaxial pump).  — ✅ / ❌
- **TU-IE|IE-08**: Vegetation rows use ESC 2015 wording. ESC 2023: vegetation ≥10 mm with any other surgical indication (including severe valve disease) is Class I; the separate ≥30 mm cut-off was dropped; isolated ≥10 mm without embolism or severe dysfunction is IIb. Persistent positive cultures >1 week is IIa.  — ✅ / ❌
- **TU-IE|IE-10**: Penicillin-allergy row gives vancomycin + ceftriaxone; the ESC 2023 empirical table lists cefazolin or vancomycin with gentamicin for that group.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS01|CVS-15**: Aspirin dose given as 500–1000 mg every 6–8 h (review range). ESC 2015 and 2025 tables give 750–1000 mg every 8 h; lesson teaches the ESC dose.  — ✅ / ❌
- **TU-IE|IE-03**: Minor criteria list the classic five only. Duke-ISCVID 2023 has seven groups: add PET-CT uptake within 3 months of surgery and, when echo is unavailable, a new regurgitant murmur on auscultation.  — ✅ / ❌

## Right heart & PH (12)

- **CVS06A|RVF-01**: Part a gives 2–9% of acute heart failure admissions; the source usually quoted (ESC/HFA acute RV failure statement, Harjola 2016) and card RVF-02 give 3–9%. Suggest 3–9%.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS06A|RVF-20**: Defines PH as mean PA pressure >25 mmHg (and labels it 'MAP'). ESC/ERS 2022: mPAP >20 mmHg; pre-capillary adds PAWP ≤15 and PVR >2 WU. DocT still keys >25. - **CVS06A|RVF-21**: Group 5 list is the older one: splenectomy, vasculitis, thyroid disorders and congenital heart disease are no longer in group 5 (ESC/ERS 2022: haematological, systemic, metabolic, chronic renal failure, tumour thrombotic microangiopathy, fibrosing mediastinitis). - **CVS06A|RVF-17**: TGA is filed under volume overload; a systemic RV (atrial-switch d-TGA, ccTGA) is mainly pressure loaded. Low exam relevance.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS06B|RVF-25**: Part a reads 'enlarged main PA and regional oligaemia → pulmonary oedema; clear lung fields', which is self-contradictory. Isolated RV failure has clear lung fields; oedema means left heart disease. Suggest adding the asked CT cut-off (main PA >3 cm, PA:aorta >1).  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS06B|RVF-28**: Lacks PAPi, which Marrow asked: (PASP − PADP)/RAP, <0.9 in inferior MI predicts RV failure and death (<1.85 after LVAD). Suggest adding one line.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS06B|RVF-30**: Says only 'seen in PE'. DocT GT keys the 60/60 sign as more specific than McConnell's sign for PE versus RV infarction. Suggest adding that McConnell's also occurs in RV infarction.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS06B|RVF-39**: Volume loading '>200 mL / 15–30 min' is the HFA/ESC 2016 acute RV failure statement figure (Harjola); ESC 2019 PE says cautious loading of ≤500 mL over 15–30 min. Both taught; suggest noting the 500 mL cap.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS06B|RVF-42**: Lists hypotension and hypoxia as adverse effects of inhaled prostaglandins; these belong mainly to IV prostacyclin (inhaled is largely selective). Card also lacks the asked fact: abrupt iNO withdrawal causes rebound PH (wean to 1 ppm).  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS06B|RVF-36**: Lists IABP as mechanical support for RV failure; it has little role for the RV (RVF-43 does not list it). DocT's 'lifelong anticoagulation in IPAH on epoprostenol' key is not supported by ESC/ERS 2022 (individualised).  — ✅ / ❌
- **HY2|CAR-24**: Lacks the Wells score, asked at GT8 Q10 (missed). New card V12-040 written; the items are taught in this step's lesson. Daniel score '≥10 = severe pulmonary hypertension' is better read as systolic PA pressure above about 50 mmHg.  — ✅ / ❌
- **HY2|CAR-23**: No error. Note for currency: the 2026 AHA/ACC acute PE guideline (JACC 2026) introduces clinical categories A–E (A subclinical to E cardiopulmonary failure) alongside the ESC classes; advanced therapy is reserved for the higher categories. NEET keys the ESC classes.  — ✅ / ❌

## Aorta (7)

- **CVS09|AD-10**: Defines Stanford A as 'proximal to the left subclavian origin' and B as 'distal'. Strictly, A = any involvement of the ASCENDING aorta (as the card's own last line says); an arch-only dissection is proximal to the subclavian but is non-A non-B (AD-11, ESC 2024). Suggest: 'A = ascending aorta involved, wherever the tear; B = ascending not involved (distal to the left subclavian)'. Lesson teaches the card's line as the usual exam wording and names the gap.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS09|AD-22**: 'Valve involved, AR, or dilated root >55 mm → simultaneous aortic valve replacement' overstates it. ESC 2024 (I, B) and ACC/AHA 2022: with normal leaflets the valve is RESUSPENDED; root replacement with a valved conduit is for extensive root destruction, root aneurysm or a genetic aortopathy (Marfan). Lesson teaches the card line plus the refinement.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS09|AD-16**: 'Widened mediastinum 50%': IRAD (Hagan 2000) found a widened mediastinum in ~62% and an abnormal aortic contour in ~50%; 12.4% of films were normal. Card also lacks the calcium sign (>5 mm, Marrow) and the double or irregular contour that Marrow asked. Lesson says 'half to two thirds'.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS09|AD-09**: No IIIa/IIIb subdivision, which a DocT GT asked (38% right), and no mapping of complications to type (DocT GT, 12%). Covered by new card V12-030; consider adding 'IIIa above the diaphragm, IIIb below' to AD-09.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS09|AD-21**: Says only 'cyanide toxicity, unsuitable for prolonged use' for nitroprusside and nothing on nitroglycerin's effects, though six bank questions test them (thiosulfate, thiocyanate level, methaemoglobinaemia). Covered by new card V12-032. Also: DocT GrandT1 keys nitroglycerin for the almond-breath vignette; the key is wrong (nitroprusside).  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS09|AD-17**: Card is correct (CT definitive; ESC 2024 note). The DocT QBank key linked to it ('TEE preferred' in a stable patient, 40%) is outdated; lesson teaches both and advises CT when the patient is stable.  — ✅ / ❌
- **CVS09|AD-19**: Not wrong. ESC 2024 names labetalol as the first-choice IV beta-blocker (I, B), while the DocT keys prefer esmolol (then nicardipine). Lesson teaches the keyed answer and notes labetalol alone is acceptable.  — ✅ / ❌
