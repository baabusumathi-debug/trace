## GT7-105 Arsenic — urinary speciation (key C, agree)
- Corrected the chronic/steady-state split: was "half DMA, a quarter MMA, remainder inorganic"; actual is roughly **60–80% DMA, 10–20% MMA, 10–20% inorganic**. https://www.cdc.gov/biomonitoring/Arsenic_BiomonitoringSummary.html
- Corrected urine thresholds: total urinary arsenic **>100 µg/L abnormal** (>50 µg/L linked to effects in chronically exposed groups); occupational BEI **35 µg/L** speciated at end of work week. Removed the invented "100 µg/24 h" figure. https://archive.cdc.gov/www_atsdr_cdc_gov/csem/arsenic/clinical_assessment.html
- Seafood: specified **48–72 seafood-free hours** before repeating, and that seafood arsenic can add hundreds to thousands of µg/L (same source).
- Added elimination half-life **2–4 days**; softened "arsenic leaves the blood within hours" to within a day or two; Mees lines **weeks to months** (not "some weeks"), and the caveat that hair/nail contamination gives false positives.
- Trimmed to stay inside the length rule. Links remain `[]` (no card covers arsenic speciation; the odour card is too thin for this question).

## GT7-109 Enoxaparin in renal impairment (key B, agree)
- Fixed a real error: the draft said fondaparinux "is avoided below about 20 mL/min" **and** that it is the preferred drug in this patient. It is **contraindicated below CrCl 30 mL/min** for both prophylaxis and treatment, so at 24 mL/min it is out — rewritten to teach that fondaparinux is the Class I choice for conservatively managed NSTE-ACS *except* at this clearance. https://www.drugs.com/pro/fondaparinux-sodium.html
- Framed the enoxaparin cut-off accurately: the licence reduces to **1 mg/kg once daily below CrCl 30**; texts/exam use the 15–30 band and avoid LMWH below ~15. Option D rewritten.

## GT7-111 Arsine gas (key C, agree)
- Mechanism corrected and completed: arsine is **oxidised in the red cell, depletes glutathione and peroxidises the membrane**, and G6PD deficiency worsens it.
- Chelation statement corrected: the draft said "where it is given, BAL is the agent". BAL is **not effective and not recommended** in acute arsine poisoning — now taught as the fact that separates arsine from all other arsenic exposures.
- Timeline corrected: haemolysis can continue **up to 96 h**, renal failure delayed **up to 72 h**; added mortality ~25% with modern care, and fluids/urine output with alkalinisation of debated benefit. https://wikem.org/wiki/Arsine_gas_exposure
