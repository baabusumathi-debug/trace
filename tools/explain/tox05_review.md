# TOX05 (snake bite) — independent review

Sources used: Indian MoHFW Standard Treatment Guidelines: Management of Snake Bite (Aug 2017, "STG 2017"); older Indian national protocol text (Singh & Singh, "Snake Bite: Indian Guidelines and Protocol", 2009 protocol); WHO SEARO Guidelines for the Management of Snake-bites (2nd ed. 2010 text for the syndromic approach and pituitary; 2016 2nd ed. executive summary for prophylactic adrenaline); de Silva et al., PLoS Med 2011; Suraweera et al., eLife 2020.

## Changes
- SNAKE-01 a: vague "survey-based estimates are higher still" → names STG 2017 (~45,900 deaths a year) and the Million Death Study (~58,000 a year). (STG 2017; eLife 2020)
- SNAKE-04 extra: invented threshold "PaCO₂ e.g. 60 mmHg" → "a rising PaCO₂". (no source found for the figure)
- SNAKE-05 b: chemosis and pituitary infarction stated without region → assigned to Myanmar Russell's viper; ptosis and dark urine to South India and Sri Lanka. (WHO SEARO 6.3)
- SNAKE-05 c: → WHO wording "cobra or king cobra". (WHO SEARO 6.3)
- SNAKE-05 e: lacked the discriminator → adds land bite with coagulopathy = Russell's viper, sea bite with normal clotting = sea snake, and notes WHO's non-Indian kraits. (WHO SEARO 6.3)
- SNAKE-07 a: "acute pituitary failure (and hypopituitarism later)" → anterior pituitary haemorrhagic infarction, acute shock and hypoglycaemia, then chronic hypopituitarism months to years later (loss of hair, amenorrhoea or testicular atrophy, hypothyroidism). (WHO SEARO 6.2; STG 2017)
- SNAKE-08 c: stated as fact that pressure "worsens local necrosis" in viper bites → reframed as the concern. Adds WHO 2016 (pressure pad for suspected elapid bites) and STG 2017 (crepe bandage only by trained staff, hospital 30 min to 3 h away). (WHO SEARO; STG 2017)
- SNAKE-08 traps: "Australian practice" trap refined. The tourniquet trap now says to remove the tourniquet only after antivenom has started and resuscitation is ready. (STG 2017; WHO SEARO)
- SNAKE-10 b: "judged at about an hour" → positive test means ≥50% recovery of ptosis at 1 hour; a decode line added for this. (STG 2017)
- SNAKE-11 d (factual): "It is not routine — kept for high-reaction settings or high-risk patients" was wrong → WHO 2016 recommends prophylactic SC adrenaline routinely unless the reaction rate is known to be low, and STG 2017 advises it before ASV except in known hypertensives. Trial named as de Silva, PLoS Med 2011; adds the mechanism; trap updated to match. (WHO SEARO 2016 exec summary; STG 2017; de Silva 2011)
- SNAKE-13 a: adds that the older protocol allows 8–10 vials, and the low-dose vs high-dose evidence: Indian studies found no extra benefit from higher doses, and STG 2017 says low-dose infusion is as effective as high-dose bolus (expert consensus). Adds haematoma as a reason against IM. (STG 2017; 2009 protocol)
- SNAKE-16 extra: "urine output above 100 mL/h" was presented as a guideline target → now attributed to the card, and adds "started within 24 hours". (STG 2017 FAD within 24 h)

Checked and correct as written: big four and ASV coverage (hump-nosed pit viper and king cobra not covered); RVV activates factors X and V, Echis activates prothrombin; β-bungarotoxin is a PLA₂; 20WBCT method, glass vs plastic, 6-hour repeat after ASV; ASV indications (INR >1.2, more than half the limb, digit, tender node; local swelling alone is not an indication); 10 vials; same dose in children; neurotoxic maximum of 20 vials; 6 mg Russell's venom per vial; average 63 mg (range 5–147); liquid ASV 2 years at 2–8 °C, lyophilised 5 years; no test dose; reaction timings (10–180 min, 1–2 h, 1–12 days); IM adrenaline 0.5 mg first (child 0.01 mg/kg); chlorphenamine 10 mg IV; response times (bleeding 15–30 min, clotting 3–9 h, postsynaptic paralysis improving from 30 min); paracetamol or tramadol, no NSAIDs; FFP only after ASV; fasciotomy at >40 mmHg after coagulopathy is corrected; ATN the commonest renal lesion; never diagnose brain death in envenoming.

## Final FLAGS
- SNAKE-01: 35,000–50,000 is the older WHO figure. STG 2017 gives ~45,900 and the Million Death Study ~58,000 a year. Source for "34% of proven bites" not found.
- SNAKE-02: "Hydrophidae" is obsolete (sea snakes are now in Elapidae, Hydrophiinae); sea snake venom is neurotoxic as well as myotoxic.
- SNAKE-03: hyaluronidase spreads venom; tissue destruction comes mainly from metalloproteinases, PLA₂ and cytotoxins.
- SNAKE-05 (revised; the writer's doubt was wrong): card matches WHO SEARO for India. Minor omissions: "cobra or king cobra"; syndrome 5 splits land bite with coagulopathy (Russell's) from sea bite without (sea snake); non-Indian kraits are also listed.
- SNAKE-09 (new): "every 30 min for 3 h, then hourly" is the 2009 protocol. STG 2017 says hourly for 3 h, then 6-hourly up to 24 h.
- SNAKE-10 (refined): STG 2017 gives atropine 0.6 mg then neostigmine 1.5 mg IV, then 0.5 mg with atropine every 30 min for 5 doses (child: atropine 0.05 mg/kg, neostigmine 0.04 mg/kg, repeat 0.01 mg/kg). Positive = ≥50% recovery of ptosis at 1 h. WHO uses IM (~0.02 mg/kg). The card's "0.5–2 mg IM" blends the test dose with the repeat dose.
- SNAKE-11 (new): omits that SC adrenaline premedication is now advised routinely (WHO 2016; STG 2017 except in hypertensives). STG gives lyophilised shelf life as 3–5 years.
- SNAKE-13 (new): STG 2017 regimens differ. Neurotoxic: 10 vials over 30 min, second 10 at 1 h, maximum 20. Haemotoxic low-dose: 10 (Russell's) or 6 (saw-scaled), then 2 vials 6-hourly. High-dose: 10, then 6 vials 6-hourly. "5–10 vials 6-hourly" is the older protocol.
- SNAKE-15: IM adrenaline 0.5 mg (child 0.01 mg/kg) is FIRST-line at the earliest sign of anaphylaxis, not "if severe".
- SNAKE-16 (new): the "urine above 100 mL/h" myoglobinuria target could not be confirmed. STG 2017 describes forced alkaline diuresis cycles expecting ~3 mL/min, within 24 h.
