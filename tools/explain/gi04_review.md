## Changes
- MES-03 a: added the sourced figures. The card's 10–35% is kept; the usual figures are about 25% at rest and 35% after a meal (Oldenburg, Arch Intern Med 2004), with about 30% to the whole hepatosplanchnic bed (Ackland, Crit Care 2000;4:269).
- MES-03 b: one sentence only restated the card. Added the evidence: in SMA-restriction models, mucosal pH falls only below about 50% of baseline flow, but supply dependency can start after a 30% fall in real hypovolaemia (Ackland 2000).
- MES-03 c (factual softening): the explanation said MAP 45 mmHg was the figure "for a gut with normal arteries". No primary source could be found, so it now says the figure is from the notes, could not be traced and is approximate, and gives the autoregulation reasoning.
- MES-03 d: the 75% for up to 12 h figure is now attributed to classical AMI reviews and collateral flow. It is consistent with the standard review literature (Oldenburg-era reviews, cited by WikiDoc).
- MES-05 b (minor): "falling as AF is anticoagulated more often" was an unsupported causal claim. It now reads "recent series put it lower, at about 25% (WSES 2022)".
- MES-06 a (unverified card figure): added that the 17% proximal aortic thrombus figure could not be traced. Acosta, Ann Surg 2005;241:516 (213 autopsies) found an embolic source in 80%, residual cardiac thrombus in 48% and synchronous emboli in 68%. It gives aortic wall thrombosis only as a borderline association with thrombotic occlusion.
- MES-10 c (unverified card figure): added that cut-offs depend on method. The Dutch tonometry group uses 0.8 kPa as the upper limit of the gastric–arterial gradient after exercise (Mensink PBF thesis, Erasmus 2006; Mensink, Dig Dis Sci 2007), which is close to the card's 0.9 kPa. The jejunal 1.4 kPa could not be traced.
- MES-10 d: added source support for "only valid test". Gastric or sigmoid tonometry is "the only proven diagnostic tool for detecting ischaemia at a reversible stage" (Mensink 2006), and its current use is mainly in chronic mesenteric ischaemia.
- MES-11 d (minor): resolved the apparent conflict with MES-08. "No oral contrast" applies to suspected AMI; ACG 2015 does use IV plus oral contrast CT in suspected colonic ischaemia.
- MES-14 b (factual softening): removed "endovascular share rose from about 12% to 30%" because the exact figures could not be confirmed. It now says the share rose over the study and that ESVS 2025 makes endovascular repair first-line without peritonitis.
- Header: added the reviewer's sources-checked lines.
