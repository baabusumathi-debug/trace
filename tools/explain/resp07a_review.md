# RESP07A review: independent fact-check (11 Nov)

## Changes
- PLEU-03 a (factual): added under the bold restatement that 24 and 9.8 per lakh a year are the English GP consultation rates for all pneumothorax (primary and secondary combined), not the incidence of primary pneumothorax. BTS 2010 gives PSP as 18–28 per lakh a year in men and 1.2–6 in women. Sources: Gupta et al., Thorax 2000;55:666 abstract ("primary and secondary combined"); MacDuff, BTS 2010 pneumothorax guideline (Thorax 2010;65 Suppl 2:ii18).
- PLEU-03 a (minor, sourced): "recurrence about one in three" → now gives pooled 32%, 29% within one year. Source: Walker et al., ERJ 2018 meta-analysis (UWE repository abstract).
- PLEU-04 c (factual): "a 5 cm cannula fails to reach the pleura in about one patient in five (and in most obese patients)" plus "ATLS suggests an 8 cm catheter" (both unsourced) → predicted 5 cm failure 38% at 2nd ICS MCL, 31% at 4th–5th ICS MAL, 13% at 4th–5th ICS AAL, rising with BMI; longer 7–8 cm catheters advised by many authors (not attributed to ATLS). ATLS 10th adult site (4th/5th ICS just anterior to MAL) and 2nd ICS MCL still accepted in children confirmed. Sources: Laan et al., Injury 2016 (via REBEL EM summary); Scand J Trauma Resusc Emerg Med 2019, doi 10.1186/s13049-019-0623-5 (ATLS 10th wording).
- PLEU-06 d (factual): OPTICC "respiratory distress was similar" → 142 patients; distress 38% observed vs 25% drained, not significant in an underpowered trial, mortality the same. "~40% of those ventilated more than 4 days" → 5 days or more. About a quarter of observed patients needing a drain confirmed. Source: OPTICC final report, Am J Surg 2021;221:1252 (EM-Consulte abstract).
- PLEU-09 b and table (factual): the writer taught pneumothorax sensitivity 81% and said the card's 88% was wrong. The 2008 abstract does print 81%, but there were 9 pneumothoraces and 81% cannot be a fraction of 9. 8/9 = 88%, which is the figure in Lichtenstein's own later review table. Explanation now teaches 88%/100% (as on the card) and calls the abstract figure a misprint. Sources: PubMed 18403664 abstract (n=9, "81%"); Lichtenstein, Ann Intensive Care 2014;4:1, Table 1 (88%/100%). Other BLUE figures (260 patients, 90.5%, 97/95, 89/97, 81/99, 89/94) confirmed from the same abstract.
- PLEU-11 b (factual): "ambulatory valve failure 46% vs 15% in a 2021 trial" → Walker 2021 had 41 patients, no shorter stay; the 46% (6/13) applies to one device (8 Fr pleural vent) vs 15% (3/20) standard drain, not significant (the other device, Atrium Pneumostat, 0/8). Source: Walker et al., ERJ 2021 abstract (UCL Discovery 10118926).
- PLEU-11 e (factual): "the 2023 guidance also lists a second ipsilateral and a first contralateral pneumothorax" → "BTS also lists", since these are long-standing BTS (2010) surgical indications and I could not confirm them as new in 2023. Source: BAPS spontaneous pneumothorax page (citing MacDuff 2010).
- Header comments of both files: updated to record what was verified and where (no content effect).
- Top-up T_bb56c02c a (minor): "about three-quarters in theatre in one review" added. Source: J Cardiothorac Surg 2021, doi 10.1186/s13019-021-01439-5 ("75% of the events occur intraoperatively"; mortality 50% recognised, 100% undiagnosed; patch rather than direct suture; right kinks SVC and IVC, left gives ischaemia and arrhythmia; suction on the drain is a precipitant).
- Top-up T_bb56c02c c (minor, addition): air injection into the operated hemithorax added as a temporising step. Positioning non-operated side down (operated side up) confirmed. Source: PMC7049486 (conservative treatment of cardiac herniation after intrapericardial pneumonectomy).
- Top-up T_bb56c02c trap 2 (softening): "drain left on water seal or balanced drainage" → kept off suction (clamped with periodic release, or balanced drainage, by local practice). Coughing against an underwater seal can itself shift the mediastinum. Source: de Groot, S Afr J Radiol 1996 (post-pneumonectomy complications).

Checked and left unchanged: ACURASYS pneumothorax 4.0% vs 11.7% (barotrauma 5.1% vs 11.7%), from WikiJournalClub; lung point (Lichtenstein, ICM 2000: 44/66 = 66%, 6/8 = 75% radio-occult, specificity 100%), from the QxMD abstract; absent sliding specificity 91.1%, NPV 100% (Lichtenstein, Chest 1995, QxMD abstract); Tocino 1985 (112 pneumothoraces, anteromedial 38%, subpulmonic 26%, 30% missed, half of those to tension), from the Scilit abstract; Brown NEJM 2020 (316 patients, 94.4% vs 98.5%, about 15% of the conservative arm intervened), from First10EM; RAMPP (236 patients, median stay 0 vs 4 days, adverse events 55% vs 39%, all 14 serious events in the ambulatory arm, further pleural procedures 21% vs 35%), from the PubMed abstract mirror and REBEL EM; BTS 2010 (large = >2 cm at the hilum, 3–5-day surgical opinion, stop aspiration at 2.5 L, small-bore drain after failed aspiration, suction not routine), from the Thorax 2010 guideline text; BTS 2023 (size no longer decides intervention, only safety of bedside drain; conservative care of minimally symptomatic PSP regardless of size; high-risk features include age >50 with significant smoking), from a 2024 PMC review (PMC11320271) and CHEST Physician; normal pleural fluid pH about 7.6 from higher bicarbonate, fall below 7.2 from cell and bacterial metabolism, from acutecaretesting.org. Chest-drain practice (bubbling drain never clamped, no routine clamping for pneumothorax, removal in expiration or Valsalva) confirmed from the Royal Marsden manual (citing Havelock 2010) and NHS Borders guidance; safe-triangle borders confirmed from Radiopaedia. Not separately sourced but standard teaching and left as written: visceral pleura drains largely to pulmonary veins; three-sided dressing for open pneumothorax; ATLS wound two-thirds of tracheal diameter. check_learn.py RESP07A: OK.

## Final FLAGS
- PLEU-03 (strengthened, now verified): card a is wrong. 24 and 9.8 per lakh a year are GP consultation rates for all pneumothorax, primary and secondary combined (Gupta 2000), not the incidence of primary pneumothorax. BTS 2010: PSP 18–28 (men), 1.2–6 (women) per lakh a year. Catamenial pneumothorax and the subclavian "abort and film" rule are not on the card.
- PLEU-04 (kept): card right. Marrow key for stable-vitals subclavian pneumothorax is disputed. Consider adding the needle-as-bridge point and the ATLS 10th adult/child sites.
- PLEU-06 (edited): incomplete, add surgical emphysema to b. Card d's "no difference in respiratory distress" overstates OPTICC: 38% vs 25%, not significant in an underpowered trial; a quarter of observed patients needed a drain, about 40% if ventilated 5 days or more.
- PLEU-09 (REMOVED): the writer's flag was wrong. The card's 88%/100% for A′ + lung point is correct (8 of 9 patients). The 81% in the 2008 abstract is a misprint.
- PLEU-10 (kept): dated (BTS 2010). BTS 2023 lets symptoms and high-risk features decide, allows conservative care of minimally symptomatic PSP regardless of size, and adds ambulatory devices.
- PLEU-11 (edited): DocT GT7 key disputed. Card e's "thoracotomy with pleurectomy" is dated (VATS is usual). Ambulatory evidence in SSP corrected: Walker 2021 (41 patients), 46% early failure with the 8 Fr pleural vent vs 15% standard, not significant.
- PLEU-12 (kept): card c's clamping step is not BTS practice. BTS advises no routine clamping, and a bubbling drain is never clamped.
