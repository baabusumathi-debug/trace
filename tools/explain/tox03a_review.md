# TOX03A — independent review of Explain v2 texts

Deck: 13 cards (PEST-01 to PEST-13), organophosphates and carbamates. `check_learn.py TOX03A` → OK.

## Changes made

- PEST-02 b: said "diethyl compounds also tend to need metabolic activation" → now attributes the delayed, prolonged onset to thion chemistry (chlorpyrifos, parathion, diazinon are thions converted in the liver to the oxon), not to the ethyl group. Source: Goldfrank's / Eddleston & Chowdhury, Br J Clin Pharmacol — thion-to-oxon activation is independent of the dimethyl/diethyl split (dichlorvos is already an oxon, malathion a dimethyl thion).
- PEST-02 c: profenofos "ages faster still" was explained as plain ageing → now explained as inhibited enzyme that is poorly reactivatable by oximes, so the rescuable window is effectively absent (card wording preserved in the restatement). Source: Eddleston, profenofos pharmacology (oxime-resistant inhibition).
- PEST-04 traps: "around a quarter of patients are tachycardic on arrival" was an unverifiable figure → softened to "a substantial minority".
- PEST-06 d: mechanism of the intermediate syndrome stated as settled → now notes the mechanism is unsettled but all proposed mechanisms are post-synaptic, which is why neither atropine nor an oxime alters it. Source: Karalliedde/Senanayake reviews.
- PEST-08 a: "many diethyl compounds are converted to their active oxon" → corrected to thion compounds (chlorpyrifos, parathion, fenthion, malathion), same source as PEST-02 b.
- PEST-10 a: added that published protocols open at 1.8–3 mg and double identically, and corrected the vague "tens or hundreds of milligrams" to "about 10 mg up to 100 mg or more in the first day". Source: Eddleston atropine protocol as reproduced in Austin Health atropine guideline 2024 and Queensland Poisons Information atropine monograph (1.2–3 mg, double every 5 min, endpoints clear chest / HR >80 / SBP >80, infusion 10–20% of loading dose per hour).
- PEST-11 c: the explanation asserted that an infusion is better → now gives the WHO regimen (30 mg/kg over 30 min, then 8 mg/kg/h until atropine is no longer needed), the 4 mg/L rationale, and the actual conflicting evidence: small Indian trial of 1 g/h for 48 h vs 1 g every 4 h (fewer deaths, median 5 vs 10 ventilator days) against the placebo-controlled Sri Lankan trial of the WHO dose (no clinical benefit, trend to harm). Sources: Pawar et al., Lancet 2006; Buckley/Eddleston Cochrane review "Oximes for acute organophosphate pesticide poisoning" (CD005085).
- PEST-11 traps: "Trial evidence is negative, so never give an oxime" → replaced with "trial evidence settles the question either way", corrected using the Cochrane conclusion (insufficient evidence either way) while keeping the practical point that an oxime is started early for an unknown compound.
- PEST-11 decode: added a line that the WHO regimen is not proven to save lives.
- PEST-12 d: FFP described as evidenced harm → now states the single randomised pilot (FFP vs albumin vs saline) raised plasma cholinesterase but gave no improvement in deaths or ventilator days, with numerically longer ventilation on FFP, so "no benefit, not recommended" rather than proven harm. Source: Pichamuthu et al., Clin Toxicol (Phila) 2010;48:813 (deaths 4/19 FFP vs 2/19 saline, p = 0.6; ventilation median 10.0 vs 7.5 days).
- PEST-12 d: magnesium "no scientific data" → explained as unproven rather than undocumented (several small randomised trials, pooled mortality OR 0.55, 95% CI 0.32–0.94; hypotension at the highest dose; evidence insufficient to recommend). Source: Brvar et al., Clin Toxicol (Phila) 2018 systematic review.
- PEST-12 traps and decode: FFP lines reworded to match the corrected evidence while keeping FFP as the intended exam answer.

Verified and left unchanged: WHO hazard classes by oral LD50 (Ia <5, Ib 5–50, II 50–2000, III >2000, U ≥5000); ageing half-lives dimethyl ~3 h vs diethyl ~33 h; soman ageing in minutes with pyridostigmine pretreatment; intermediate syndrome 10–40% at 24–96 h with neck flexor, proximal, bulbar and respiratory weakness; OPIDN via neuropathy target esterase, distal and ascending at 1–3 weeks, independent of acute severity, triorthocresyl phosphate and mipafox as the classic agents; RBC AChE severity bands (mild 20–50%, moderate 10–20%, severe <10%) and ~1%/day recovery; plasma BChE as exposure marker and suxamethonium warning; atropine endpoints and the 70–80% restart after toxicity; pralidoxime 4 mg/L target; carbamate decarbamylation within hours and the old carbaryl evidence of worse outcome with an oxime; no dialysis (large Vd, lipophilic).

## Final FLAGS

- **PEST-01** — card a gives up to 300,000 deaths a year from organophosphates specifically; most sources quote ~250,000–370,000 deaths from pesticide self-poisoning as a whole, with organophosphates the largest share. Card figure kept. The WHO classification quoted is the older oral-LD50 table (the 2019 revision also uses dermal LD50 and reclassifies some compounds) — fine for exam use.
- **PEST-02** — card lists malathion as dimethyl (correct) and the watch note calls it lipophilic (correct), but malathion is of low mammalian toxicity because of rapid carboxylesterase detoxification; a stem pairing malathion with rapid death is usually testing formulation/solvent or massive dose. No change needed.
- **PEST-09** — card c's atropine challenge (rise in heart rate >20–25/min with flushing after a small dose) is traditional teaching from resource-limited settings, not a validated test, and is absent from Goldfrank's/UpToDate; it should not delay empirical atropine. Card statement kept.
- **PEST-10** — card c's maintenance infusion (10–20% of the loading dose per hour) is confirmed standard practice (Eddleston protocol, Austin Health 2024, Queensland PIC). Card b's heart rate >80 and systolic >80 are the published endpoints; some protocols drop the pupil criterion entirely because it is unreliable — the explanation says so.
- **PEST-11** — card c gives pralidoxime 1–2 g over 30 min with repeat at 1 hour; the WHO regimen (30 mg/kg then 8 mg/kg/h) is also given, card first. The card implies oximes work if given in time: Cochrane concludes the evidence is insufficient to show benefit or harm, the Sri Lankan placebo-controlled trial of the WHO dose found no benefit with a trend to harm, and the small Indian high-dose trial found fewer deaths. The explanation now states this rather than asserting benefit.
- **PEST-12** — card b's activated charcoal 1 g/kg within 1 hour has no proven mortality benefit (negative Sri Lankan RCT) and carries aspiration risk; reasonable but not mandatory. Card c's glycopyrrolate figures (30% vs 90% confusion, 7.5 mg in 200 mL) come from a small single-centre study; kept as the card states.
- **PEST-12 (FFP)** — card d calls fresh frozen plasma harmful; the single randomised pilot shows no benefit rather than proven harm (Pichamuthu 2010). "Do not use" is defensible, "harmful" overstates it; FFP remains the intended answer if the exam asks for the harmful item on this list.
- **PEST-12 (magnesium)** — card d's "magnesium has no scientific data" is outdated: four randomised trials and several cohorts, pooled mortality OR 0.55 (0.32–0.94) in Brvar 2018, insufficient to recommend but not undocumented.
