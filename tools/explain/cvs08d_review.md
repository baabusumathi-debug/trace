## Points I was asked to settle
- **'Rescue breaths' retired:** not supported. The 2025 adult BLS chapter uses "breaths" and "ventilations" and does not announce a change of term. The 2025 paediatric BLS chapter still says "rescue breathing" (knowledge-gaps table). FLAG kept and sharpened.
- **2020 classes:** IV first 2a, with IO 2b ("may be considered"). Vasopressin 2b ("may be considered but offers no advantage"). Post-arrest: avoid SBP <90 and MAP <65 (2b); the 2020 text calls these "common cutoffs". Shockable-rhythm epinephrine after failed shocks 2b. **2025:** IV 1 (A), IO 2a (A), vasopressin 3: No Benefit (B-R), shockable epinephrine 2a. MAP ≥65 is class 1 and the 2025 text says "updated to remove a previous systolic blood pressure target".
- **Diastolic BP:**
  - Paediatric 2020: the ≥25 mmHg (infants) and ≥30 mmHg (children) figures appear only in the supporting text (verified), under a 2b recommendation to use DBP to judge CPR quality.
  - Paediatric 2025: explicit target, ≥25 infants and ≥30 children ≥1 year (2b, C-LD; verified).
  - Adults: AHA 2025 gives no DBP target. ERC 2025 uses ≥30 mmHg.
- **Cardioversion energies:**
  - 2010 algorithm (unchanged in 2015): AF 120–200 J biphasic or 200 J monophasic; flutter and other narrow regular rhythms 50–100 J; monomorphic VT 100 J. From memory; the 2010 page would not open.
  - 2020 algorithm: use the device's recommended energy. The 2020 text (verified) already said that ≥200 J improves first-shock success.
  - 2025 (verified): AF ≥200 J (2a, B-R); flutter 200 J (2b, C-LD).
- **Opioid/naloxone wording (verified):**
  - 2025 Systems of Care: "Public policies should allow for possession, use, and immunity from civil and criminal liability for good faith administration of naloxone by lay rescuers" (1, B-NR). "Naloxone distribution programs can be beneficial" (2a). The synopsis only likens public naloxone to "AED cabinets".
  - Special circumstances: naloxone and teaching at discharge (1); naloxone in arrest may be reasonable (2b) and must not delay CPR or activation (1).
  - The card's "recommended alongside PAD" is a loose paraphrase (FLAG added).
- **Disputed-key trap (ACLS-62, 29 °C VF):** the truth side is correct. AHA 2025: one shock, then defer further shocks (2b, C-LD) and epinephrine (2b, C-EO) until ≥30 °C. The DocT "AHA more permissive" note describes 2020.

## Final FLAGS
- **ACLS-59:** in g, "rescue breaths retired" is not confirmed. Adult BLS 2025 just says "breaths", and the paediatric BLS 2025 chapter still uses "rescue breathing". The >8 cm is pad diameter. In d, "not routine" was already the 2020 position; 2025 grades it 3: No Benefit, with 2b for selected settings.
- **ACLS-62:** in c, "public access naloxone recommended alongside PAD" is looser than the guideline. The guideline says lay possession, use and immunity (class 1 policy) and distribution programmes (2a), and public naloxone is only likened to AED cabinets. "New algorithm" means redrawn. The DocT "AHA more permissive" hypothermia note is outdated (that was 2020). Keyed C stands; "defibrillate once and rewarm" is also defensible.
- **ACLS-63:** in b, "depth now has a 6 cm ceiling" is not new; avoiding >6 cm was in 2020.
- **ACLS-64:** in a and b, the "old" 120–200 J / 50–100 J figures come from the 2010/2015 algorithm; 2020 said to use the device's recommended energy. In f, 32–37.5 °C was already AHA policy from the 2023 focused update; only ≥36 h is new in 2025.
- **ACLS-65:** in f, infant abdominal thrusts were never recommended. In d, the DBP 25/30 figures were in the 2020 supporting text; 2025 makes them an explicit target.
- **ACLS-68:** in b, the 2025 chapter says "portable orders for life-sustaining treatment"; "Physician Orders …" is the original expansion.
