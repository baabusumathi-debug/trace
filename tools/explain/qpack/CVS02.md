- **[DocT GT · GT6]** Which of the following infectious agents have been associated with the development of inflammatory myocarditis?

  - Answer: D · All of the above · cohort right 89%
  - line: DocT: Diphtheria (exotoxin myocarditis with conduction block, the leading cause of death in diphtheria) and Q fever (Coxiella burnetii) are also infective causes of myocarditis, alongside Coxsackie B (the commonest viral cause); so 'all of the above' is right.

## CVS-42 · For cardiac MRI in — 1 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT2]** A 29-year-old man presents with chest pain, elevated troponin, and suspected myocarditis. Cardiac MRI shows subepicardial late gadolinium enhancement in the inferolateral wall. Which statement is correct regarding cardiac MRI in myocarditis?
  - Options: A) LGE location confirms viral etiology || B) MRI sensitivity highest after 7 days || C) Extent of LGE predicts major events || D) MRI replaces endomyocardial biopsy
  - Answer: B · MRI sensitivity highest after 7 days · cohort right 50%
  - line: DocT: CMR yield is higher when it is done more than ~7 days after symptom onset, when oedema, hyperaemia and LGE are all visible (ideally within 2-3 weeks). CMR cannot tell a viral from a non-viral cause and does not replace endomyocardial biopsy when giant-cell or fulminant myocarditis is suspected.
  - key_note: Option C (extent of LGE predicts events) is also supported by current data: LGE presence/extent, especially mid-wall septal LGE, independently predicts MACE (ITAMY, Grani JACC 2017; ESC 2025 myocarditis guidance). DocT's 'LVEF not LGE' is dated; B is defensible but not uniquely right.

## CVS-43 · For endomyocardial biopsy, — 2 questions

- **[DocT GT · CGT4]** A 40-year-old patient presents with rapidly progressive cardiogenic shock and refractory ventricular arrhythmias. An endomyocardial biopsy is performed. The pathology shows "multinucleated giant cells and extensive myocyte necrosis." What is the most critical management priority for this specific diagnosis?
  - Options: A) Initiation of high-dose immunosuppression and early consideration for mechanical circulatory support (MCS) or transplant. || B) Strict bed rest and supportive care only, as immunosuppression has no role in viral myocarditis. || C) Routine administration of IVIG and Plasmapheresis. || D) Long-term therapy with Penicillin to prevent recurrence.
  - Answer: A · Initiation of high-dose immunosuppression and early consideration f...
  - line: DocT: Giant cell myocarditis (giant cells + extensive myocyte necrosis on biopsy) is the one myocarditis where combined immunosuppression (steroid + cyclosporine ± azathioprine) is life-saving; refractory VT and shock are typical, so plan early MCS or transplant. Lymphocytic viral myocarditis, by contrast, gets supportive care.
