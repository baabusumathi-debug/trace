## NUT-08 · Enteral formulas: — 1 questions

- **[DocT GT · CGT6]** A 59-year-old woman with chronic pancreatitis and cirrhosis requires enteral nutrition. She develops steatorrhea despite adequate pancreatic enzyme replacement. Which lipid formulation is most likely to improve intestinal fat absorption?
  - Options: A)

Pure long-chain triglycerides || B)

High omega-6 emulsion || C)

Medium-chain triglycerides || D)

High total fat intake
  - Answer: C · Medium-chain triglycerides
  - line: DocT: Medium-chain triglycerides are absorbed straight into the portal blood with little need for pancreatic lipase, bile salts, micelles or chylomicrons. That makes them the fat of choice in pancreatic insufficiency, cholestatic/cirrhotic malabsorption, short bowel and chylous leaks; LCT and high omega-6 emulsions all need lipase and bile.

## NUT-09 · Measuring energy needs: — 4 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT1o]** A 39-year-old 80-kg trauma patient is intubated and sedated in the ICU. You are planning to start enteral nutrition. What is his daily calorie goal that you need to meet with tube feeding?
  - Options: A) 2100 kcal/d || B) 1800 kcal/d || C) 2600 kcal/d || D) 1500 kcal/d
  - Answer: A · 2100 kcal/d · cohort right 32%
  - key_note: 80 kg x 25-30 kcal/kg = 2000-2400 kcal/day, so 2100 is the only option in range (ASPEN, when indirect calorimetry is unavailable).
- **[DocT GT · GT1o]** Indirect calorimetry determines energy expenditure by measuring:
  - Options: A) The O2 consumption and CO2 production over 24 hours || B) The O2 consumption via measurements from a pulmonary artery catheter || C) The O2 consumption and CO2 production at rest || D) The heat released by the body and steam released via the skin and respiration
  - Answer: C · The O2 consumption and CO2 production at rest · cohort right 44%
  - key_note: Indirect calorimetry measures VO2 and VCO2 at rest over minutes (Weir equation), not over 24 h. PAC-derived VO2 (Fick) underestimates true consumption.
- **[DocT GT · CGT5]** A mechanically ventilated patient undergoes indirect calorimetry. The measured respiratory quotient (RQ) is 1.55 despite clinical stability and adequate calibration. Which factor most likely explains an unreliable result?
  - Options: A) FiO₂ 0.40 || B) Measurement duration adequate || C) FiO₂ 0.90 || D) Normal nutritional intake
  - Answer: C · FiO₂ 0.90
  - line: DocT: Indirect calorimetry becomes unreliable at FiO2 >0.6 (AARC): the analyser cannot resolve the tiny inspired–expired O2 difference, so small errors blow up VO2 and the RQ. Also invalid with air leaks (bronchopleural fistula, chest drain, cuff leak) and during RRT/ECMO; an RQ outside 0.67–1.3 (here 1.55) means the measurement, not the patient, is wrong.
