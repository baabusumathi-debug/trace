## EOL-03 · Four ethical principles at the end of life: — 2 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT1o]** Which of the following is true regarding the concept of respect for autonomy?
  - Options: A) A patient may be coerced into a treatment by using a credible threat || B) A patient may be persuaded into a treatment by using a legitimate argument || C) Physicians are obligated to "do whatever the patient wants" || D) If a patient refuses a treatment, there is no way a physician can be liable for injuries occurred owing to lack of treatment
  - Answer: B · A patient may be persuaded into a treatment by using a legitimate arg… · cohort right 20%
  - line: DocT: Respecting autonomy allows PERSUASION (legitimate argument and evidence) but never coercion (a credible threat) or manipulation. It does not oblige doctors to do whatever the patient wants: there is no duty to give non-beneficial treatment. A refusal protects the doctor only if it was properly informed; inadequately explained risks still carry liability.
- **[DocT GT · CGT1]** Which ethical principle primarily supports the use of advance directives and surrogate decision making in the ICU?
  - Options: A) Autonomy || B) Beneficence || C) Nonmaleficence || D) Justice
  - Answer: A · Autonomy

## EOL-06 · The steps of end-of-life care (ISCCM): — 2 questions

- **[DocT GT · AIM2]** A palliative care consult is initiated for a ventilated patient with poor prognosis. Which benefit is most consistently associated with multidisciplinary palliative care involvement?
  - Options: A) Reduced ICU readmission and cost savings || B) Increased hospital length of stay || C) Higher rates of invasive procedures || D) Decreased patient satisfaction scores
  - Answer: A · Reduced ICU readmission and cost savings · cohort right 95%
  - line: DocT: Proactive palliative-care involvement for ventilated patients with poor prognosis consistently shortens ICU and hospital stay, reduces non-beneficial procedures and ICU readmission, and lowers cost, with equal or better family satisfaction and no rise in mortality. SSC 2021 still advises against routine formal consults for every septic patient — the benefit is in triggered (poor-prognosis) c
