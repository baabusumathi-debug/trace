## SNAKE-11 · Indian antivenom: — 2 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** What are the contraindications for administering ASV?
  - Options: A) Hypotension || B) Respiratory suppression || C) Egg allergy || D) None
  - Answer: D · None · cohort right 76%
  - line: DocT: ASV has no absolute contraindication: give it whenever systemic envenoming is present, because untreated envenoming is deadlier than the reaction. A prior equine-serum reaction or atopy means keeping resuscitation ready, not withholding ASV; egg allergy is irrelevant (that is vaccines).
- **[DocT QBank]** One vial of ASV neutralises how much amount of Russell viper toxin?
  - Options: A) 6 mg || B) 60 mg || C) 6 microgram || D) 60 microgram
  - Answer: A · 6 mg · cohort right 37%
  - line: DocT: Indian polyvalent ASV is standardised so each mL neutralises 0.6 mg Russell's viper venom (and 0.6 mg cobra, 0.45 mg krait, 0.45 mg saw-scaled) — one 10 mL vial = 6 mg Russell's. A Russell's viper injects ~63 mg on average (up to ~150 mg), which is why the starting dose is 10 vials (~60 mg) and children get the same dose.

  - Options: A) Stop ASV and give IV hydrocortisone || B) Slow ASV rate || C) Stop ASV, give IM epinephrine and supportive care || D) Continue ASV with antihistamines
  - Answer: C · Stop ASV, give IM epinephrine and supportive care · cohort right 69%
  - line: DocT: In ASV anaphylaxis (hypotension, wheeze, rash) stop the infusion and give IM adrenaline 0.5 mg (0.01 mg/kg in a child) first, not 'only if severe' (WHO SEARO 2016). Antihistamine and hydrocortisone are slow adjuncts, so option A, which omits adrenaline, is wrong.
