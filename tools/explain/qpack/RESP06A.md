## PE-03 · Natural history of venous thromboembolism: — 3 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** Which of the following forms of pulmonary embolism is the commonest cause of secondary pulmonary hypertension?
  - Options: A) Air embolism (Caisson's disease) || B) Fat embolism || C) Massive pulmonary embolism (e.g., saddle embolism) || D) Multiple small recurrent pulmonary embolism
  - Answer: D · Multiple small recurrent pulmonary embolism · cohort right 51%
  - line: DocT: CTEPH (PH group 4) comes from incomplete resolution of emboli — classically multiple small, recurrent, often silent emboli that remodel the pulmonary arteries — not from one massive saddle embolus (which kills or lyses) and not from air or fat embolism (acute, no chronic PH). Suspect it when dyspnoea persists >3 months after PE; V/Q scan is the screening test (a normal scan excludes it).

## PE-04 · Pathophysiology of acute pulmonary embolism: — 1 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT3]** A 64-year-old woman with recent hip surgery develops sudden dyspnea and pleuritic chest pain. CT angiography confirms a large pulmonary embolism. She becomes hypotensive, with echocardiography showing a dilated right ventricle. Which finding explains her acute hemodynamic collapse?
  - Options: A) Pulmonary artery occlusion >40% || B) Mean pulmonary artery pressure >40 mm Hg || C) Increased left ventricular afterload || D) Hypercapnia from excessive minute ventilation
  - Answer: B · Mean pulmonary artery pressure >40 mm Hg · cohort right 73%
  - key_note: Key B is physiologically wrong: a previously normal RV cannot generate a mean PA pressure >40 mmHg acutely (PE-04); a value that high implies chronic PH. Collapse follows obstruction of >30-50% of the pulmonary bed with acute RV failure, so A is the better answer.

- **[DocT GT · GT7]** You request a chest X-ray on a patient who presented to the out-patients with a history of exertional breathlessness. The formal report comments on the presence of "pulmonary oligemia consistent with the Westermark sign". Which of the following conditions is being suggested by the radiologist?
  - Options: A) Macleod's syndrome || B) Primary pulmonary hypertension || C) Pulmonary embolism || D) Pulmonary stenosis
  - Answer: C · Pulmonary embolism · cohort right 83%
  - line: DocT: Westermark sign = focal oligaemia distal to a pulmonary embolus (specific, not sensitive); other PE film signs: Hampton hump (pleural-based wedge infarct), Fleischner/Palla sign (enlarged central PA). ## PE-12 · Echocardiography and compression ultrasound in PE: — 6 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** A 56-year-old ICU patient with sudden hypoxia and hypotension is suspected to have a pulmonary embolus. Multiorgan ultrasound is performed. Which finding most supports this diagnosis?
  - Options: A) Subpleural infarcts with RV dilation || B) Bilateral pleural sliding present || C) Normal left ventricular function || D) Absence of DVT on leg scan
  - Answer: A · Subpleural infarcts with RV dilation · cohort right 86%
  - line: DocT: Lung ultrasound adds a third PE window — small pleural-based wedge/triangular hypoechoic consolidations (infarcts); combined with RV overload and a non-compressible vein, multiorgan POCUS is far more specific than any single window. Normal LV (C) is expected in PE, pleural sliding (B) only excludes pneumothorax, and a negative leg scan (D) does not exclude PE.

- **[NEET 2025 recall]** A 58-year-old male presents with sudden onset dyspnea and pleuritic chest pain. Clinical suspicion for pulmonary embolism is high.
