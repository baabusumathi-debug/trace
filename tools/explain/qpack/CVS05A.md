## ECHO-02 · For the transthoracic echo probe, give: — 2 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** A 55-year-old ventilated patient is evaluated for pleural effusion using POCUS. Which probe offers optimal visualization of the pleural cavity?
  - Options: A) Linear probe (10 MHz) || B) Curvilinear probe (4-5 MHz) || C) Phased array probe (2 MHz) || D) Microconvex probe (8 MHz)
  - Answer: B · Curvilinear probe (4-5 MHz) · cohort right 59%
  - line: DocT: Match the probe to depth and window: pleural effusion/consolidation, abdomen and FAST → curvilinear 2–5 MHz (deep, wide field); pleural line and sliding, vessels, ONSD and the cricothyroid membrane → linear 5–15 MHz (sharp but ≤6 cm deep). The phased array fits between ribs for the heart but has a narrow near field, so it is second choice for pleural fluid.

  - Options: A) 10-27 MHz || B) 5-10 MHz || C) 2-5 MHz || D) 15-20 MHz
  - Answer: C · 2-5 MHz · cohort right 60%

## ECHO-05 · For the Doppler principle in echo, give: — 1 questions

- **[DocT GT · CGT2]** Which of the following is the resistance to propagation of sound?
  - Options: A) Echo Range Principle || B) Attenuation || C) Acoustic Impedance || D) Piezoelectric Effect
  - Answer: C · Acoustic Impedance
  - line: DocT: Acoustic impedance (Z = tissue density × speed of sound) is a medium's resistance to sound propagation. Reflection at an interface depends on the impedance mismatch: air–tissue is huge, hence gel and the beam blocked by lung and bowel gas. Attenuation is energy lost with depth; a higher frequency means more attenuation and less penetration.
