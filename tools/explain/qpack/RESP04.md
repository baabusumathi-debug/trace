## RSC-19 · Lung complications after stem-cell — 4 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** A 58-year-old male with a history of acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL), 4 weeks post-hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT), develops worsening respiratory distress, fever, and a rash characterised by erythematous maculopapular lesions. Bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) is negative for bacteria, fungi, and CMV. Skin biopsy shows perivascular lymphocytic infiltrates. Despite broad-spectrum antimicrobials, his condition deteriorates. What is the most likely diagnosis?
  - Options: A) Drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS) syndrome || B) Graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) of the lung and skin || C) Invasive fungal infection || D) Diffuse alveolar haemorrhage
  - Answer: B · Graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) of the lung and skin · cohort right 59%
  - line: DocT: Acute GVHD hits skin, gut and liver; lung GVHD is chronic (bronchiolitis obliterans), so 'GVHD of lung and skin' at 4 weeks is loose. Rash + fever + non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema at neutrophil recovery (day 7-30) is engraftment syndrome - steroid-responsive like GVHD - so pick GVHD only when engraftment syndrome is not offered.
  - key_note: Least wrong. Acute GVHD rarely involves the lung; this picture (rash + capillary-leak lung + day ~28 + perivascular lymphocytes) also fits engraftment syndrome, which is not offered. GVHD is the best of the options.
- **[DocT GT · CGT4]** A post-HSCT patient develops sudden dyspnea and hemoptysis. A BAL is performed. Which of the following BAL findings is most diagnostic for DAH in this population?
  - Options: A) The presence of >5% neutrophils and positive cultures for Aspergillus fumigatus. || B) Successive BAL aliquots that become progressively more blood-stained. || C) A high concentration of hemosiderin-laden macrophages (>20%) in a BAL performed within the first 24 hours of symptoms. || D) The presence of "smudge cells" in the cytology, indicating viral-induced alveolar breakdown.
  - Answer: B · Successive BAL aliquots that become progressively more blood-stained.
- **[DocT GT · CGT4]** A patient 20-day post-HSCT presents with acute hypoxic respiratory failure, diffuse bilateral infiltrates on CXR, and no evidence of cardiac failure. Bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) is negative for all bacterial, viral, and fungal pathogens. Which statement regarding IPS management and diagnosis is most accurate?
  - Options: A) IPS is a diagnosis of exclusion that occurs only in the presence of documented pulmonary GvHD. || B) Treatment involves high-dose steroids and the potential addition of Etanercept, based on the role of TNF-alpha in IPS pathogenesis. || C) IPS is strictly caused by the direct toxic effects of the conditioning regimen (chemotherapy/radiation) and does not involve the donor immune system. || D) Empiric antibiotics should be discontinued immediately once the BAL returns negative to prevent fungal overgrowth
  - Answer: B · Treatment involves high-dose steroids and the potential addition of...
  - key_note: Etanercept added to steroids for idiopathic pneumonia syndrome improved responses in early studies, but the adult phase III RCT was stopped early without significant benefit. B is still the best option.
- **[DocT GT · GT6]** Which among the following is not the criterion for diagnosing idiopathic pneumonia syndrome?
  - Options: A) Increased alveolar to arterial oxygen difference || B) Iatrogenic Fluid overload || C) Symptoms and signs of pneumonia || D) Chest X-ray or CT showing multilobar infiltrates
  - Answer: B · Iatrogenic Fluid overload · cohort right 93%
  - line: DocT: Idiopathic pneumonia syndrome is a diagnosis of exclusion: widespread alveolar injury (multilobar infiltrates, pneumonia symptoms, raised A-a gradient) AND no active lower respiratory infection AND no cardiac, renal or iatrogenic fluid overload; so 'iatrogenic fluid overload' is an exclusion, not a criterion.

- **[DocT GT · GT1o]** You admit a 61-year-old man to the intensive care unit after a witnessed generalized tonic-clonic seizure at home. He has a history of sarcoidosis and has been treated with prednisone 10 to 60 mg for the last year. Before admission, he had no cough or sputum production and had felt well.   - Options: A) Pneumocystis jirovecii (formerly carinii) infection || B) Sarcoidosis || C) Tuberculosis || D) Nocardiosis
  - Answer: D · Nocardiosis · cohort right 19%

## RSC-21 · Non-invasive support in the — 1 questions

- **[NEET 2025 recall]** Non-invasive ventilation (NIV/NIPPV) is NOT useful in which of the following conditions?
  - Options: A) Interstitial lung disease (ILD) exacerbation || B) Cardiogenic pulmonary edema || C) COPD exacerbation || D) Post-extubation respiratory failure (high risk)
  - Answer: A · Interstitial lung disease (ILD) exacerbation
  - line: DocT: NIV works where the problem is reversible by positive pressure: COPD hypercapnia, cardiogenic pulmonary oedema, high-risk post-extubation. It fails in ILD/IPF exacerbation (stiff, non-recruitable lungs; failure >70%) and delays intubation in severe hypoxaemia; acute exacerbation of IPF on a ventilator carries very high mortality.

## RSC-25 · Advanced ventilation for a large air — 2 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** A 65-year-old patient with severe ARDS due to influenza is undergoing invasive mechanical ventilation. You are considering using high-frequency jet ventilation (HFJV). What is the primary advantage of HFJV in the management of ARDS?
  - Options: A) It is used only for patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) || B) It provides higher tidal volumes, improving oxygenation || C) It allows for very small tidal volumes with high rates, minimizing ventilator-induced lung injury || D) It eliminates the need for positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP)
  - Answer: C · It allows for very small tidal volumes with high rates, minimizing ... · cohort right 93%
  - line: DocT: High-frequency JET ventilation pulses small jets (VT ~2–5 mL/kg, at or below dead space) at 100–200/min with PASSIVE expiration; its real ICU niche is a large air leak/bronchopleural fistula and airway surgery, where small volumes cut fistula flow. 'Minimises VILI in ARDS' is the theory DocT keys, not a proven benefit.
  - key_note: Keyed C is plainly the best option (A, B, D are wrong); HFV is not recommended for adult ARDS (ATS 2017, ESICM 2023) — nuance only, flag removed.
- **[Marrow QBank]** Post-thoracic ICU patient: suspected double-lumen tube migration — next step
  - Answer: C · Bedside flexible bronchoscopy
  - note: ✎ Add: suspected DLT malposition → confirm by bedside FOB, never transport; left DLT has wider safety margin
  - topic: DLT · malposition
