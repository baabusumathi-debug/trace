- **[DocT QBank]** A 36 year old man is brought to the emergency department after being rescued from a drowning incident in cold water. On arrival, he is comatose, intubated, and on mechanical ventilation. His core temperature is 30° C. Neurological examination shows absence of brainstem reflexes, including fixed pupils, absent corneal and oculocephalic reflexes, and no respiratory effort is observed. The apnea test is deferred due to hypothermia.What is the next best step in management?
  - Options: A) Declare brain death || B) Repeat exam in 2 hours || C) Rewarm to ≥36° C || D) Perform EEG
  - Answer: C · Rewarm to ≥36° C · cohort right 68%
  - key_note: Image not needed: core 30 °C makes the exam invalid; rewarm to ≥36 °C first.
  - pattern: lookalike: brain-death mimics — hypothermia (rewarm ≥36 °C) and krait locked-in state (A1 Q15) both look brain-dead but are not
- **[DocT QBank]** A 24 year old man is brought to the emergency department after a high velocity road traffic accident. On arrival, he is comatose with a Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score of 3. He shows no eye opening, no motor response, and no verbalization, even to deep painful stimuli. Brainstem reflexes are absent. A NCCT scan of the brain is done and shown below.   - Answer: B · Global cerebral edema · cohort right 64%
  - line: DocT: CT never diagnoses brain death: diffuse loss of grey-white differentiation with effaced sulci and cisterns is global cerebral oedema, which only supplies the 'known irreversible cause'; brainstem testing and the apnoea test still decide death.

- **[DocT QBank]** A 45 year old male is admitted to the ICU after a large subarachnoid hemorrhage. He is comatose and intubated on mechanical ventilation.   - Options: A) Proceed with apnea test || B) Stop sedatives, wait, and reassess || C) Start confirmatory EEG || D) Declare brain death
  - Answer: B · Stop sedatives, wait, and reassess · cohort right 88%

## DONOR-07 · What happens as the brainstem dies: — 3 questions

- **[DocT GT · CGT2]** Cushing’s triad includes all EXCEPT:
  - Options: A) Hypertension || B) Bradycardia || C) Irregular respiration || D) Tachycardia
  - Answer: D · Tachycardia
  - line: DocT: Cushing's triad = hypertension (wide pulse pressure) + bradycardia + irregular respiration, from brainstem ischaemia as ICP rises; it is a late sign of impending herniation. Tachycardia is not part of it; that belongs to the later autonomic storm when the vagal nucleus dies.
  - pattern: lookalike: Cushing reflex (hypertension + BRADYcardia) vs brainstem-death autonomic storm (hypertension + TACHYcardia)
- **[DocT GT · AIM2]** A 30-year-old woman with TBI presents with hypertension, bradycardia, and irregular respirations. What is this finding called?
  - Options: A) Beck's triad || B) Cushing reflex || C) Kernig's sign || D) Cheyne-Stokes respiration
  - Answer: B · Cushing reflex · cohort right 98%
