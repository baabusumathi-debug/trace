## CVS-18 · Give the treatment for: — 2 questions

- **[DocT GT · AIM1]** A 62-year-old man with prior cardiac surgery develops progressive ascites, hepatomegaly, and peripheral edema. CT reveals pericardial calcification. What is the definitive treatment?
  - Options: A) High-dose diuretics || B) Pericardiectomy || C) Long-term anticoagulation || D) Corticosteroid therapy
  - Answer: B · Pericardiectomy · cohort right 83%
  - line: DocT: Chronic calcific constrictive pericarditis is cured only by pericardiectomy. Diuretics only palliate congestion, and anti-inflammatory therapy helps only transient (inflammatory, CMR-LGE-positive) constriction. Calcification on CT after cardiac surgery or TB is the classic clue.
- **[DocT GT · AIM1]** A 45-year-old woman with history of tuberculosis presents with ascites, hepatomegaly, and pleural effusion. Echocardiography shows respiratory variation in mitral inflow but preserved ejection fraction. Which is the most likely diagnosis?
  - Options: A) Restrictive cardiomyopathy || B) Constrictive pericarditis || C) Dilated cardiomyopathy || D) Acute myocarditis
  - Answer: B · Constrictive pericarditis · cohort right 66%
  - line: DocT: Constriction vs restrictive cardiomyopathy on echo: constriction shows exaggerated respiratory variation of mitral inflow (>25%), septal bounce, preserved or high septal e' ('annulus paradoxus') and a thick or calcified pericardium. Restriction has little respiratory variation and a low e'. TB is the classic cause of constriction in India.

## CVS-19 · For cardiac tamponade, give: — 1 questions

- **[NEET 2025 recall]** A 19-year-old male presents with a gunshot injury to the left chest.  Findings:  BP: 70/40 mmHg  HR: 140 bpm  Neck veins distended  Heart sounds muffled  Pulse regular and bounding  CXR: Globular heart with normal lungs and mediastinum  What will be the definitive management?
  - Options: A) Bilateral chest tubes || B) Application of an external fixator || C) Urgent pericardiocentesis || D) Venographic embolization
  - Answer: C · Urgent pericardiocentesis
  - line: DocT: Penetrating chest wound + Beck's triad (hypotension, raised JVP, muffled sounds) + globular heart with clear lungs = traumatic tamponade. Pericardiocentesis is the bedside life-saving step offered here; in trauma the true definitive treatment is thoracotomy/sternotomy to repair the injury.
  - key_note: Keyed pericardiocentesis is best of the options; definitive care for traumatic tamponade is surgical (ATLS: thoracotomy), not offered.

- **[DocT GT · CGT4]** A patient with a large pericardial effusion is being monitored in the ICU. You observe a drop in systolic blood pressure of 15 mmHg during inspiration. What is the physiological mechanism behind this?
  - Options: A) Negative intrathoracic pressure during inspiration increases the pooling of blood in the lungs, reducing LV filling. || B) Inspiration increases venous return to the right heart, which displaces the interventricular septum to the left, further restricting LV filling. || C) Inspiration causes the pericardium to tighten, directly compressing the coronary arteries. || D) Diaphragmatic contraction during inspiration increases intra-abdominal pressure, causing a reflex drop in cardiac output.
  - Answer: B · Inspiration increases venous return to the right heart, which displ...
  - pattern: shared-skeleton: ventricular interdependence via a fixed pericardium — pulsus paradoxus in tamponade, discordant RV/LV pressures in constriction, D-shaped LV in acute RV overload
- **[DocT GT · CGT5]** A 64-year-old woman with metastatic breast cancer presents with dyspnea, hypotension (BP 82/56 mm Hg), jugular venous distention, tachycardia, and pulsus paradoxus. Echocardiography demonstrates a large pericardial effusion with right ventricular diastolic collapse. She requires urgent airway management. Which factor places her at greatest risk for cardiovascular collapse after intubation?
  - Options: A) Increased venous return || B) Positive intrathoracic pressure || C) Enhanced adrenergic tone || D) Reduced myocardial oxygen demand
  - Answer: B · Positive intrathoracic pressure
  - line: DocT: Intubating a patient in tamponade can precipitate arrest: positive intrathoracic pressure cuts the venous return that keeps the compressed right heart filling, and induction removes the compensating sympathetic tone. Drain first (pericardiocentesis under local anaesthesia, sitting up); if intubation is unavoidable, keep spontaneous breathing, use ketamine and low PEEP/VT, and have the needle
  - pattern: shared-skeleton: peri-intubation collapse from lost preload/tone: tamponade, anterior mediastinal mass, hypovolaemic trauma (POLY-10), severe asthma/auto-PEEP.
