## RSC-11 · Ventilating a patient with abdominal — 1 questions

- **[DocT GT · CGT5]** A morbidly obese patient is ventilated with a plateau pressure of 38 cm H₂O. Pleural pressure is markedly elevated because of abdominal and chest wall compression. Which interpretation is most accurate?
  - Options: A) Severe alveolar rupture || B) High transpulmonary pressure || C) Safe distending pressure || D) Bilateral volutrauma
  - Answer: C · Safe distending pressure
  - line: DocT: In obesity or abdominal distension a plateau of 38 cmH2O can still be a safe distending pressure: the stiff chest wall raises pleural (oesophageal) pressure, so transpulmonary pressure (Pplat − Ppl) may be well below the ~20–25 cmH2O overdistension limit. VILI follows transpulmonary pressure, not airway pressure.
