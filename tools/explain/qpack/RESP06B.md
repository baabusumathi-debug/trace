## PE-18 · Parenteral anticoagulation in PE: — 3 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT3]** A 65-year-old ICU patient with acute PE is started on unfractionated heparin. Which of the following is the most appropriate monitoring parameter for dose adjustment?
  - Options: A) INR 2.0-3.0 || B) aPTT 1.5-2.5 times normal || C) Anti-Xa 1.0-1.5 IU/mL || D) Platelet count daily
  - Answer: B · aPTT 1.5-2.5 times normal · cohort right 73%
- **[DocT GT · CGT4]** A 65-year-old male with septic shock and a history of nephrotic syndrome is being treated for a deep vein thrombosis (DVT). He is receiving a continuous infusion of Unfractionated Heparin (UFH) at 25 units/kg/hr, but his activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (aPTT) remains at the baseline of 30 seconds. What is the most likely physiological cause, and what is the recommended next step?
  - Options: A) Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT); stop heparin and start Argatroban. || B) Antithrombin (AT) deficiency; measure AT levels or switch to a Direct Thrombin Inhibitor (DTI). || C) High levels of Factor VIII; increase the heparin dose until the aPTT reaches 2.5times baseline. || D) Enhanced renal clearance of heparin; switch to Low Molecular Weight Heparin (LMWH).
  - Answer: B · Antithrombin (AT) deficiency; measure AT levels or switch to a Dire...
  - line: DocT: Heparin resistance (aPTT stays flat despite >35,000 U/day) usually means antithrombin deficiency: lost in the urine in nephrotic syndrome, consumed in sepsis/DIC or on ECMO. Check the AT level or anti-Xa, then give AT concentrate or switch to a direct thrombin inhibitor. A high factor VIII causes only "pseudo-resistance" (aPTT short, anti-Xa adequate).
- **[DocT GT · CGT4]** Which of the following statements regarding Fondaparinux is correct according to Vincent’s?
  - Options: A) It is a naturally occurring heparinoid derived from porcine intestinal mucosa. || B) It can be safely used in patients with an eGFR of 15mL/min. || C) It does not bind to Platelet Factor 4 (PF4) and therefore does not cause HIT. || D) It can be rapidly reversed using Protamine Sulphate in the event of bleeding.
  - Answer: C · It does not bind to Platelet Factor 4 (PF4) and therefore does not ...
  - line: DocT: Fondaparinux is a synthetic pentasaccharide that barely binds PF4, so it does not cause HIT and is used off-label to treat it. It is cleared only by the kidneys (half-life 17–21 h, contraindicated if CrCl <30) and protamine does not reverse it.

## PE-22 · Contraindications to thrombolysis: — 4 questions
