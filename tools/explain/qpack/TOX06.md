  - Answer: B · Initiate anti–IL-6 therapy with tocilizumab
- **[DocT GT · CGT5]** A 24-year-old man with relapsed B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia receives CD19-directed CAR-T cell therapy. - **[DocT GT · AIM2]** Which of the following best describes the mechanism of action of tocilizumab in Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS)?
  - Options: A) Directly neutralises IL-6 in circulation || B) Blocks IL-6 receptor || C) Inhibits gp-130 signalling directly || D) Prevents IL-1 receptor activation
  - Answer: B · Blocks IL-6 receptor · cohort right 59%
  - line: DocT: Tocilizumab is a humanised monoclonal antibody against the IL-6 RECEPTOR (both membrane-bound and soluble IL-6R), blocking classic and trans-signalling; it does not bind IL-6 itself (siltuximab does) and does not act on gp130 or IL-1 (anakinra). Because it blocks the receptor, serum IL-6 rises after a dose.

  - line: DocT: The classic transplant cause of PRES is a calcineurin inhibitor (cyclosporine or tacrolimus): seizures, hypertension, cortical blindness and posterior (parieto-occipital) vasogenic oedema on MRI.
