## CLD-25 · Precipitants of hepatic — 4 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** A 56-year-old male with cirrhosis presents with confusion, asterixis, and disorientation. His ammonia level is elevated. He was recently prescribed furosemide and lactulose. He denies any GI bleeding or infection. Serum sodium is 122 mmol/L. What is the most likely precipitating factor for his hepatic encephalopathy?
  - Options: A) Hyponatremia || B) Use of lactulose || C) Use of furosemide || D) Elevated ammonia
  - Answer: A · Hyponatremia · cohort right 25%
  - line: DocT: Hyponatraemia (especially Na <130) is an independent precipitant of HE. Astrocytes already loaded with glutamine have used up their organic osmolytes (myo-inositol), so low plasma osmolality swells them further. In a cirrhotic on furosemide with Na 122, name hyponatraemia as the precipitant - furosemide is its cause and ammonia is the mediator.

## CLD-29 · Other measures, and the evidence: — 5 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** A patient with chronic liver disease and recurrent HE is found to have low serum zinc levels. How does zinc deficiency contribute to HE?
  - Options: A) By impairing lactulose metabolism || B) By stimulating glutamine synthetase || C) By reducing urea cycle efficiency || D) By increasing ammonia production in the kidneys
  - Answer: C · By reducing urea cycle efficiency · cohort right 42%
  - line: DocT: Zinc is a cofactor for ornithine transcarbamylase (urea cycle) and muscle glutamine synthetase, so deficiency lowers ureagenesis and raises ammonia. Cirrhotics lose zinc through poor intake, diuretic-driven urinary loss and malabsorption.
- **[DocT QBank]** A cirrhotic patient with multiple episodes of HE is being considered for neomycin therapy. What is the primary concern with neomycin use in this context?
  - Options: A) Ineffectiveness compared to lactulose || B) Risk of nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity || C) Development of antibiotic resistance || D) Hyperkalaemia
  - Answer: B · Risk of nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity · cohort right 87%
- **[DocT QBank]** Which trial demonstrated the efficacy of rifaximin in preventing recurrent hepatic encephalopathy?
  - Options: A) CONFIRM || B) REVEAL || C) Bass et al. NEJM 2010 || D) PALOMA trial
  - Answer: C · Bass et al. NEJM 2010 · cohort right 29%
  - line: DocT: Rifaximin for preventing recurrent HE = Bass et al., NEJM 2010: 299 cirrhotics after >=2 episodes, rifaximin 550 mg BD vs placebo for 6 months (91% also on lactulose). Breakthrough HE 22% vs 46%; HE hospitalisation 14% vs 23%. CONFIRM is the terlipressin-HRS trial.
- **[DocT GT · CGT5]** A 58-year-old ICU patient with severe protein-calorie malnutrition receives a nutrition regimen containing leucine, isoleucine, and valine along with other essential amino acids at 0.8 g/kg/day. Which effect is most consistently demonstrated?

  - Answer: A · Improved nitrogen balance
  - line: DocT: In malnourished or critically ill patients, BCAA-enriched amino acids (leucine, isoleucine, valine at 0.5–1.2 g/kg/day) consistently improve nitrogen balance, but no trial shows lower mortality or shorter ventilation, and outside cirrhosis they do not reduce encephalopathy; they are not routine ICU nutrition.
