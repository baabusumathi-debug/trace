- **[DocT QBank]** A 35-year-old male with chronic alcohol abuse is admitted to the ICU with confusion, ataxia, and ophthalmoplegia. Despite thiamine administration, he develops severe hypotension and requires vasopressor support. Which of the following is the most likely cause of his hemodynamic instability?
  - Options: A) Septic shock due to aspiration pneumonia || B) Alcoholic cardiomyopathy causing cardiogenic shock || C) Neurogenic shock secondary to Wernicke's encephalopathy || D) Hypovolemic shock due to vomiting and diarrhoea
  - Answer: B · Alcoholic cardiomyopathy causing cardiogenic shock · cohort right 34%
  - line: DocT: In a chronic drinker, shock that persists after IV thiamine is alcoholic dilated cardiomyopathy (>80 g/day for >5 years; low EF, cold, needs inotropes; partly reversible with abstinence). Wet beriberi is the look-alike — warm high-output failure with low SVR that reverses within hours of thiamine — and Wernicke's has no cord lesion to cause neurogenic shock (C).
- **[DocT GT · PYQ]** A 58-year-old woman develops sudden severe chest pain, dyspnea, and diaphoresis immediately after hearing about the unexpected death of her son. ECG shows ST-segment elevation, but coronary angiography reveals no obstructive lesions. What is the most likely diagnosis?
  - Options: A) Inferior wall myocardial infarction || B) Takotsubo cardiomyopathy || C) Aortic dissection || D) Pulmonary embolism
  - Answer: B · Takotsubo cardiomyopathy
  - line: DocT: Takotsubo: an emotional or physical stressor (often a post-menopausal woman) → chest pain with ST elevation or deep T inversion and a modest troponin rise, but unobstructed coronaries and apical ballooning with basal hyperkinesis on ventriculography or echo. It recovers over weeks; watch for LVOT obstruction (avoid inotropes, use fluids, phenylephrine and beta-blockers).
