## ACLS-62 · Name the 2025 changes for special circumstances covering: — 1 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** A 40-year-old male is rescued from cold water after a boating accident. His core temperature is 29°C, and he is in ventricular fibrillation. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?
  - Options: A) Administer antiarrhythmic drugs. || B) Perform immediate active rewarming to 36°C. || C) Continue CPR and withhold antiarrhythmics until the temperature is >30°C. || D) Defibrillate once and proceed with active rewarming.
  - Answer: C · Continue CPR and withhold antiarrhythmics until the temperature is ... · cohort right 53%
  - line: DocT: In hypothermic arrest below 30 °C (ERC 2021), give up to 3 shocks, then withhold further shocks, adrenaline and antiarrhythmics until core >30 °C, because the cold myocardium does not respond and drugs accumulate; at 30–35 °C double the drug interval (adrenaline every 6–10 min). AHA is more permissive (standard shocks and vasopressor may be reasonable); DocT's key follows ERC.
  - pattern: same-number: 30 °C — the core temperature below which shocks/drugs are withheld in hypothermic arrest

## ACLS-65 · Name the 2025 paediatric changes covering: — 1 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** A 7-year-old child collapses on the playground at school, he is unresponsive and is not breathing and has no pulse. The PT teacher initiates CPR. At what rate and depth would he deliver compressions to this child?
  - Options: A) 90 to 100 compressions per minute at a depth of about 1 inch || B) 100 to 120 compressions per minute at a depth of about 1 inch || C) 90 to 100 compressions per minute at a depth of about 2 inch || D) 100 to 120 compressions per minute at a depth of about 2 inch
  - Answer: D · 100 to 120 compressions per minute at a depth of about 2 inch · cohort right 67%
  - line: DocT: Paediatric compressions: rate 100–120/min (same as adults); depth at least one-third of the AP chest diameter, about 4 cm (1.5 in) in infants and 5 cm (2 in) in children. Adolescents use the adult 5–6 cm. Ratio 30:2 with one rescuer, 15:2 with two.

## ACLS-68 · For the new standalone ethics chapter, give: — 2 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT1o]** Which of the following is true regarding the concept of respect for autonomy?
  - Options: A) A patient may be coerced into a treatment by using a credible threat || B) A patient may be persuaded into a treatment by using a legitimate argument || C) Physicians are obligated to "do whatever the patient wants" || D) If a patient refuses a treatment, there is no way a physician can be liable for injuries occurred owing to lack of treatment
  - Answer: B · A patient may be persuaded into a treatment by using a legitimate arg… · cohort right 20%
  - line: DocT: Respecting autonomy allows PERSUASION (legitimate argument and evidence) but never coercion (a credible threat) or manipulation. It does not oblige doctors to do whatever the patient wants: there is no duty to give non-beneficial treatment. A refusal protects the doctor only if it was properly informed; inadequately explained risks still carry liability.
- **[DocT GT · CGT1]** Which ethical principle primarily supports the use of advance directives and surrogate decision making in the ICU?
  - Options: A) Autonomy || B) Beneficence || C) Nonmaleficence || D) Justice
  - Answer: A · Autonomy

## ACLS-70 · For neuroprognostication after cardiac arrest, state the 2025 additions: — 2 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT5]** An amateur boxer shows elevated CSF neurofilament light chain (NF-L) after bouts. What does this finding most likely suggest?
  - Options: A) BBB closure || B) Axonal injury || C) Glial scarring || D) Synaptic sprouting
  - Answer: B · Axonal injury · cohort right 69%
  - line: DocT: Neurofilament light chain (NfL) is a structural protein of large myelinated AXONS, so a raised CSF/serum NfL means axonal injury — it rises even after repetitive mild TBI (boxers) and is the 2025 post-arrest biomarker. Contrast: NSE = neuronal cell body, S-100B/GFAP = glia/astrocytes, so 'glial scarring' and 'BBB closure' are wrong.
- **[DocT GT · GT5]** A 58-year-old man is admitted to the ICU after cardiac arrest and resuscitation. He is undergoing therapeutic hypothermia. On day 2, he shows: Absent corneal reflexes No response to painful stimuli Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in prognostication?
  - Options: A) Withdraw life-sustaining treatment || B) Repeat neurologic examination immediately || C) Wait at least 72 hours before determining prognosis || D) Obtain serum neuron-specific enolase level to guide immediate prognosis
  - Answer: C · Wait at least 72 hours before determining prognosis · cohort right 83%
