## RVF-01 · For acute right ventricular — 1 questions

- **[DocT GT · GrandT1]** A 72-year-old male with septic shock is receiving fluid resuscitation and vasopressors. His CVP is 16 mm Hg, and his cardiac output remains low. Echocardiography shows a dilated right ventricle with poor systolic function. Which of the following best defines true right ventricular (RV) failure in this context? Mean systemic filling pressure (MSFP)
  - Options: A) Elevated CVP with normal RV function || B) Increased RV filling without decreased output || C) RV dysfunction with limited RV filling || D) High MSFP with normal CVP
  - Answer: C · RV dysfunction with limited RV filling · cohort right 69%
  - line: DocT: RV FAILURE = RV dysfunction PLUS limited RV filling: the RV cannot fill without a high CVP (venous congestion) and cannot deliver enough stroke volume to the LV. RV dysfunction with a normal CVP is dysfunction, not failure; a high CVP with normal RV function is volume, not failure.

## RVF-20 · For pulmonary hypertension, — 7 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT1o]** A patient with known pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) from congenital heart disease has recently been diagnosed with ES. What is the most appropriate treatment that should be initiated in this patient?
  - Options: A) Digoxin || B) Beta blocker therapy || C) Calcium channel blocker therapy || D) Bosentan
  - Answer: D · Bosentan · cohort right 75%
  - line: DocT: Eisenmenger syndrome is treated with PAH-targeted therapy. The first-line evidence is for the endothelin receptor antagonist bosentan (BREATHE-5: better exercise capacity and haemodynamics, and saturation did not worsen); PDE-5 inhibitors are the alternative. Calcium-channel blockers lower SVR and increase the right-to-left shunt. Beta-blockers and digoxin do not treat PAH.
- **[DocT GT · GrandT1]** A 42-year-old woman presents with exertional dyspnea and leg swelling. She reports progressive fatigue, occasional chest pressure, and a history of Raynaud phenomenon and morning headaches. Exam reveals JVD, lower extremity edema, and a loud P2. Which is the most likely underlying diagnosis?
  - Options: A) Left heart failure || B) Pulmonary arterial hypertension || C) Interstitial lung disease || D) Chronic thromboembolic disease
  - Answer: B · Pulmonary arterial hypertension · cohort right 54%
  - line: DocT: Insidious exertional dyspnoea, chest pressure, right heart failure (JVD, oedema) and a loud P2 in a young woman with Raynaud's = pulmonary ARTERIAL hypertension (Group 1, connective-tissue disease such as systemic sclerosis). Confirm on right heart catheter: mPAP >20 mmHg (ESC 2022), PAWP ≤15, PVR >2 WU – a normal wedge excludes left heart disease (Group 2).

- **[DocT GT · GT6]** Which of the following statements regarding Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension on right heart catheterization is true?
  - Options: A) Increased mean PAP>25mmHg || B) PCWP>25mmHg || C) LVEDP >15mmHg || D) PVR < 3 wood units
  - Answer: A · Increased mean PAP>25mmHg · cohort right 76%
  - line: DocT: On right-heart catheterisation, pre-capillary PH/PAH = raised mPAP + PAWP (or LVEDP) ≤15 mmHg + raised PVR. The 2022 ESC/ERS thresholds are mPAP >20 mmHg and PVR >2 Wood units (older teaching >25 and >3), so a high PCWP or LVEDP means post-capillary (group 2) PH, not PAH.
  - key_note: Key A (mPAP >25) uses the pre-2022 threshold but is clearly the only true option.
- **[DocT GT · GT7]** Which of the following is the most common symptom attributable to pulmonary hypertension?
  - Options: A) Edema || B) Exertional dyspnea || C) Syncope || D) Peripheral edema
  - Answer: B · Exertional dyspnea · cohort right 76%
  - line: DocT: The commonest symptom of pulmonary hypertension is exertional dyspnoea (RV cannot raise output); then fatigue, exertional angina (RV ischaemia), exertional syncope or near-syncope (fixed output) and late peripheral oedema.

## RVF-21 · Give the name and the — 4 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** Which of the following forms of pulmonary embolism is the commonest cause of secondary pulmonary hypertension?
  - Options: A) Air embolism (Caisson's disease) || B) Fat embolism || C) Massive pulmonary embolism (e.g., saddle embolism) || D) Multiple small recurrent pulmonary embolism
  - Answer: D · Multiple small recurrent pulmonary embolism · cohort right 51%
  - line: DocT: CTEPH (PH group 4) comes from incomplete resolution of emboli — classically multiple small, recurrent, often silent emboli that remodel the pulmonary arteries — not from one massive saddle embolus (which kills or lyses) and not from air or fat embolism (acute, no chronic PH). Suspect it when dyspnoea persists >3 months after PE; V/Q scan is the screening test (a normal scan excludes it).
