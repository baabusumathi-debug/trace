- **[DocT QBank]** In a ventilated ICU patient, the intensivist notes an IVC diameter of 17 mm with >20% collapsibility during inspiration. What does this suggest?
  - Options: A) Low right atrial pressure || B) Elevated RAP from overload || C) Tamponade physiology || D) Right heart failure
  - Answer: A · Low right atrial pressure · cohort right 49%
  - key_note: Wording error, answer holds: a passive ventilated IVC DISTENDS on inspiration (distensibility >18% = fluid responsive); 'collapsibility' is spontaneous-breathing language. A is plainly best. Flag removed.
  - pattern: same-number: fluid-responsiveness cut-offs — PLR/VTI rise ≥10–15%, IVC distensibility >18% (ventilated), PPV >12–13%, sniff collapse >50% (spontaneous)
- **[DocT QBank]** During bedside echo, a patient's TR jet peak velocity is 3.5 m/s, and RAP is estimated at 10 mm Hg. What is the approximate RV systolic pressure (RVSP)?
  - Options: A) 49 mm Hg || B) 60 mm Hg || C) 35 mm Hg || D) 75 mm Hg
  - Answer: B · 60 mm Hg · cohort right 33%
  - line: DocT: RVSP = 4 × (peak TR velocity)² + RAP: 4 × 3.5² = 49, + 10 = 59 ≈ 60 mmHg. 49 (the commonest wrong pick) is only the RV–RA gradient with RAP forgotten; TR velocity >2.8 m/s suggests pulmonary hypertension.

## ECHO-21 · For the pleural view, give: — 1 questions

- **[DocT GT · PYQ]** On ultrasound, the "sinusoid sign" is characteristic of which condition?
  - Options: A) Pleural effusion || B) Pneumothorax || C) Lung consolidation || D) Pulmonary edema
  - Answer: A · Pleural effusion
  - line: DocT: On M-mode, a pleural effusion gives the sinusoid sign: the lung line (visceral pleura) moves toward the pleural line in inspiration, tracing a sine wave. With the quad sign (fluid boxed by the pleural line, rib shadows and lung line) it confirms free fluid. Pneumothorax gives the barcode (stratosphere) sign and a lung point.

- **[DocT GT · CGT2]** Anechoic fluid anterior to the descending aorta in the parasternal long-axis (PLAX) view is consistent with which of the following diagnoses?

## ECHO-36 · Give the four goals of FATE, — 4 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** During POCUS of a patient with sepsis, a dependent anechoic pleural collection moves sinusoidally with respiration. What does this indicate?
  - Options: A) Highly viscous empyema || B) Simple transudative effusion || C) Organized hemothorax || D) Consolidated lung parenchyma
  - Answer: B · Simple transudative effusion · cohort right 61%
  - line: DocT: The sinusoid sign (M-mode: the lung line swings toward the pleural line on inspiration) proves FREE-FLOWING, low-viscosity fluid — a simple effusion — but it cannot prove a transudate. Septated or viscous collections (empyema, organised haemothorax) damp the swing.
  - key_note: Keyed B is plainly the best option (A, C, D are wrong); 'transudative' is an over-read — the sinusoid sign shows free-flowing fluid, and anechoic fluid can be exudate. Flag removed.
