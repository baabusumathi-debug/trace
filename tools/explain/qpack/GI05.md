- **[DocT QBank]** A patient with peritonitis develops refractory shock and acidosis. Bedside US shows significant free intraperitoneal fluid. Surgeons plan open abdomen (OA) with temporary abdominal closure (TAC). Which of the following is TRUE about TAC?
  - Options: A) Always contraindicated in trauma patients || B) Should be closed within 48-72 hours || C) Increases ACS risk || D) Used only for damage control surgery
  - Answer: B · Should be closed within 48-72 hours · cohort right 39%
  - line: DocT: After open abdomen with temporary closure (negative-pressure preferred, WSACS 2013), aim for primary fascial closure as soon as physiology allows, ideally within 4-7 days, because closure rates fall and fistula risk rises each day; '48-72 h' is DocT's version of 'early'. TAC is used in damage control, severe peritonitis and ACS decompression, and it prevents ACS rather than causing it.
  - key_note: B is plainly the best of the options (the others are false). Nuance: no guideline fixes closure at 48-72 h; WSACS recommends early or same-admission fascial closure, usually within about 7 days.
