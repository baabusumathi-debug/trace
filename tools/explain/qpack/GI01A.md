## ALF-06 · Causes of acute liver failure: five — 3 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT8]** A 40-year-old with myeloproliferative disorder develops abdominal pain, hepatomegaly, jaundice, and ALF. Which diagnosis best fits?
  - Options: A) Budd-Chiari syndrome || B) Autoimmune hepatitis || C) Hepatitis E || D) Reye syndrome
  - Answer: A · Budd-Chiari syndrome · cohort right 79%
  - line: DocT: ALF with abdominal pain, tender hepatomegaly and ascites in a myeloproliferative neoplasm (PV, ET; JAK2) = Budd–Chiari syndrome (hepatic vein/IVC thrombosis). Confirm with Doppler ultrasound; treat with anticoagulation, then angioplasty/TIPS, transplant if fulminant.
- **[DocT GT · GT8]** A 42-year-old man on isoniazid presents with jaundice, INR 2.5, encephalopathy after 4 weeks of illness. No acetaminophen use. Which etiology is most consistent?
  - Options: A) Idiosyncratic drug-induced liver injury || B) Viral hepatitis A || C) Wilson's disease || D) Acetaminophen toxicity
  - Answer: A · Idiosyncratic drug-induced liver injury · cohort right 100%
- **[Marrow QBank]** Colon cancer, acute liver failure from Budd-Chiari on CT: additional step?
  - Answer: D · Evaluate for liver transplant
  - note: ✎ ALF-32: add 'Budd–Chiari presenting as ALF → transplant evaluation (MRI, ursodiol, surgery no role)'. ALF-32 lists extrahepatic malignancy as a contraindication, and this patient had colon cancer 2 months ago
  - topic: Budd-Chiari · ALF transplant

## ALF-09 · Drugs, toxins and herbs causing — 2 questions

- **[DocT GT · AIM2]** A 29-year-old woman develops encephalopathy and jaundice 2 weeks after eating mushrooms while camping. Which toxin is implicated?
  - Options: A) Amatoxin from Amanita phalloides || B) Aflatoxin from Aspergillus flavus || C) Tetrodotoxin from pufferfish || D) Botulinum toxin

## ALF-10 · Cause-specific treatment: — 4 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** A 40 yr old woman with a known diagnosis of autoimmune hepatitis presents to the emergency department with progressive jaundice, altered mental status, and coagulopathy. She has been on high dose corticosteroids for the past 7 days, but there has been no clinical or biochemical improvement. On examination, she is drowsy with asterixis and scleral icterus. Laboratory tests reveal: Total bilirubin: 22 mg/dL; INR: 5.2;AST/ALT: 1300/1100 U/L; Serum ammonia: Elevated; Serum creatinine: 1.6 mg/dL; She is diagnosed with acute liver failure (ALF) secondary to autoimmune hepatitis and is unresponsive to corticosteroids. What is the next best management ?
  - Options: A) Increase corticosteroid dose || B) Add azathioprine || C) Evaluate for liver transplantation || D) Start mycophenolate mofetil
  - Answer: C · Evaluate for liver transplantation · cohort right 60%
  - line: DocT: Autoimmune hepatitis presenting as ALF: steroids only in early ALF (no grade 3-4 HE), with transplant listing in parallel; no improvement in bilirubin/INR within ~7 days = steroid failure -> urgent transplant. Do not escalate steroids or add azathioprine/MMF: they act slowly and add sepsis risk.
- **[DocT QBank]** A 45-year-old woman with chronic hepatitis B presents with jaundice, ascites, and a MELD score of 18. What is the most appropriate next step in management?
  - Options: A) Initiate antiviral therapy || B) Evaluate for liver transplantation || C) Start diuretics for ascites || D) Begin lactulose therapy
  - Answer: A · Initiate antiviral therapy · cohort right 25%
  - line: DocT: Hepatitis B is the exception to 'viral hepatitis = supportive'. Start a nucleos(t)ide analogue (tenofovir or entecavir) at once in acute severe HBV/ALF, HBV reactivation, HBV-ACLF and decompensated HBV cirrhosis, whatever the HBV DNA level; it lowers short-term mortality and graft reinfection. Interferon is contraindicated in decompensation.
  - key_note: Antivirals are right, but MELD 18 (>=15) with decompensation also meets transplant-referral criteria, so B is not wrong; AASLD advises both together. A is the 'reversible cause first' answer.
- **[DocT GT · CGT4]** A 28-yr old woman presents to the emergency department with jaundice, fatigue, and altered mental status. She has no history of alcohol or drug use. Her past medical history is significant for autoimmune hepatitis, diagnosed two years ago but noncompliant with immunosuppressive therapy. On examination, she is drowsy and disoriented. She has scleral icterus and mild hepatomegaly. Which of the following autoantibodies is most specific for her condition?
  - Options: A)

Anti-smooth muscle antibody || B)

Anti-mitochondrial antibody || C)

Anti-LKM1 antibody || D)

Anti-centromere antibody
  - Answer: C · Anti-LKM1 antibody
  - line: DocT: Autoantibodies in autoimmune hepatitis: anti-LKM1 is the marker of type 2 AIH (young patients, often fulminant); ANA and anti-smooth-muscle mark type 1 (commoner, less specific). Anti-mitochondrial = primary biliary cholangitis; anti-centromere = limited scleroderma.

## ALF-13 · Work-up and monitoring: — 2 questions

- **[DocT GT · CGT5]** A cirrhotic patient has prolonged PT. Factor II is decreased, but factor V remains normal. What is the most likely diagnosis?
  - Options: A) Disseminated intravascular coagulation || B) Advanced liver failure || C) Acquired hemophilia || D) Vitamin K deficiency
  - Answer: D · Vitamin K deficiency
  - line: DocT: Factor V is the liver-made factor that is NOT vitamin K-dependent: low II/VII with normal V = vitamin K deficiency (cholestasis, antibiotics, poor intake) → give IV vitamin K 10 mg; low II, VII AND V = synthetic failure; low V with low VIII (made by endothelium) = DIC/consumption.
- **[Marrow QBank]** Acute hepatitis after Mexico travel, HEV IgM negative: next step
  - Answer: B · Hepatitis E PCR
  - note: ✎ Add to ALF-13 b: HEV IgM can be falsely negative; send HEV RNA PCR if suspicion high.
