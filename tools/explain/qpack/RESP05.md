## SWAN-05 · Insertion: — 1 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** While inserting a pulmonary artery catheter, the balloon is accidentally left inflated after placement. Which of the following is the most serious potential complication?
  - Options: A) Catheter knotting in the right atrium || B) Pulmonary artery rupture || C) Pacing wire displacement || D) Thrombus formation on catheter tip
  - Answer: B · Pulmonary artery rupture · cohort right 66%
  - line: DocT: A balloon left inflated (or a tip migrated into permanent wedge) occludes the PA branch — infarction — and is the main route to PA rupture, heralded by haemoptysis. Inflate (≤1.5 mL) only to advance or briefly wedge, then deflate; never withdraw or flush with the balloon up. Knotting (A) comes from over-advancing, not inflation.

- **[DocT GT · GT7]** All are true except:
  - Options: A) Ventilation is greater at the bases in the erect position || B) Perfusion is greater at the bases in the erect position || C) Ventilation-perfusion ratio (V/Q) is greater at the bases in the erect patient || D) Ventilation-perfusion ratio (V/Q) is greater at the anterior lungs in the supine patient
