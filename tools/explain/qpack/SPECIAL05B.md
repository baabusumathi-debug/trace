# SPECIAL05B: real questions linked to each card

## POLY-11 · The lethal triad: — 1 questions

- **[DocT GT · CGT5]** A trauma patient arrives with a core temperature of 33°C. Despite adequate platelet count and normal coagulation factor levels, diffuse microvascular bleeding persists. Which hemostatic defect is most directly responsible?
  - Options: A) Enhanced fibrinolysis || B) Reduced platelet adhesion || C) Increased thrombin generation || D) Excess tissue factor
  - Answer: B · Reduced platelet adhesion
  - line: DocT: Hypothermia's main haemostatic hit is on platelets: adhesion and activation fall (about one-third less adhesion at 33 °C), with enzyme slowing mostly below ~33 °C. PT/aPTT are run at 37 °C, so they look normal while the patient oozes; rewarming, not more products, fixes it.

## POLY-12 · Trauma-induced coagulopathy: — 5 questions

- **[DocT GT · CGT5]** A trauma patient undergoes thromboelastography. The LY30 is 5%. Which interpretation is most accurate?
  - Options: A) Mild platelet dysfunction || B) Normal fibrinolysis || C) Tenfold mortality increase || D) Isolated factor deficiency
  - Answer: C · Tenfold mortality increase
  - line: DocT: On TEG, LY30 >3% defines hyperfibrinolysis in trauma and carries about a 10-fold higher mortality, so an LY30 of 5% is NOT normal; treat with tranexamic acid (CRASH-2: 1 g within 3 h of injury, then 1 g over 8 h). Very low LY30 (<0.9%, 'shutdown') also predicts worse outcome.
- **[DocT GT · CGT5]** A 29-year-old trauma patient develops hemorrhagic shock with diffuse microvascular bleeding despite no anticoagulant exposure. Which clotting factor deficiency is most consistently observed in shock?
  - Options: A) Factor V deficiency || B) Factor IX deficiency || C) Factor XI deficiency || D) Factor XIII deficiency
  - Answer: A · Factor V deficiency
  - line: DocT: Shock-activated protein C cleaves factors Va and VIIIa, so factor V is the factor most consistently depleted in haemorrhagic shock, falling with shock severity before any dilution (alongside early fibrinogen loss). Factors IX, XI and XIII are not the typical deficits.
- **[DocT GT · CGT6]** A 28-year-old trauma patient arrives after a high-speed collision with profound hemorrhagic shock. Despite rapid transfusion, diffuse oozing develops from venipuncture sites. Which mechanism is primarily responsible for the systemic acute coagulopathy of hemorrhage?

  - Answer: B · Thrombin–thrombomodulin–protein C activation
- **[DocT GT · GT5]** A 25-year-old male with polytrauma presents in hemorrhagic shock. Despite minimal crystalloid resuscitation, he demonstrates coagulopathy on admission. What mechanism best explains this?

## POLY-13 · Lactate and base excess in trauma: — 2 questions

- **[NEET 2025 recall]** A 34-year-old male presents to the emergency department after a motor vehicle accident (MVA).  BP: 90/60 mmHg  Blood lactate: 3 mmol/L  What is the next step in management?
  - Options: A) Early total care || B) Damage control surgery || C) Further blood investigations || D) Further resuscitation and repeat lactate levels
  - Answer: D · Further resuscitation and repeat lactate levels
  - line: DocT: SBP 90 with lactate 3 after trauma = early shock, not extremis: keep resuscitating and repeat the lactate (clearance shows adequacy). Damage-control surgery is for the physiologically exhausted (lethal triad: pH <7.2, core <35 °C, coagulopathy); early total care only once stable with lactate <2.5.
- **[NEET 2025 recall]** What is the base deficit in Class III shock in an injured patient?
  - Options: A) -2 to -6 mEq/L || B) 0 to -2 mEq/L || C) -6 to -10 mEq/L || D) -10 or more mEq/L
  - Answer: C · -6 to -10 mEq/L
  - line: DocT: ATLS 10th edition ties base deficit to shock class: Class I 0 to −2, Class II −2 to −6, Class III −6 to −10, Class IV −10 or worse (mEq/L). The card's mild/moderate/severe bands are a different scheme.
