- **[DocT QBank]** A 19-year-old is intubated for respiratory failure due to OP poisoning. Chest X-ray shows bilateral infiltrates with normal heart size and CVP. Diagnosis?
  - Options: A) Cardiogenic pulmonary edema || B) Aspiration pneumonia || C) Non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema || D) ARDS
  - Answer: C · Non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema · cohort right 50%
  - line: DocT: Bilateral infiltrates in OP poisoning with a normal heart and CVP = non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema. The causes are bronchorrhoea flooding the alveoli, raised capillary permeability, and aspiration (common, given vomiting with a low GCS). If it meets Berlin criteria (within 1 week, PEEP ≥5, not cardiac), it is ARDS and gets lung-protective ventilation.
  - key_note: By Berlin, bilateral infiltrates not explained by cardiac failure after a known insult = ARDS, so D is equally defensible. 'Non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema' is the umbrella term DocT keys.

  - Answer: C · Reactivates acetylcholinesterase · cohort right 88%
- **[DocT QBank]** What is the recommended pralidoxime dosing strategy for severe OP poisoning?
  - Options: A) 1 g bolus only || B) 2 g IV over 30 min, then 500 mg/hour infusion || C) 500 mg every 8 hours || D) 5 g stat, then stop
  - Answer: B · 2 g IV over 30 min, then 500 mg/hour infusion · cohort right 97%
  - line: DocT: The WHO pralidoxime regimen is 30 mg/kg (about 2 g) IV over 30 min, then a continuous infusion of 8 mg/kg/h (about 500 mg/h), kept going until atropine is no longer needed.
