## MI-58 · For ventricular arrhythmias after MI, give: — 4 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT2]** A 67-year-old man develops ventricular tachycardia 48 hours after a large anterior myocardial infarction. This arrhythmia most likely results from which mechanism?
  - Options: A) Enhanced normal automaticity || B) Abnormal automaticity || C) Early afterdepolarizations || D) Delayed afterdepolarizations
  - Answer: B · Abnormal automaticity · cohort right 87%
  - line: DocT: VT 24-72 h after a large MI comes from abnormal automaticity in partially depolarised ischaemic myocytes (non-pacemaker cells); AIVR at reperfusion is enhanced normal automaticity, and late monomorphic VT (weeks on) is scar re-entry. Afterdepolarisations belong to long QT (early) and digoxin/catecholamine Ca overload (delayed).
  - key_note: Re-entry is the dominant mechanism of sustained post-MI VT overall but is not offered; B is the best option for the 48-h window.
- **[DocT GT · GrandT1]** A 68-year-old man develops nonsustained ventricular tachycardia 48 hours after an acute MI. He is hemodynamically stable. What is the most likely underlying mechanism?
  - Options: A) Reentry || B) Abnormal automaticity || C) Early afterdepolarizations || D) Conduction block
  - Answer: B · Abnormal automaticity · cohort right 67%
  - line: DocT: Ventricular arrhythmias 24–72 h after MI (PVCs, NSVT, AIVR) arise mainly from ABNORMAL AUTOMATICITY (and triggered activity) in partially depolarised ischaemic Purkinje fibres and myocytes, driven by catecholamines and low K/Mg. Re-entry around a healed scar causes LATE sustained monomorphic VT (weeks–years). Early NSVT needs only electrolytes and a beta-blocker.
- **[DocT GT · GT4]** A 67-year-old man develops ventricular tachycardia 48 hours after an extensive anterior myocardial infarction. This arrhythmia most likely results from which mechanism?
  - Options: A) Enhanced normal automaticity || B) Abnormal automaticity || C) Early afterdepolarizations || D) Delayed afterdepolarizations
  - Answer: B · Abnormal automaticity · cohort right 83%
  - line: DocT: Ventricular arrhythmia mechanism by time after MI — first minutes–hours (ischaemic phase): re-entry; 24–72 h (delayed phase): abnormal automaticity of surviving Purkinje/ischaemic myocytes; reperfusion AIVR: enhanced normal automaticity; triggered activity — EADs give torsades with long QT, DADs come from digoxin/catecholamines.

- **[DocT GT · GT6]** Which is the true statement in Atrioventricular Block in the setting of Acute myocardial infarction?
  - Options: A) Anterior wall MI most commonly produce 2nd and 3rd degree heart block || B) Inferior wall MI most commonly produce 1st and 2nd degree heart block || C) In acute Myocardial Infarction (MI), AV block transiently develops in 10-25% of patients || D) Anterior wall MI associated with narrow complex escape in AV block
  - Answer: C · In acute Myocardial Infarction (MI), AV block transiently develops ... · cohort right 15%

ECG shows sinus tachycardia; troponin is mildly elevated. He is anxious and struggling to breathe, but still alert. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?
  - Options: A) Start loop diuretics and wait for effect || B) Intubate and initiate mechanical ventilation || C) Administer nitrates and initiate noninvasive positive-pressure ventilation (NIPPV) || D) Begin antibiotics and IV fluids
  - Answer: C · Administer nitrates and initiate noninvasive positive-pressure vent... · cohort right 65%
  - line: DocT: In hypertensive acute pulmonary oedema (SCAPE) the first moves are high-dose IV nitroglycerin (rapid preload and afterload reduction) plus NIV/CPAP; most patients are not fluid-overloaded but redistributed, so 'loop diuretic and wait' is too slow and intubation is reserved for failure of NIV or a falling GCS.

  - Answer: B · IV Nitroglycerin · cohort right 94%
  - line: DocT: For hypertensive pulmonary oedema the drug of choice is IV nitroglycerin (venodilation at low dose, arterial dilation at high dose, titratable in minutes); hydralazine is unpredictable and causes reflex tachycardia, labetalol's beta-blockade depresses a failing LV, oral ACEi is too slow.
