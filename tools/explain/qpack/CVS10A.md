## MI-04 · Sort the other (non-ischaemic) causes of — 2 questions

- **[DocT GT · PYQ]** A 58-year-old woman develops sudden severe chest pain, dyspnea, and diaphoresis immediately after hearing about the unexpected death of her son. ECG shows ST-segment elevation, but coronary angiography reveals no obstructive lesions. What is the most likely diagnosis?
  - Options: A) Inferior wall myocardial infarction || B) Takotsubo cardiomyopathy || C) Aortic dissection || D) Pulmonary embolism
  - Answer: B · Takotsubo cardiomyopathy
  - line: DocT: Takotsubo: an emotional or physical stressor (often a post-menopausal woman) → chest pain with ST elevation or deep T inversion and a modest troponin rise, but unobstructed coronaries and apical ballooning with basal hyperkinesis on ventriculography or echo. It recovers over weeks; watch for LVOT obstruction (avoid inotropes, use fluids, phenylephrine and beta-blockers).
- **[Marrow QBank]** Stroke post-tPA with raised troponin: cause and drugs
  - Answer: C · Stroke; DAPT ≥24 h after tPA, statin now
  - note: ✎ MI-04: add stroke-related troponin rise is injury, not NSTEMI; post-tPA hold antiplatelets 24 h (stroke card, not ACS decks). key?: 21-day DAPT is for minor stroke/TIA, and unknown-onset (woke after 2 h) stroke is a doubtful tPA case
  - topic: Troponin · stroke

## MI-16 · For ischaemic chest pain in ACS, give: — 2 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** A 31-year-old male presented with a gradual onset of mild chest pain that worsens with activity and is relieved by rest. His vitals and cardiac auscultation are normal. On palpation, tenderness is present over the left sternal area. ECG reveals a normal sinus rhythm. His father died of a heart attack in his sixties. What is the next step in management?
  - Options: A) Administer Aspirin and send for a troponin || B) Administer Aspirin, clopidogrel, and heparin and admit for acute coronary syndrome (ACS) || C) Administer ibuprofen and reassure the patient that he is not having a heart attack || D) Observe patient for 6 hours
  - Answer: C · Administer ibuprofen and reassure the patient that he is not having a  · cohort right 45%
  - line: DocT: Chest-wall tenderness (MI-17) does not exclude ACS — it is found in a minority of MIs — and pain that worsens with exertion and settles with rest (MI-16) with a family history of MI is anginal in character. Safe practice is aspirin + troponin (option A) before reassurance; DocT keys 'ibuprofen and reassure'.
  - key_note: Key C is unsafe as written: exertional pain relieved by rest + family history is anginal; chest-wall tenderness does not exclude ACS. Most guidelines would get ECG + troponin first (A). Item is outpatient/ED triage, low CCM yield.
- **[DocT GT · GT6]** Increased likelihood of Acute MI is indicated by all of the following characteristic of chest pain except:
  - Options: A) Associated with nausea/ vomiting || B) Described as sharp || C) Described as pressure || D) Radiation to right arm/ shoulder
  - Answer: B · Described as sharp · cohort right 76%
  - key_note: Sharp/pleuritic pain lowers MI likelihood; pressure, nausea and radiation (even to the right arm) raise it.
