## APAP-03 · What raises and what lowers the risk — 3 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** A patient with a history of chronic alcohol use disorder is brought to the emergency department after a suspected overdose of acetaminophen. Which of the following statements is most accurate regarding this patient's risk of hepatotoxicity?
  - Options: A) Chronic alcohol use protects against acetaminophen hepatotoxicity. || B) The risk of hepatotoxicity is the same as in patients without chronic alcohol use disorder. || C) Chronic alcohol use increases the risk of hepatotoxicity, even at doses below the recommended safe level. || D) Acetaminophen is not metabolized in patients with chronic alcohol use disorder.
  - Answer: C · Chronic alcohol use increases the risk of hepatotoxicity, even at dose · cohort right 92%
- **[DocT GT · GT5]** A 3-year-old accidentally ingests 220 mg/kg of acetaminophen. Which metabolic pathway is predominant in children under 10 years?
  - Options: A) Sulfation pathway metabolism || B) Glucuronidation pathway metabolism || C) Cytochrome P450 pathway metabolism || D) Unchanged renal excretion
  - Answer: A · Sulfation pathway metabolism · cohort right 31%
  - line: DocT: Young children (<~10 y) clear paracetamol mainly by SULFATION (adults mainly glucuronidation), and have more glutathione, so less goes down the CYP2E1 route to NAPQI — the reason toddlers tolerate accidental doses better. The same nomogram and NAC rules still apply.
- **[DocT GT · GT5]** A 42-year-old man with chronic alcoholism presents after repeated supratherapeutic acetaminophen use. Why is he at increased risk of hepatotoxicity?
  - Options: A) Enhanced renal clearance of acetaminophen || B) Increased CYP2E1 activity and low glutathione || C) Decreased enterohepatic recirculation || D) Reduced intestinal absorption of acetaminophen
  - Answer: B · Increased CYP2E1 activity and low glutathione · cohort right 94%

## APAP-05 · The four clinical stages of — 2 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** A 35-year-old man is admitted following a serious attempt at paracetamol overdose. Despite efforts to treat him, he develops liver failure. Which of the following is most likely associated with the ensuing liver failure?
  - Options: A) Better prognosis in older patients || B) Better prognosis in those with chronic alcohol consumption || C) Hypoglycaemia rarely happens within 12 hours of the onset of encephalopathy || D) Lactic acidosis is a recognised complication
  - Answer: D · Lactic acidosis is a recognised complication · cohort right 70%
- **[DocT GT · GT5]** A 30-year-old man presents 36 hours after acetaminophen overdose with jaundice, INR 3.0, and creatinine 2.5 mg/dL. Which complication is most likely associated with this stage?
  - Options: A) Cerebral edema and dysrhythmias || B) Right upper quadrant tenderness || C) Mild diaphoresis and nausea || D) Normal aminotransferase levels
  - Answer: A · Cerebral edema and dysrhythmias · cohort right 31%
  - line: DocT: Severe paracetamol poisoning can reach the stage-III picture early — jaundice, INR 3 and AKI at 36 h already mean fulminant hepatic failure, whose killers are cerebral oedema (ammonia) and dysrhythmias/multi-organ failure. RUQ tenderness and rising enzymes are stage II; nausea/diaphoresis with normal enzymes is stage I.

  - Options: A) Administer oral activated charcoal 50 g || B) Give N-acetylcysteine (NAC) intravenously || C) Measure plasma paracetamol concentration in one hour || D) Take no action
  - Answer: C · Measure plasma paracetamol concentration in one hour · cohort right 32%
  - key_note: Card conflict to resolve outside this map: TOX-09 says activated charcoal within 1 h, APAP-08 says 1 g/kg within 4 h for paracetamol. UK/AACT guidance is 1 h (up to 2 h for large doses). Image not viewed.
- **[DocT QBank]** Two 20-year-old patients present to the emergency department 1 hour after ingesting 15 grams of acetaminophen each in a suicide pact. You are confident of the time of ingestion and that time is confirmed by text messages sent by the patients. Patient A has a 4-hour acetaminophen concentration of 130 mg/dL. Patient B's 4-hour acetaminophen concentration is 170 mg/dL. What is the appropriate treatment for both patients?
  - Options: A) Neither patient requires treatment because the acetaminophen concentrations are below the treatment cut off. || B) Patient A should receive immediate N-acetylcysteine (NAC); patient B should not receive NAC. || C) Patient A should not receive NAC; patient B should receive immediate NAC treatment. || D) Both patients should receive immediate NAC therapy.
  - Answer: C · Patient A should not receive NAC; patient B should receive immediate N · cohort right 42%
  - key_note: Units are wrong: the levels are µg/mL, not mg/dL. Key C follows the US 150 line. Under the UK 100 line (APAP-06d) both patients get NAC, and both took 15 g (more than 200 mg/kg), which many centres would treat anyway.
