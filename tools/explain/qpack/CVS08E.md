- **[DocT GT · GT7]** A 5-year-old child is pulled from a swimming pool after being submerged for an unknown period. He is unresponsive with no signs of breathing. Which of the following is the MOST appropriate initial management strategy?
  - Options: A) Perform abdominal thrusts to expel water from the lungs. || B) Initiate CPR with a focus on rescue breathing. || C) Administer high-flow oxygen and monitor for spontaneous respiration. || D) Perform a neurological examination to assess the extent of brain injury.
  - Answer: B · Initiate CPR with a focus on rescue breathing. · cohort right 91%
  - line: DocT: In drowning the arrest is hypoxic, so CPR starts with rescue breaths (ERC: 5 initial breaths, then 30:2); abdominal thrusts to 'drain water' are not recommended because little water is aspirated and they delay ventilation and cause regurgitation.
  - pattern: same-intervention: hypoxic arrest (drowning, asphyxia, paediatric) - ventilation first, CPR before calling

The clinical course is complicated by worsening acute kidney injury. The latest laboratory workup reveals acidosis with a pH of 7.18 and hyperkalemia of 6.5 mEq/L. Sodium bicarbonate, calcium gluconate, and insulin-dextrose are administered. Although adding on a continuous renal replacement therapy circuit to the ECMO circuit, the patient develops a short run of ventricular tachycardia, which quickly degenerates into asystole. What is the immediate next step in managing this patient?
  - Options: A) Initiate chest compressions || B) Administer epinephrine only and avoid chest compressions || C) Urgent conversion to VA ECMO || D) Defibrillation with 200 J
  - Answer: A · Initiate chest compressions · cohort right 69%
  - line: DocT: Cardiac arrest on VV ECMO is managed by standard ACLS — start chest compressions at once, because VV ECMO gives no circulatory support and oxygenated blood still needs the native heart to reach the body. Defibrillate only VF/pVT (not asystole) and treat the cause (here hyperkalaemia); conversion to VA ECMO is a later decision.

## ACLS-82 · For extracorporeal CPR (ECPR), state: — 1 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT6]** According to the Surugadai Nihon University Hospital, Tokyo (Japan) criteria, the following are exclusion criteria for Extracorporeal Perfusion for Restoration of Circulation (ECPR) except:
  - Options: A) Presumed noncardiac etiology || B) Successful ROSC <10min || C) Core body temperature <35 degrees Celsius || D) Pregnancy
  - Answer: C · Core body temperature <35 degrees Celsius · cohort right 42%
  - line: DocT: In the Surugadai (Tokyo) ECPR criteria the exclusions are presumed non-cardiac cause, ROSC within 10 min of arrival, core temperature <30 °C and pregnancy; a core temperature of 30-35 °C is not an exclusion (hypothermia is actually a favourable reversible cause).

## ACLS-85 · Place IABP, Impella and VA ECMO in ascending — 1 questions

- **[DocT GT · CGT6]** A 58-year-old woman with refractory cardiogenic shock requires temporary mechanical circulatory support.   - Options: A)

Intra-aortic balloon pump || B)

Impella CP || C)

TandemHeart || D)

VA-ECMO
  - Answer: B · Impella CP
  - key_note: Only the IABP needs cardiac synchrony (ECG/pressure-triggered). TandemHeart and VA-ECMO are continuous-flow and just as rhythm-independent as Impella (VA-ECMO even supports VF). Impella is keyed, but options C and D are also correct.
