## CS-42 · For the intra-aortic balloon — 3 questions

- **[DocT GT · GrandT1]** A 65-year-old patient in cardiogenic shock is planned for mechanical circulatory support. The team decides to insert an intra-aortic balloon pump (IABP). Which of the following is TRUE regarding IABP use?
  - Options: A) Implant > 2 weeks is safe || B) Minimum femoral artery size is >4 mm || C) Usual sheath size is 13-14 Fr || D) Requires unstable cardiac rhythm
  - Answer: B · Minimum femoral artery size is >4 mm · cohort right 46%
  - line: DocT: IABP practicalities: 7–8 Fr femoral sheath (13–14 Fr is Impella/ECMO territory), femoral artery ≥4 mm to avoid limb ischaemia, balloon sized to height, needs a regular rhythm for ECG triggering (tachyarrhythmias degrade timing). Usual support is days; beyond ~1–2 weeks limb ischaemia, infection and thrombocytopenia rise.
- **[DocT GT · CGT5]** A patient with cardiogenic shock is supported with an intra-aortic balloon pump (IABP). Arterial waveform analysis shows balloon inflation occurring before aortic valve closure. Which physiologic consequence is most likely?
  - Options: A) Increased coronary perfusion || B) Reduced LV afterload || C) Enhanced diastolic augmentation || D) Increased left ventricular workload
  - Answer: D · Increased left ventricular workload
  - line: DocT: IABP inflation is timed to the dicrotic notch (aortic valve closure). Early inflation inflates against LV ejection → raises afterload and LV workload (may close the aortic valve early); late inflation loses diastolic augmentation; late deflation is the most harmful because the balloon is still up as the LV ejects.
- **[DocT GT · AIM1]** A 64-year-old man with cardiogenic shock after MI is considered for intra-aortic balloon pump (IABP). Which is a contraindication?
  - Options: A) Aortic valve insufficiency || B) Severe LV systolic dysfunction || C) Coronary artery disease || D) Hypertension with LVH
  - Answer: A · Aortic valve insufficiency · cohort right 79%
  - line: DocT: Moderate-to-severe aortic regurgitation is an absolute IABP contraindication: diastolic balloon inflation drives more blood back across the leaking valve into the LV. Aortic dissection and severe aorto-iliac disease are the other absolute contraindications. LV systolic dysfunction and CAD are its usual indications.

## CS-47 · For TandemHeart, give: — 1 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT6]** Which one of the following is not a contraindication to Tandem Heart system?
  - Options: A) Peripheral vascular disease || B) Left ventricular failure || C) VSD || D) Right ventricular failure
  - Answer: B · Left ventricular failure · cohort right 100%
  - line: DocT: TandemHeart is contraindicated in VSD (LA decompression reverses the shunt right-to-left → hypoxaemia), isolated RV failure (an LA-to-artery device cannot fill), severe peripheral vascular disease (femoral cannulae), LA thrombus and moderate-severe AR; LV failure/cardiogenic shock is its indication.
