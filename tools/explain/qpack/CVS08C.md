## ACLS-41 · For airway management in — 4 questions

- **[DocT GT · CGT4]** In an out-of-hospital setting where the EMS system has a low success rate for endotracheal intubation (ETI), what is the preferred advanced airway strategy if one is needed?
  - Options: A) Endotracheal intubation (ETI) || B) Supraglottic airway (SGA) || C) Surgical cricothyrotomy || D) Bag-mask ventilation (BMV) only
  - Answer: B · Supraglottic airway (SGA)
  - line: DocT: In an EMS system with low intubation success or little training, a supraglottic airway is the preferred advanced airway in OHCA: it is placed faster with fewer compression pauses. Endotracheal intubation is reserved for systems with high success rates or frequent practice.
- **[DocT GT · GT8]** What is the key limitation of BVM ventilation?
  - Options: A) Risk of tracheal injury || B) Causes chest wall collapse || C) Promotes gastric inflation || D) Always requires two rescuers
  - Answer: C · Promotes gastric inflation · cohort right 100%
  - line: DocT: The main limitation of bag-valve-mask ventilation is gastric insufflation — airway pressure above ~20 cmH2O opens the lower oesophageal sphincter, risking regurgitation and aspiration. Give each breath over 1 s with just visible chest rise (~500–600 mL), two-person technique when possible.

## ACLS-42 · Give the breathing — 3 questions

- **[DocT GT · GrandT1]** A 55-year-old man collapses in a public area. Bystanders observe agonal respirations for about 90 seconds, delaying CPR. EMS initiates resuscitation with bag-valve-mask ventilation and chest compressions. Which action optimizes cerebral perfusion during CPR?
  - Options: A) Deliver 10 breaths per minute || B) Increase ventilation to 900 mL || C) Pause compressions for pulse checks || D) Avoid hyperexpansion during breaths
  - Answer: D · Avoid hyperexpansion during breaths · cohort right 31%
  - line: DocT: During CPR avoid excessive ventilation: give each breath over ~1 s with just enough volume for visible chest rise (~500–600 mL, 6–7 mL/kg). Hyperinflation raises intrathoracic pressure, cuts venous return and so coronary and cerebral perfusion; 900 mL is too much, and pulse-check pauses should stay under 10 s.
  - key_note: 10/min (A) applies only once an advanced airway is in; with BVM it is 30:2, so D is the best answer.
- **[DocT GT · PYQ]** During cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), after a patient has been successfully intubated, what is the recommended ventilation rate?
  - Options: A) 1 breath every 2 seconds || B) 1 breath every 5-6 seconds || C) 2 breaths every 30 compressions || D) 5 breaths per minute
  - Answer: B · 1 breath every 5-6 seconds

## ACLS-44 · Give the circulation — 2 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT4]** A 58-year-old man collapses and is found pulseless. High-quality chest compressions are started. Which parameter best correlates with the successful return of spontaneous circulation ROSC)?
  - Options: A) Coronary perfusion pressure || B) Systolic pressure during compressions || C) Peak arterial pressure || D) Palpable femoral pulse
  - Answer: A · Coronary perfusion pressure · cohort right 58%
  - line: DocT: Coronary perfusion pressure (aortic diastolic minus right-atrial diastolic pressure during the relaxation phase) is the best predictor of ROSC; CPP ≥15–20 mmHg is usually needed. The arterial-line diastolic >20 mmHg target is its bedside surrogate; systolic pressure and a palpable femoral pulse (often venous) do not predict ROSC.
  - key_note: Image not needed.
- **[DocT GT · GT5]** A 58-year-old man collapses and is found pulseless. High-quality chest compressions are started. Which parameter best correlates with successful return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC)?
  - Options: A) Coronary perfusion pressure || B) Systolic pressure during compressions || C) Peak arterial pressure || D) Palpable femoral pulse
  - Answer: A · Coronary perfusion pressure · cohort right 53%
  - line: DocT: Coronary perfusion pressure (aortic DIASTOLIC minus right-atrial diastolic pressure during the release phase of compressions) is the single best predictor of ROSC — ≥15 mmHg is usually needed. That is why the arterial-line target is DIASTOLIC >20 mmHg; systolic/peak compression pressure and a palpable femoral pulse (may be venous) do not reflect myocardial perfusion.
