## ACLS-03 · Give the parameters of — 1 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** A 7-year-old child collapses on the playground at school, he is unresponsive and is not breathing and has no pulse. The PT teacher initiates CPR. At what rate and depth would he deliver compressions to this child?
  - Options: A) 90 to 100 compressions per minute at a depth of about 1 inch || B) 100 to 120 compressions per minute at a depth of about 1 inch || C) 90 to 100 compressions per minute at a depth of about 2 inch || D) 100 to 120 compressions per minute at a depth of about 2 inch
  - Answer: D · 100 to 120 compressions per minute at a depth of about 2 inch · cohort right 67%
  - line: DocT: Paediatric compressions: rate 100–120/min (same as adults); depth at least one-third of the AP chest diameter, about 4 cm (1.5 in) in infants and 5 cm (2 in) in children. Adolescents use the adult 5–6 cm. Ratio 30:2 with one rescuer, 15:2 with two.

- **[DocT GT · GT4]** Which rhythm cannot support perfusion?
  - Options: A) VF || B) VT || C) AF with RVR || D) Junctional rhythm
  - Answer: A · VF · cohort right 82%
  - key_note: Repeat keys (GT5 Q150, CGT3 Q101) are also A — no disagreement.
- **[DocT GT · GT5]** Which rhythm cannot support perfusion?
  - Answer: A · Ventricular fibrillation (VF) · cohort right 100%
  - line: DocT: VF is the one rhythm that can never perfuse: chaotic ventricular activity gives no coordinated contraction, so cardiac output is zero by definition (with asystole, the other always-pulseless rhythm). VT, AF with RVR and junctional rhythm can each carry a pulse, so VT is shockable only when pulseless; with a pulse it is synchronised cardioversion or drugs.

- **[DocT GT · CGT4]** A patient with a pulse is found to be in polymorphic ventricular tachycardia. What is the most critical immediate action?

Stable polymorphic VT is treated by cause: IV magnesium if long QT (as Q53 in this paper keys), ischaemia workup, amiodarone if QT is normal. The key holds only if instability is assumed.

## ACLS-19 · For vascular access in adult — 2 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT8]** A 32-year-old male with multiple traumatic injuries is brought to the ED in hemorrhagic shock. Two attempts at peripheral IV access failed. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step to achieve rapid vascular access for resuscitation?
  - Options: A) Attempt subclavian central line || B) Insert intraosseous (IO) needle || C) Attempt internal jugular line || D) Prepare ultrasound-guided peripheral IV
  - Answer: B · Insert intraosseous (IO) needle · cohort right 81%
  - line: DocT: In haemorrhagic shock, if peripheral IV access fails after 2 attempts or 90 s, place an intraosseous needle (proximal humerus or tibia) — it takes blood, drugs and pressure infusion within a minute. Central lines take longer and risk pneumothorax in a collapsed patient.
