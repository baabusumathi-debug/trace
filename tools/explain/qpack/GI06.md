- **[DocT GT · GT2]** Which is true regarding infection prevention in cirrhosis?
  - Options: A) Only SBP needs prevention || B) Rifaximin has systemic side effects || C) Fluoroquinolones are completely safe || D) Infection often leads to ACLF
  - Answer: D · Infection often leads to ACLF · cohort right 88%
  - line: DocT: Bacterial infection is the commonest ACLF trigger (~1/3 to 44% of ACLF) and 40-60% of infections in cirrhosis progress to ACLF, so prevention covers every infection, not only SBP. Norfloxacin prophylaxis selects quinolone-resistant organisms and C. difficile and carries QT/tendon toxicity, whereas rifaximin is minimally absorbed with few systemic effects.

## ACLF-13 · Coagulation in liver disease: — 3 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** A 58-year-old man with alcoholic cirrhosis is admitted to the ICU for evaluation of melena. He is hemodynamically stable and scheduled for an elective upper GI endoscopy. His labs show haemoglobin 9.2 g/dL, platelet count 42,000/µL, INR 1.6, and fibrinogen 180 mg/dL. What is the most appropriate next step?
  - Options: A) Platelet transfusion || B) Desmopressin || C) Proceed without intervention || D) Cryoprecipitate
  - Answer: A · Platelet transfusion · cohort right 62%
  - line: DocT: Diagnostic or banding endoscopy is a low-bleeding-risk procedure: AGA 2021, EASL 2022 and AASLD 2024 advise no prophylactic platelets, FFP or cryo before it in cirrhosis, whatever the count or INR, because transfusion raises portal pressure. A platelet target (~50,000) is considered only for high-risk procedures (polypectomy, liver biopsy).
  - key_note: Key A follows the old '<50,000 before any procedure' rule (and quotes sepsis guidance). Current cirrhosis guidance makes 'proceed without intervention' (C) the better answer for a stable patient having elective endoscopy.
  - pattern: same-number: platelets 50,000 = transfuse before invasive procedures/active bleeding in sepsis and DIC, but NOT a trigger before low-risk procedures in cirrhosis.
- **[DocT GT · CGT5]** A patient with compensated cirrhosis undergoes coagulation testing. Which coagulation factor is typically elevated despite liver disease?
  - Options: A) Factor VII || B) Factor II || C) Factor VIII || D) Factor V
  - Answer: C · Factor VIII
  - line: DocT: Factor VIII (with von Willebrand factor) RISES in cirrhosis: it is made by endothelium (sinusoidal and extrahepatic) and its clearance falls, while II, V, VII, IX, X and protein C/S fall. High VIII with low protein C is one reason cirrhotics clot despite a raised INR, and a low VIII points to DIC rather than liver failure.
- **[DocT GT · GT7]** Prothrombin complex concentrates contain all of the following factors except?
  - Options: A) Factor II || B) Factor VII || C) Factor IX || D) Factor X
  - Answer: B · Factor VII · cohort right 59%
  - key_note: Card is right: modern 4-factor PCC (the one used for warfarin reversal) contains II, VII, IX and X (plus proteins C and S). Only older 3-factor PCC lacks factor VII, so key B is outdated.

## ACLF-15 · Treating HRS-AKI in the ICU: — 3 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** Which of the following factors is most predictive of poor prognosis in patients with HRS?
  - Options: A) Elevated bilirubin || B) Non-response to vasoconstrictor therapy || C) Degree of ascites || D) Elevated serum ammonia
  - Answer: B · Non-response to vasoconstrictor therapy · cohort right 78%
  - line: DocT: Non-response to terlipressin + albumin is the strongest predictor of death in HRS-AKI: non-responders survive weeks to months, and RRT is only a bridge to transplant. Bilirubin, ascites volume and ammonia are weaker outcome predictors; baseline creatinine and bilirubin predict who will respond.

- **[DocT QBank]** What is the hallmark finding on contrast-enhanced echocardiography in a patient with hepatopulmonary syndrome?
  - Options: A) Delayed contrast in the left atrium after 5 beats || B) Rapid contrast in the left atrium within 1-2 beats || C) Delayed appearance of contrast in the left atrium after 3-6 cardiac cycles || D) No contrast seen in the left heart
  - Answer: C · Delayed appearance of contrast in the left atrium after 3-6 cardiac cy · cohort right 72%
  - line: DocT: Bubble echo window - agitated saline reaching the left atrium after >=3 cardiac cycles (typically 3-6) means an intrapulmonary shunt (HPS); within <3 cycles means an intracardiac shunt (PFO/ASD). The card's '3-4 beats' is the start of the window, not its end: a bubble at beat 5 or 6 still counts as HPS.

- **[DocT GT · GrandT1]** A 58-year-old patient with chronic liver disease complains of worsening breathlessness when sitting up, which improves on lying down. What does this positional worsening of dyspnea indicate?

  - Answer: B · Platypnea · cohort right 60%
  - line: DocT: Platypnoea = breathlessness on sitting/standing relieved by lying flat; orthodeoxia = the matching fall in SaO2 (≥4–5%) when upright. ## ACLF-17 · Lungs and circulation in ACLF: — 1 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** A 60-year-old male with a history of chronic alcohol use is admitted with signs of hepatic encephalopathy. His blood pressure is 105/70 mmHg, heart rate is 100 bpm, and CVP is 22 mmHg. What is the most likely cause of his elevated CVP?
  - Options: A) Cirrhosis with portal hypertension || B) Cardiogenic shock from acute heart failure || C) Sepsis with vasodilation || D) Acute kidney injury causing fluid overload
  - Answer: A · Cirrhosis with portal hypertension · cohort right 70%
  - line: DocT: Portal hypertension does not raise the CVP — portal pressure sits upstream of the liver, and the hyperdynamic cirrhotic circulation (ACLF-17d) leaves CVP low-normal. A CVP of 22 in a cirrhotic points to the right heart or chest: portopulmonary hypertension, cirrhotic cardiomyopathy, tense ascites/hydrothorax raising intrathoracic pressure, or volume overload from AKI (option D). DocT's key A
  - key_note: Key A (portal hypertension raises CVP) is physiologically wrong: portal pressure is upstream of the liver and cirrhosis gives a low-SVR hyperdynamic state with low/normal CVP. Of the options, D (AKI with fluid overload) or a cardiac cause explains CVP 22; best real answer = look for portopulmonary HTN / cirrhotic cardiomyopathy / volume overload.
