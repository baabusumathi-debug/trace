## GUT-03 · The gut as the "motor" of multiple — 2 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT2]** A 72-year-old man with a history of coronary artery disease is admitted to the ICU after open abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) repair. On postoperative day 2, he remains intubated and on moderate-dose vasopressors. He has abdominal distension and has not passed stool or gas. A nasogastric tube is in place and draining bilious fluid. Gastric residuals are persistently high. You suspect gastrointestinal dysmotility. Which of the following best explains the mechanism of impaired intestinal motility in this patient?
  - Options: A) Activation of cholecystokinin receptors promotes bowel atony || B) Increased nitric oxide and VIP release suppress migrating motor complexes || C) Increased parasympathetic tone reduces peristalsis || D) Whey-based formulas slow down intestinal transit compared to casein
  - Answer: B · Increased nitric oxide and VIP release suppress migrating motor com... · cohort right 69%
  - line: DocT: ICU/post-operative ileus: inflammatory mediators (IL-6, nitric oxide, VIP), sympathetic overactivity, opioids and bowel handling suppress the migrating motor complex. Parasympathetic tone promotes peristalsis (it does not cause atony), and formula type (whey vs casein) is not the mechanism.
- **[DocT GT · GT2]** A 60-year-old ICU patient develops intra-abdominal sepsis after prolonged antibiotic use. Which factor most likely contributed to this infection?
  - Options: A) Increased gastric acidity || B) Strong peristalsis || C) Overgrowth of commensal flora || D) Disruption of gut mucosal barrier
  - Answer: D · Disruption of gut mucosal barrier · cohort right 80%

## GUT-06 · Causes of gastroparesis: — 1 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT1o]** A 55-year-old man with hypertension, anxiety, and type 1 diabetes mellitus recently adjusted his medication regimen and began taking clonidine. He presents now with abdominal pain and nausea. This has been associated with occasional episodes of emesis of gastric contents over the past few weeks. His weight is unchanged. His vital signs are within normal limits. On physical examination, his abdomen is distended with moderate tenderness in the epigastrium, but no rebound or guarding. His electrolytes are unremarkable and his finger stick glucose is 350 mg/dL. Upper endoscopy and CT scan show large amount of gastric contents with no evidence of mechanical obstruction. In addition to reviewing his medication list, what is the BEST next step in caring for this patient?
  - Options: A) Nasogastric decompression || B) Dietary modification and optimization of glycemic control || C) PPI treatment || D) Percutaneous gastrostomy tube for venting
  - Answer: D · Percutaneous gastrostomy tube for venting · cohort right 7%
  - key_note: Keyed D (venting gastrostomy) is wrong for a first step. Gastroparesis in type 1 DM with glucose 350 and a new alpha-2 agonist (clonidine): first-line care is dietary change (small, low-fat, low-fibre meals), tight glycaemic control (hyperglycaemia itself slows emptying), stopping the offending drug, then prokinetics. That is answer B. Venting PEG or jejunal feeding is for refractory disease only

## GUT-10 · Osmotic vs secretory diarrhoea: — 1 questions

- **[DocT GT · GrandT1]** A 72-year-old woman in the ICU with septic shock has been receiving broad-spectrum antibiotics and enteral nutrition. Over the past 24 hours, she has developed frequent watery stools. She is afebrile and hemodynamically stable. A stool test for Clostridium difficile is pending. The team is considering next steps in management. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in the management of this patient?
  - Options: A) Initiate loperamide to reduce stool frequency || B) Hold enteral nutrition and initiate total parenteral nutrition (TPN) || C) Discontinue enteral nutrition immediately until diarrhea resolves || D) Review medications and consider stopping prokinetic agents
  - Answer: D · Review medications and consider stopping prokinetic agents · cohort right 65%
  - line: DocT: First step in new ICU diarrhoea is a drug review: stop prokinetics (metoclopramide, erythromycin), laxatives, sorbitol-containing syrups and Mg salts, and test for C. difficile. Keep enteral feeding going (stopping EN or switching to TPN is wrong), and give no loperamide until C. difficile is excluded (toxic megacolon).
