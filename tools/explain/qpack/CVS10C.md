His presenting electrocardiogram demonstrates inferior ST elevations with reciprocal changes in thehigh lateral leads. Emergent coronary angiography is pursued with percutaneous coronary intervention undertaken on a subtotally occluded right coronary artery. Aspirin, ticagrelor, metoprolol, and atorvastatin are initiated. He is admitted to the intensive care unit for postintervention monitoring after repeat electrocardiogram shows resolution of previous ST segment elevations. Twelve hours later, the patient develops subacute significant shortness of breath following medication administration. The patient's oxygen saturation is 99% on room air and his physical exam is unremarkable. A repeat electrocardiogram is unchanged from postintervention.   - Answer: C · Ticagrelor · cohort right 30%

## MI-48 · Map each drug group to its target: — 3 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT2]** A 64-year-old man with recent coronary stent placement is admitted to the ICU for septic shock. He is on dual antiplatelet therapy with aspirin and clopidogrel but requires urgent surgery. Which intravenous antiplatelet agent is most appropriate as a short-term bridging therapy in this setting?
  - Options: A) Abciximab infusion || B) Tirofiban infusion || C) Cangrelor infusion || D) Eptifibatide infusion
  - Answer: C · Cangrelor infusion · cohort right 80%
  - line: DocT: Cangrelor is the only IV P2Y12 inhibitor: reversible, half-life 3-6 min, platelet function back within 30-60 min, so it is the bridge when DAPT must stop for urgent surgery after a recent stent (BRIDGE trial). Eptifibatide and tirofiban block GP IIb/IIIa, not P2Y12, and abciximab binds platelets for days.
- **[DocT GT · AIM2]** A 72-year-old man on daily aspirin 81 mg presents with gastrointestinal bleeding. Platelet count is normal, and platelet aggregation testing shows an absent response to arachidonic acid. Which mechanism best explains his bleeding?
  - Options: A) Reversible COX-1 inhibition || B) Irreversible COX-1 inhibition || C) P2Y12 receptor blockade || D) GPIIb/IIIa receptor inhibition
  - Answer: B · Irreversible COX-1 inhibition · cohort right 82%
  - line: DocT: Aspirin IRREVERSIBLY acetylates platelet COX-1 (serine 529), so thromboxane A2 production stops for the platelet's life (7–10 days); an absent aggregation response to arachidonic acid confirms it. Recovery needs new platelets (~10%/day), so platelet transfusion, not waiting hours, reverses it for urgent bleeding. NSAIDs inhibit COX-1 reversibly; P2Y12 blockers abolish the ADP response.
