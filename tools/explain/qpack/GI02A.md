## PANC-01 · Acute pancreatitis: causes. — 5 questions

- **[DocT QBank]** A 65-year-old woman undergoes ERCP for choledocholithiasis. Six hours later, she develops epigastric pain, tachycardia, and elevated amylase levels. What is the most likely diagnosis?
  - Options: A) Acute cholecystitis || B) Acute pancreatitis || C) Acute cholangitis || D) Duodenal perforation
  - Answer: B · Acute pancreatitis · cohort right 87%
  - line: DocT: Post-ERCP pancreatitis is the commonest ERCP complication (3-15%): new epigastric pain within hours with lipase/amylase >3x normal. Risk is highest with difficult cannulation, pancreatic-duct injection and young women; prevent it with rectal indomethacin or diclofenac 100 mg, a pancreatic-duct stent in high-risk cases, and generous Ringer's lactate.
- **[DocT GT · CGT6]** A 55-year-old woman with acute pancreatitis has leukocytosis, hyperglycemia, mild hyperbilirubinemia, and markedly elevated serum triglycerides with lactescent serum. Which etiology is most strongly suggested by these lipid findings?
  - Options: A)

Alcohol-induced pancreatitis || B)

Hyperlipoproteinemia || C)

Drug-induced pancreatitis || D)

Autoimmune pancreatitis
  - Answer: B · Hyperlipoproteinemia
- **[DocT GT · GT7]** All the following are associated with acute pancreatitis except-
  - Options: A) Pancreatic Cancer || B) Hyperthyroidism || C) Hypercalcemia || D) Hypertriglyceridemia
  - Answer: B · Hyperthyroidism · cohort right 66%
  - key_note: DocT explanation is an image; hyperthyroidism is not a cause (pancreatic cancer causes it by duct obstruction, not on the card list).
- **[DocT GT · AIM1]** A 40-year-old woman develops acute abdominal pain 12 hours after ERCP. Lipase is elevated, CT shows pancreatic edema. What mechanism most likely caused this?
  - Options: A) Pancreatic duct contrast toxicity || B) Alcohol-induced ductal hypertension || C) Gallstone obstruction of bile duct || D) Hypertriglyceridemia-induced autodigestion
  - Answer: A · Pancreatic duct contrast toxicity · cohort right 68%
  - line: DocT: Post-ERCP pancreatitis (3-10%) comes from papillary trauma during cannulation plus hydrostatic and chemical injury from injecting contrast into the pancreatic duct. Prevent it with rectal indomethacin or diclofenac 100 mg, aggressive Ringer's lactate and a pancreatic-duct stent in high-risk cases.
  - key_note: Mechanism is mainly mechanical/hydrostatic; "contrast toxicity" is the best of the options given.
- **[Marrow QBank]** AP with ALT 270 > AST, raised ALP and bilirubin: cause
  - Answer: A · Gallstones
  - note: ✎ Add to PANC-01: ALT > AST (ALT >150 = 3x) with raised ALP/bilirubin → gallstone; AST > ALT → alcohol; confirm with ultrasound.

- **[DocT GT · CGT2]** BISAP score includes which of the following parameters?
  - Options: A) Hematocrit || B) Pleural effusion || C) Serum calcium || D) LDH
  - Answer: B · Pleural effusion
- **[DocT GT · GT8]** A 54-year-old woman with acute pancreatitis has hemoconcentration, tachycardia, and collapsed neck veins. Which lab finding most strongly supports intravascular depletion?
  - Options: A) Elevated BUN and creatinine || B) Mild hyperbilirubinemia || C) Elevated serum amylase || D) Hypocalcemia from hypoalbuminemia
  - Answer: A · Elevated BUN and creatinine · cohort right 81%
  - line: DocT: In acute pancreatitis a raised or rising BUN (prerenal azotaemia from third-space loss) is the lab marker of intravascular depletion and predicts mortality (BUN >20 mg/dL or rising at 24 h). Amylase level has no prognostic value, and hypocalcaemia reflects saponification and low albumin, not volume.
- **[DocT GT · AIM1]** A 61-year-old patient with acute pancreatitis has a BISAP score of 4. Which outcome is most likely?
  - Options: A) Minimal risk of mortality || B) High risk of mortality || C) Moderate severity pancreatitis || D) Exclusion of organ failure
  - Answer: B · High risk of mortality · cohort right 58%
