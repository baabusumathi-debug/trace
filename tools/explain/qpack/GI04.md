## MES-06 · Risk factors and history: — 5 questions

- **[DocT GT · GT1o]** Which of the following is NOT considered a risk factor for nonocclusive mesenteric ischemia (NOMI)?
  - Options: A) Infrarenal aortic bifemoral bypass surgery (ABF) || B) Hemodialysis || C) Acute myocardial infarction || D) Burn injury
  - Answer: A · Infrarenal aortic bifemoral bypass surgery (ABF) · cohort right 30%
  - line: DocT: NOMI is splanchnic vasospasm in low-flow states: shock, high-dose vasopressors, cardiogenic shock or acute MI, post-arrest, haemodialysis (fluid removal), burns, cardiac surgery, digoxin and cocaine. Infrarenal aorto-bifemoral bypass is the exception: it causes ISCHAEMIC COLITIS of the left colon because the IMA is sacrificed, not NOMI.

- **[DocT GT · GrandT1]** The presence of portal venous gas in the setting of suspected AMI is most closely associated with which of the following clinical implications?
  - Options: A) Diagnostic of mesenteric venous thrombosis || B) Suggestive of early, reversible ischemia || C) High mortality and nearly universal need for urgent surgical intervention || D) Indicates mild ischemic colitis
  - Answer: C · High mortality and nearly universal need for urgent surgical interv... · cohort right 72%
  - line: DocT: Portal venous gas (with pneumatosis) in acute mesenteric ischaemia means transmural infarction: mortality up to ~75–86%, and it is a near-universal indication for urgent laparotomy. It is not specific for venous thrombosis and never means early reversible ischaemia.
