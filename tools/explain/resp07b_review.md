## Changes (F = factual, M = minor/wording/sourcing)

1. **PLEU-14a (F)**: implied the one-third/one-fifth split was established → now adds that the dog studies behind it (Krell & Rodarte, J Appl Physiol 1985; Dechman 1994) found the lung gives up about one-third, and the chest wall two-thirds, at TLC as well as FRC. Card's "one-fifth at TLC" flagged.
2. **PLEU-14b (F)**: "an inverted hemidiaphragm on ultrasound predicts relief" (unsourced) → PLEASE study (Muruganandan 2020): flattened/everted shape or absent/paradoxical movement independently predicted relief, whether or not the lung re-expanded.
3. **PLEU-15b (M)**: 50/200 mL figures now credited to Blackmore 1996 (meniscus ~50 mL lateral, ~200 mL PA, ~500 mL hides the diaphragm; verified).
4. **PLEU-16b (F)**: "any complex or echogenic pattern means an exudate" stated as absolute → attributed to Yang 1992 (verified), with the note that later series report occasional echogenic transudates.
5. **PLEU-16d (F)**: Leung list was incomplete (circumferential thickening, the most specific sign, was missing) → all four features, each specific (~88–100%) but individually insensitive. Also "CT density helps: transudate close to water" → density helps only for blood (≥~35 HU); transudates and exudates overlap.
6. **PLEU-17d (M)**: added Bielsa 2012 data: Light's criteria mislabel 29% of cardiac and 18% of hepatic transudates; albumin ratio <0.6 reclassifies ~77% of hepatic ones vs 62% for the gradient (verified).
7. **PLEU-19h (F)**: "sensitivity 94%, specificity 89% at about 40 IU/L" → these are pooled across thresholds in 40 Indian studies (3,524 patients); 40 IU/L was the commonest cut-off, and the authors judged it better for exclusion (verified, PubMed 27725052).
8. **PLEU-20b, trap, punch (F)**: "air or blood is the commonest cause of an eosinophilic effusion" → air or blood is the classic cause, but in modern series malignancy is the commonest single cause; tuberculous effusions rarely exceed 10% eosinophils. Trap now says parasites are rare and lists malignancy.
9. **PLEU-22a (F)**: "BTS 2023 notes 2–6 weeks is usual" could not be confirmed online → softened: optimal length not established, 2–6 weeks usual, recent trials (ODAPE, SLIM) suggest shorter courses may suffice in patients who settle without surgery.
10. **PLEU-22c (M)**: MIST1 detail added (Maskell NEJM 2005, 454 patients, no benefit in death, surgery, radiology or stay; verified).
11. **PLEU-22d (F)**: "later series a few per cent, higher with low platelets" (from memory) → Akulian 2022 (Chest, ~1,830 patients): bleeding 4.1% overall, 9.6% on therapeutic anticoagulation, 2.6% when it was withheld; half-dose tPA did not lower it. Low-platelet claim removed.
12. **PLEU-23d (F)**: "an IPC suits a short prognosis well" (unsourced) removed → BTS 2023 recommends a definitive option (talc or IPC) over repeated aspiration, especially when prognosis is better (verified).
13. **PLEU-24a (M)**: "fresh blood 30–45 HU" → "about 35 HU or more strongly suggests blood" (the figure in the sources found).
14. **PLEU-24d (M)**: reasoning for avoiding a drain with suspected aortic injury now rests on case reports of exsanguination on drainage before aortic control. Recommends CT angiography and stent graft or repair first.
15. **PLEU-26b (F)**: added Mahfood 1988: 11 of 53 cases fatal (~20%, verified), and about a third followed a short collapse or re-expansion without suction. So the risk factors are tendencies, not rules.
16. **PLEU-26c, table, trap (F)**: Lentz 2019 "prevented pneumothorax ex vacuo" → fewer pneumothoraces with manometry (0 vs 10%, all asymptomatic; 128 randomised, 124 analysed). "Manometry did not prevent oedema in trials" → it did not reduce discomfort and has not been shown to prevent oedema.
17. **SPINE step 2 (M)**: "ultrasound sees the least of all" (ambiguous) → "detects the smallest volumes".
18. **Header (M)**: the "from memory" lines are replaced by a list of what was checked.
19. **T_67b32ead c (F)**: "a second cytology sample adds about a fifth more" (unverified figure) → "a second sample adds some yield, and repeating it further adds little; a negative result warrants further investigation" (BTS 2023).
20. **T_67b32ead b (M)**: 25–50 mL cytology volume credited to BTS 2023 (verified).
21. **Top-ups header (M)**: the "from memory" lines are updated to show what was checked.

Verified and left unchanged: Light's criteria and Porcel figures, the albumin gradient (83%) and protein gradient (55%), BTS 2023 pleural infection pH/LDH/glucose bands, a small-bore (≤14 F) first drain, VATS over thoracotomy, tPA 10 mg + DNase 5 mg twice daily for 3 days with single agents and streptokinase not used, ADA by prevalence, image-guided taps, the IPC-or-talc first-line choice with IPC favoured if >25% of the lung is non-expandable, RAPID (PILOT 542 patients, 2.3/9.2/29.3%; purulence and infection source as stated), MIST2 (210 patients, −29.5 vs −17.2%, referral 4/6/39/16%, stay 11.8 vs 24.8 days, 2 pleural haemorrhages), TAPPS (330 patients, 22% vs 24% failure), TIME2 (106 patients, 0 vs 4 days, 6% vs 22%, more adverse events with IPC), AMPLE (146 patients, 10 vs 12 and 1 vs 4 days), Feller-Kopman 2007 (185 patients, 0.5%/2.2%, unrelated to volume or pressure), Balik (supine, 15°, posterior axillary line, end-expiration), Yang patterns, thoracotomy at >1500 mL or >200 mL/h for 2–4 h, 28–32 F tube, EAST (VATS within 3–7 days, no second tube, thrombolytics second line, 1500 mL in 24 h), lung abscess (3–6 weeks of a β-lactam/β-lactamase inhibitor or clindamycin; drainage criteria), amoebic empyema (rupture through the diaphragm; metronidazole; drainage).
