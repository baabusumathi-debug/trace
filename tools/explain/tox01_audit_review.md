# TOX01 audit review — 6 Nov 2026

Fixes made in tox01_learn.py. No fixes were needed in tox01_topups_learn.py.

- TOX-08 b (factual): "a gap still above about 25 after ethanol points to a toxic alcohol" changed to "a gap that stays raised after that (normal <10; about 25 remains in the classic stem)". Any residual gap >10 is abnormal; 25 is just this stem's number. Source: standard osmolal-gap teaching (Kraut, CJASN 2008); the GT1o key_note arithmetic.
- TOX-11 a (factual): "unlike other beta-blockers they can be dialysed" changed to "unlike lipophilic, protein-bound ones such as propranolol … (EXTRIP suggests it mainly with impaired kidney function)". Nadolol and acebutolol are also dialysable. Source: EXTRIP beta-blocker recommendations, Bouchard et al., Crit Care 2021;25:201 (from recall; the page could not be fetched).
- TOX-13 e (factual): removed pregnancy from the relative contraindications to HBO and added that pregnancy lowers the threshold in CO poisoning. The old text contradicted the card's own ">15% in pregnancy" indication. Source: UHMS indications; Weaver, NEJM 2009.
- TOX-12 a (minor): "avoid even labetalol" now says this is the DocT position and that evidence of harm with labetalol is weak or debated. Source: AHA 2008 cocaine statement; Richards et al., J Emerg Med 2017 (systematic review of β-blockers in cocaine toxicity).
- TOX-07 extra (minor): "toxicity begins at about 10 mg/kg" changed to "serious toxicity from about 10 mg/kg (milder effects lower)". Source: standard TCA thresholds (>10 mg/kg potentially life-threatening); Marrow key kept.
- TOX-14 c (minor): "recent consensus ranks high-dose insulin above glucagon" changed to "many toxicologists now rank …; formal consensus exists for CCBs". The 2017 expert consensus covers CCB poisoning, not β-blocker poisoning. Source: St-Onge et al., Crit Care Med 2017.

Checked and left unchanged: the GrandT1 nitroprusside-vs-nitroglycerin key is wrong (nitroprusside releases cyanide). TCA QRS ≥100 ms predicts seizures and ≥160 ms VT (Boehnert 1985). Physostigmine is contraindicated in TCA poisoning (asystole). Typical and atypical antipsychotics carry a similar risk (Ray, NEJM 2009). Isopropanol gives coma with a normal pH and needs no fomepizole. Methylene blue fails in G6PD deficiency and ascorbic acid is the alternative; the curve shifts left. Untreated pneumothorax is the only absolute HBO contraindication. Hydroxocobalamin is preferred over nitrites in smoke and shock. Ethanol ≥100 mg/dL defers fomepizole, and the fomepizole regimen is correct. PCP is a weak base and chlorpropamide a weak acid; alkalaemia is not a contraindication. EXTRIP salicylate and lithium criteria are correct. Digoxin Fab arithmetic and indications are correct. The ASRA LAST doses are correct.
