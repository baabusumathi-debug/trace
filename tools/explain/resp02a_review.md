# RESP02A review — independent fact-check (9 Nov)

## Changes
- COPD-11 a (factual): “BTS starts higher (IPAP 15, EPAP 3) and raises IPAP towards about 20 over the first half hour” → “BTS/ICS (2016) starts at an IPAP of about 15 cmH₂O (EPAP commonly 3–5) and raises it to 20–30 cmH₂O within 10–30 minutes, faster when the acidosis is worse; a full face mask is the usual first interface.” BTS gives no fixed EPAP of 3. Source: Davidson et al., BTS/ICS AHRF guideline, Thorax 2016;71 Suppl 2 (IPAP 15 → 20–30 within 10–30 min; Rec 2, full face mask first).
- COPD-12 a (factual): “BTS asks for a gas … usually at 1–2 hours” (BTS sets no interval) → BTS asks for a gas before and after starting NIV; most protocols recheck at 1–2 h and after any change; an early fall in RR and rise in pH (BTS cites within 4 h) predicts success, so the 2–4 h window confirms the trend. Source: BTS/ICS 2016 (good practice point; text on predictors of success).
- COPD-11 b (minor): Haldane share left unquantified → “about a quarter in Aubier’s studies”. Source: Aubier 1980, as summarised in Clinical Correlations 2016 and Abdo & Heunks, Crit Care 2012; V/Q worsening dominant, hypoxic drive least — kept.

## Final FLAGS
- COPD-02: Marrow asthma-ventilation key (lower RR, raise flow) is right, but its wording ‘metabolic compensation’ is wrong: HCO₃ 25 means acute, uncompensated respiratory acidosis. Card is right.
- COPD-06: Card a uses the older GOLD definition (symptoms leading to a change in medication). Since GOLD 2023 the Rome definition is used: dyspnoea and/or cough and sputum worsening over ≤14 days, often with tachypnoea/tachycardia. Consider updating; explanation gives both.
- COPD-08: Card c lists PaCO₂ >45 as a ‘severe’ criterion (older GINA table); in BTS a normal PaCO₂ is life-threatening and a raised one near-fatal. Marrow also tests PaO₂ <8 kPa as life-threatening; the watch’s BTS list could add PaO₂ <8 kPa, exhaustion, arrhythmia, hypotension. Explanation gives the BTS table.
- COPD-10: One DocT QBank key (pH 7.28, PaCO₂ 75, RR 30, anxious) says intubate; GOLD and ERS/ATS 2017 make this an NIV case. Card is right; key is wrong (repeated under COPD-12).
- COPD-11: DocT GT8 keys the Haldane effect for O₂-induced hypercapnia; physiological studies attribute most of the rise to V/Q worsening and dead space (option C). Card a’s starting pressures (6–8 over 3–5) are low against BTS/ICS 2016 (IPAP about 15, raised to 20–30 within 10–30 min); the watch already notes it, though BTS gives no fixed EPAP of 3 (3–5 is common practice). Card is not wrong.
- COPD-12: Same wrong DocT key as COPD-10 (intubate at presenting pH 7.28). NEET 2025 keys intubation at pH 6.9/PaCO₂ 110, consistent with the card’s spirit. Card is right.
