# RESP01B review — independent fact-check (8 Nov)

## Changes
- ARDS-14 c: "Both are secondary or subgroup results" left the status of the P/F ≤200 result vague → 90-day mortality was a secondary outcome and the P/F ≤200 intubation result came from a post hoc analysis; hazard ratios added (standard oxygen vs HFNC 2.01, 95% CI 1.01–3.99; NIV vs HFNC 2.50, 1.31–4.78). Source: Frat, NEJM 2015 (FLORALI); The Bottom Line and Critical Care Reviews FLORALI summaries.
- ARDS-14 trap (FLORALI): added "in a post hoc analysis" to the P/F ≤200 subgroup. Same source.
- ARDS-15 c: "Between the two thresholds, reassess in an hour or two" had a timing the source does not give → values between the failure cut-off and 4.88 are indeterminate, so the score is repeated. Source: Roca, AJRCCM 2019.
- ARDS-16 b: "[GCS] can push the score past 5 on its own" was vague → of 25 points, GCS ≤10 scores 10, more than the worst P/F (6), pH (4) or rate (4). Source: Duan, ICM 2017, Table (as reprinted in J Clin Med 2022;11:3509).
- ARDS-16 c: the early-intubation finding was loose ("patients with a high score intubated early") → among patients with HACOR >5 at 1 h who failed NIV, intubation within 12 h was associated with lower hospital mortality (66% vs 79%, p = 0.03). Source: Duan, ICM 2017 abstract.
- ARDS-17 b: "tidal volumes of 10–12 mL/kg are easy to reach" had no source → patients who failed NIV averaged about 10.6 mL/kg PBW, against 8.5 in those who succeeded. Source: Carteaux, Crit Care Med 2016.
- ARDS-17 c: "Vt >9.5 mL/kg … predicts NIV failure and death" → it predicts NIV failure, mainly when P/F is ≤200 (sensitivity about 82%, specificity 87%). The abstract reports no direct link to mortality, so death is now tied to NIV failure with late intubation. Source: Carteaux, Crit Care Med 2016.
- ARDS-22 table: the pH 7.15–7.30 row said "bicarbonate may be given if rate is 35" → bicarbonate removed from that row. The ARDSNet protocol card lists NaHCO₃ only under pH <7.15. Source: ARDSNet ventilator protocol summary card (2008).
- ARDS-23 a: "LOCO2 … stopped early with a signal of harm" → harm specified: higher 90-day mortality and cases of mesenteric ischaemia in the conservative arm. Source: Barrot, NEJM 2020.
- ARDS-26 c: "each rise of about 7 cmH₂O" → "each rise of one standard deviation (about 7 cmH₂O) … relative risk 1.41". Source: Amato, NEJM 2015.

Checked and left unchanged (correct):
- FLORALI: 310 patients, non-hypercapnic, P/F ≤300; HFNC given between NIV sessions; intubation 38/47/50%, p = 0.18.
- HFNC: about 1 cmH₂O per 10 L/min with the mouth closed; tidal volume unchanged or higher.
- Venturi mask up to about 0.6; ERS 2022 and ESICM 2023 favour HFNC over conventional oxygen.
- ROX: ≥4.88 at 2, 6 or 12 h; failure below 2.85, 3.47 and 3.85 (Roca 2019); worked example 6.1.
- HACOR: five components, >5 at 1 h, failure risk >80% (Duan 2017).
- Pendelluft direction; ginkgo-leaf sign; Dreyfuss volume vs pressure; stress = specific elastance × strain.
- ARMA: 861 patients, 31.0 vs 39.8%, plateau ≤30 vs ≤50, NNT about 11, worse early oxygenation.
- EOLIA criteria.
- Higher-PEEP trials:
  - ALVEOLI 2004, LOVS 2008 (recruitment manoeuvres, pressure control), ExPress 2008 (plateau 28–30).
  - Briel IPD meta-analysis, JAMA 2010 (benefit at P/F ≤200, non-significant trend to harm above).
  - ATS 2024: higher PEEP without lung recruitment manoeuvres in moderate–severe ARDS, against prolonged manoeuvres.
  - ART 2017: higher mortality with recruitment plus titrated PEEP.
- ARDSNet protocol:
  - Start at 8 mL/kg, down by 1 mL/kg every ≤2 h to 6; plateau ≤30 with a 0.5 s pause; minimum 4 mL/kg.
  - Initial rate set to baseline minute ventilation, not above 35.
  - pH goal 7.30–7.45; at pH 7.15–7.30 raise the rate until pH >7.30 or PaCO₂ <25 (maximum 35); below 7.15, rate 35, then tidal volume up in 1 mL/kg steps (plateau may exceed 30), NaHCO₃ allowed.
- Oxygenation: PaO₂ 55–80 or SpO₂ 88–95%; the lower PEEP/FiO₂ table.
- PBW formulae and worked examples (66 kg → about 400 mL; 52 kg → about 315 mL).
- Hypercapnia harms.
- Pressure arithmetic for the hold questions (plateau 24 = alveolar pressure; PL –5).
- EPVent-1: PL 0–10 at end-expiration, <25 at end-inspiration. EPVent-2 was negative.
- Mechanical power:
  - Formula and worked example (≈18 J/min).
  - >17 J/min associated with mortality in observational ICU cohorts (Serpa Neto, ICM 2018); 12 J/min from piglet data (Cressoni, Anesthesiology 2016).
- All cohort percentages match the qpack.

## Final FLAGS
- ARDS-14: DocT HFNC-mechanism keys ("positive pressure to maintain airway patency", "mild positive pressure") overstate HFNC's PEEP (about 1 cmH₂O per 10 L/min, mouth closed). The main mechanisms are a reliable FiO₂, dead-space washout and lower work of breathing. The keys are still the best options. Several stems put HFNO on a patient who is already ventilated. Card is right.
- ARDS-17: DocT CGT4 key says high VT on NPPV "is a marker of low lung compliance"; more precisely it marks high respiratory drive acting on a small lung. Key is the best option; card is right.
- ARDS-22: Marrow keys conflict with the ARDSNet protocol and with each other. They key "continue settings" at pH 7.27, where the protocol says raise the rate towards 35 when pH is 7.15–7.30. They key "increase rate" at pH 7.32, which is within the 7.30–7.45 goal. Card is right.
- ARDS-27: Card's "12 J/min in ARDS in some studies": this threshold comes mainly from experimental (animal) data; 17 J/min is an observational association (Serpa Neto 2018). No power threshold has been tested in a randomised trial. The explanation says so.
