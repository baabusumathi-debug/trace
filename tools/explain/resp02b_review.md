# RESP02B review — independent fact-check (9 Nov)

## Changes
- COPD-28 d (factual): mortality given only as Marrow's keyed ranges "citing GOLD" → GOLD's own figures (about 10% in-hospital for hypercapnic exacerbation with acidosis; about 40% at 1 year after discharge in those who needed ventilatory support), ICU series of ventilated patients about 25–30% in hospital (Seneff 1995: 24% overall, 30% at ≥65, 59% of ≥65 dead at 1 year), with Marrow's 10–30% / 40–60% described as spanning these. Sources: GOLD 2025 ch. 4 (exacerbations); Seneff et al., JAMA 1995;274:1852 (abstract via QxMD); NIS 2021 cohort, EMJ 2026 (28.4% in-hospital on IMV).
- COPD-28 a (factual): "ERS/ATS (2017) suggest NIV to speed weaning in hypercapnic patients and to prevent post-extubation failure in those at high risk" (blurred facilitation and prevention) → two separate conditional recommendations: NIV to facilitate weaning in hypercapnic patients (extubation to NIV before full readiness, in experienced units) and NIV to prevent post-extubation failure in high-risk patients (hypercapnic COPD the clearest group), not in non-high-risk patients; part b (against NIV for established post-extubation failure) confirmed. Source: Rochwerg et al., ERS/ATS NIV guideline, Eur Respir J 2017;50:1602426 (as summarised in secondary notes; the ERJ page itself could not be fetched).
- COPD-24 c (factual): explanation restated the card's Pseudomonas FEV₁ <50% as the only cut-off → notes that the older GOLD table uses FEV₁ <30% (with recent hospitalisation and oral steroid >10 mg/day in the last 2 weeks), while other lists use <50%; recent admission corrected to "2 days or more in the past 90 days"; mechanism sentence added. Sources: GOLD 2011–2017 antibiotic table; Sanford Health AECB guideline 2014 (FEV₁ <50%, ≥2 days in 90 days, ≥4 courses/year).
- COPD-22 a (factual): salbutamol "2.5–5 mg, repeated every 15–20 min or continuously" → GINA/NAEPP 2.5–5 mg every 20 min in the first hour; BTS/SIGN 5 mg oxygen-driven every 15–30 min, or continuous 5–10 mg/hour. Source: BTS/SIGN acute asthma in hospital algorithm (NHS Scotland Right Decisions); GINA 2024.
- COPD-22 b (factual): hydrocortisone dose left at the card's 200 mg → adds BTS/SIGN hydrocortisone 100 mg IV 6-hourly and prednisolone for at least 5 days. Source: as above.
- COPD-18 d (minor): "Tuxen found that hypotension and barotrauma became more likely" → complications (chiefly hypotension and barotrauma) in about two-thirds with Vei ≥1.4 L (≈20 mL/kg) and none below. Source: Tuxen et al., Am Rev Respir Dis 1992;146:1136 (abstract: 0/5 vs 11/17).
- COPD-18 c (minor): "breathe out for 20–60 seconds" → "during an apnoea of 20–60 seconds" (confirmed). Source: Critical Care review figure (cc1451, fig. 4): total exhaled volume over 20–60 s apnoea.
- COPD-29 e (fidelity): bold restatement "a safe pH and his baseline PaCO₂" did not match card's "Normal pH" → "the target is pH (his normal pH), not a normal PaCO₂", with a line explaining that his normal pH comes from his baseline PaCO₂ and that 7.35–7.45 is not forced.
- COPD-20 c (minor): "700 mL is above 10 mL/kg in most adults" → "about 10 mL/kg of predicted body weight or more in most adults".
- COPD-26 c (minor): "converted to fat, which produces more CO₂ for each unit of oxygen" → lipogenesis produces more CO₂ than O₂ used (RQ above 1).
- COPD-23 trap (minor): "Antibiotics need purulent sputum, which the stem does not state" → need purulence or ventilatory support; a productive cough is not purulence; they become indicated once he needs NIV. Source: GOLD 2025.

Checked and left unchanged: Tuxen minute ventilation <115 mL/kg/min; peak/plateau logic; permissive hypercapnia (pH ≥7.2, gradual rise, ICP contraindication); static vs dynamic PEEPi; waterfall/80% PEEPi; Mg 2 g over 20 min (GINA; BTS 1.2–2 g), calcium-blocking mechanism; aminophylline not recommended (GINA); GOLD prednisone 40 mg × 5 days (REDUCE), oral = IV; GOLD antibiotics 5–7 days; heliox density (≈1/3 of air at 80:20), viscosity slightly above air; hypercapnia cerebral vasodilatation vs pulmonary vasoconstriction; weaning-induced pulmonary oedema physiology. Clinical-judgment figures (35%/79%) are the card's and could not be traced to a source online; kept as card fact, not elaborated.
