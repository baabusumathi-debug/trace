# ACS I and ACS II addendum (read after P12_BRIEF.md)

- Everywhere the brief says S1, read your packet's code: **A1** (ACS I — recognising the infarct) or **A2** (ACS II — treating the infarct). Input `/home/claude/p12/<A1|A2>_pack.json`, output `/home/claude/p12/<A1|A2>_draft.json`.
- **Full length is wanted.** Naven asked for the full depth; there is no time target. Still: fact cards with no bank questions get 30–90 seconds, and the time goes to the cards with bank questions.
- **Where the series stands.** Shock I (reading the failing heart through filling, pump and squeeze) and Shock II (the drugs and devices that push on each number) come before ACS I. Shock II ended: "Almost every patient in this packet had an infarct… Before the shock, there's the infarct. How do you recognise it?" You may skim `/home/claude/p12/S2_draft.json` for tone. ACS I answers "how do you recognise it"; ACS II answers "now treat it", and its shock section (P12-405) calls back to Shock II rather than re-teaching inotropes and devices.
  - ACS I's last step (bridge null) ends on a teaser into ACS II: you've recognised the infarct; now the clock starts.
  - ACS II's last step (bridge null) ends on a teaser into the Rhythm packet: the infarct scar and the ischaemic heart are where arrhythmias come from; next, how to read a fast or a slow rhythm.
- **Section openings: write them** for every section EXCEPT the first (P12-301 for ACS I, P12-401 for ACS II), where Claude writes the packet's opening. About 45–75 seconds each (600–1,100 spoken characters): where we are in the arc, what this section answers, which trap to listen for, then a hand-off into its first card. Do not write any `close`; Claude writes the packet's close on the last section.
- **GT misses** are in `gt_misses` in the pack: these are what Naven actually got wrong. Teach each clearly where it belongs, and collect each in `traps` with its GT number in `asked`. Where the pack says "add a new card", add it in `new_cards` and as a step right after the card it belongs with (keep each section at 8 steps or fewer). New-card codes: ACS I uses **V12-004** onward; ACS II uses **V12-010** onward (so the two packets never clash). In a step, a new card is referenced by its bare code (e.g. `"card": "V12-004"`).
- **Current standards matter here** and the cards are mixed in age: the ESC 2023 ACS guideline, the 2025 ACC/AHA ACS guideline, the Fourth Universal Definition (2018) and the cards' "UDMI 2026" material (deck CVS10E and MI-11/MI-14). Verify the UDMI 2026 claims on the web before teaching them as fact; if you cannot confirm that a 2026 universal definition exists with those changes, teach the 2018 definition as the exam answer and add a `card_flags` entry. NEET keys are usually the older, established wording: when a newer guideline changes the answer, teach both, the exam answer first.
- ECG: no 3D model exists for ECGs. Use `{{ }}` boxes for lead groups and thresholds. Echo appears only at MI-35 (wall motion by artery): one reminder line and a `model` (PSAX-PM or A4C, highlight LV and septum); do not re-teach echo.
- Drill: 15–25 items for the packet.
