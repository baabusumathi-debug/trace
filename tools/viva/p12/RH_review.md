## Sources used
- Imazio 2026 review of ESC 2025 (mp.pl/paim): definite pericarditis = compatible presentation plus more than one additional criterion; possible = one; criteria are rub, ECG, raised CRP, new or worsening effusion, CMR inflammation. Aspirin 750–1000 mg three times daily, ibuprofen 600–800 mg three times daily, indomethacin 25–50 mg three times daily, 1–2 weeks then taper; colchicine 0.5 mg twice daily (once daily under 70 kg), about 3 months after a first episode, at least 6 for recurrence; anti-IL-1 Class I.
- Trends Cardiovasc Med 2026 comparative review (arts.units.it): same definite/possible scheme; IMPS umbrella term; ESC 2025 fulminant = haemodynamic instability needing inotropes or MCS; early CMR ideally within 1–2 weeks.
- Rev Esp Cardiol 2025 editorial: IMPS; fulminant reserved for cardiogenic shock; IL-1 inhibitors IIa C for refractory cases, I A when CRP raised; EMB also when there is no response to therapy; steroids not first line.
- JCMR 2026 editorial: CMR Class I B; follow-up CMR within 6 months; anti-IL-1 IIa C with CMR inflammation.
- PubMed 10502224 (Mercé 1999): 110 patients; collapse figures 90/65, 68/66, 60/90, 45/92, venous flow 75/91. They match card CVS-26 and the lesson.
- ACC summary of ESC 2023 IE: emergency under 24 h, urgent 3–5 days; vegetation 10 mm or more after one or more emboli, or with another surgical indication; no 30 mm cut-off; rifampicin only with foreign material; no aminoglycoside in staphylococcal NVE; daptomycin 10 mg/kg; TEE before oral step-down; right-sided indications (vegetation over 20 mm).
- MSD table of Duke-ISCVID 2023: major and minor criteria, with PET-CT within 3 months and a new murmur on auscultation (when echo is unavailable) as new minor groups.
