# DR review (Cardiac drugs and the poisoned heart): independent fact-check, 23 Nov 2026

The packet is accurate overall. I checked every dose, threshold, trial and guideline position the brief listed and found no keyed answer wrong. The edits below make the text more precise or bring it in line with current guidelines. JSON is valid. All caps are met: spoken length, paragraphs, takeaways, boxes and bridges. The only digit-bearing spoken token is CYP3A4, which is an abbreviation. The step order is unchanged.

## Verified as correct (no change)

**Anticoagulant reversal**
- Idarucizumab: 5 g given as 2 × 2.5 g, binding affinity about 350 times that of thrombin, and adverse effects including hypokalaemia.
- Andexanet: decoy mechanism.
- ANNEXA-I: better haemostasis and roughly double the thrombosis.
- US sales of Andexxa ended on 22 Dec 2025 after an FDA safety communication (AABB, 23 Dec 2025; final thrombosis rates 14.6% vs 6.9%).
- 4F-PCC 50 IU/kg for factor Xa inhibitors.
- Warfarin: PCC 25/35/50 U/kg by INR band, plus vitamin K 10 mg IV.
- In ICH, AHA 2022 gives 10–20 IU/kg for INR 1.3–1.9 and 25–50 IU/kg for INR ≥2. It also gives vitamin K as 5–10 mg IV, and the 10 mg in the packet sits within that range.
- FFP only if PCC is unavailable.
- Protamine for UFH: 1 mg per 100 U given in the last 2–3 h, maximum 50 mg.
- Protamine for enoxaparin: 1 mg/mg under 8 h, 0.5 mg/mg at 8–12 h, about 60% reversal.
- Dalteparin: 1 mg protamine per 100 anti-Xa units.
- Dabigatran can be removed by dialysis; the factor Xa inhibitors cannot. Charcoal within 2 h of a DOAC dose.
- Non-bleeding high INR: CHEST rule (hold doses for INR 4.5–10; oral vitamin K for INR >10).

**ICH**
- PATCH showed harm from platelet transfusion. AHA 2022 rates routine platelets for aspirin-associated ICH as Class 3 harm, and platelets with emergency surgery as Class 2b.
- BP: target 140 within a range of 130–150 (COR 2b); lowering below 130 is Class 3 harm; aim for smooth control.
- INTERACT2 and ATACH-2 results as described (ATACH-2 showed more renal adverse events).
- Cerebellar evacuation criteria: ≥15 mL, deterioration, brainstem compression or hydrocephalus.

**Toxicology**
- TCA: Boehnert and Lovejoy 1985 (QRS ≥100 ms gave 34% seizures; ≥160 ms gave 50% ventricular arrhythmias).
- Bicarbonate 1–2 mEq/kg to pH 7.45–7.55. AHA 2023 rates bicarbonate COR 1 for TCAs and 2a for other sodium-channel blockers. Avoid Ia/Ic drugs and physostigmine.
- Digoxin, Bismuth 1973: 91 patients; all with K >5.5 died and all with K <5 survived.
- Fab: 0.5 mg digoxin per vial; empirical 10–20 vials acute, 3–6 chronic; indications as stated (AHA 2023 COR 1).
- β-blocker and CCB poisoning (AHA 2023):
  - High-dose insulin COR 1 for both; vasopressors COR 1.
  - Calcium 2a in CCB poisoning.
  - Glucagon 2a for β-blocker poisoning and 2b for CCB.
  - Lipid: Class 3 for both.
  - VA-ECMO 2a.
  - Dialysis 2b for atenolol and sotalol.
- High-dose insulin dosing: 1 U/kg bolus, then 1–10 U/kg/h, with dextrose.
- Calcium dosing: CaCl₂ 10–20 mL, or gluconate at three times the volume.
- Glucagon: 3–10 mg in poisoning; 1–5 mg in anaphylaxis on a β-blocker.
- Cocaine:
  - benzodiazepines first;
  - IV labetalol is about 7:1 β:α;
  - bicarbonate for a wide QRS;
  - no succinylcholine (hyperkalaemia, and plasma cholinesterase is shared);
  - nitrates, CCB or phentolamine are AHA 2023 COR 2a.
- LAST (ASRA 2020):
  - doses: 1.5 mL/kg bolus over 2–3 min, 0.25 mL/kg/min infusion, 12 mL/kg ceiling, repeat bolus once or twice and double the rate;
  - adrenaline ≤1 µg/kg;
  - observe 4–6 h after a cardiovascular event, 2 h after a CNS event;
  - Weinberg 1998 rat study and the 2006 first clinical case.

## Unverified or left as is
- HIET potassium: the packet says "replace below about three". Many protocols use 2.5–2.8, so "about three" is on the conservative side. I left it.
- Andexanet low-dose and high-dose regimens (400 mg + 4 mg/min vs 800 mg + 8 mg/min) are not in the packet. Nothing is wrong; they are just absent. INTERACT3 is not mentioned either.
- Bank-derived keys I did not check independently: NEET 2025 cocaine and ICH, synthetic cannabinoids being least likely to cause rhabdomyolysis, the DocT key of 16 vials, and Marrow's FFP key.
- Consistency: traps refer to the new card as "TOX01|V12-020", but the step uses the bare "V12-020".

## Final card flags (6)
- CAR-21: andexanet withdrawn in the US (Dec 2025).
- NEURO-92: andexanet; the enoxaparin row has no time rule.
- NEU-09: the 3 cm criterion is the older one; AHA 2022 uses ≥15 mL.
- TOX-14: the explanation says 0.5–1 U/kg/h (confirmed in the pack); glucagon is ranked above HDI, although AHA 2023 makes HDI COR 1 and glucagon 2a.
- CAR-14: lipid for CCB is Class 3 under AHA 2023.
- TOX-15 (new): the bank keys lipid for propranolol; AHA 2023 says it is not likely to be beneficial.
