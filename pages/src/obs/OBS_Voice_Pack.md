# OBS01 · Physiology, recognition, airway, organ support
Before you start (fold on HOME): The fetus has no oxygen supply of its own: what reaches it is maternal arterial oxygen content multiplied by uterine blood flow, and the uterine vessels are already fully dilated and cannot autoregulate, so any fall in maternal blood pressure, cardiac output or saturation is passed straight on. Fetal carbon dioxide leaves by diffusing down a gradient into maternal blood, which is why the mother normally runs a low PaCO₂. From about 20 weeks the uterus is large enough to press on the inferior vena cava and aorta when she lies flat. Treating the mother well is the treatment of the fetus; almost nothing in this deck asks you to trade one against the other.

## OBS01 · Concept 1 — Who gets sick, and why
NAV: on HOME tap the tile under OBS01 labelled "Concept 1: Who gets sick, and why". Tabs: Mimics 1 · Traps 3 · Cards 0 · Asked 4.
Summary line (italic): Pregnant and postpartum women are a small share of ICU admissions, the causes are either diseases of pregnancy itself or ordinary diseases made worse by it, and most of the deaths are preventable.

### Core (numbered boxes)
1. Obstetric patients are 1–4.5% of ICU admissions; mortality 5–20%; 70% of maternal deaths are preventable.
2. Commonest reason for ICU: pre-eclampsia/eclampsia (~75%), then medical disease, then haemorrhage.
3. Diseases of pregnancy: pre-eclampsia, HELLP, fatty liver, amniotic fluid embolism, peripartum cardiomyopathy, obstetric haemorrhage, chorioamnionitis/septic abortion, magnesium toxicity, tocolytic pulmonary oedema, OHSS.
4. Diseases worsened by pregnancy: ARDS, aspiration, pulmonary embolism, urosepsis, hepatitis E, varicella pneumonia, falciparum malaria, H1N1, cerebral venous thrombosis, DKA, valve disease, pulmonary hypertension.
5. Why it is hard: altered physiology, pregnancy-specific diseases, the fetus, and clinicians' unfamiliarity.
6. The obstetric lethal triad is hypotension, hypoxia, anaemia: the three determinants of oxygen delivery to mother and fetus.
7. Heart disease (top-up): NYHA III–IV mothers are ~10% of cardiac patients but ~85% of cardiac deaths; pregnancy is contraindicated in PAH, EF <30%, severe MS, symptomatic AS, Marfan aorta >45 mm.

## OBS01 · Concept 2 — The pregnant body’s new normal
NAV: on HOME tap the tile under OBS01 labelled "Concept 2: The pregnant body’s new normal". Tabs: Mimics 4 · Traps 5 · Cards 3 · Asked 16.
Summary line (italic): By term cardiac output and blood volume are up by nearly half, resistance and diastolic pressure are down, she hyperventilates to a PaCO₂ of 30 with a bicarbonate of 20, and creatinine and haemoglobin are low, so ‘normal’ adult values can mean disease.

### Core (numbered boxes)
1. Heart: cardiac output +40–50% (stroke volume first, then heart rate +15–25%); blood volume +45%; SVR −20%; diastolic falls mid-pregnancy; CVP unchanged; O₂ consumption +30–40%.
2. Lung: tidal volume +40%, FRC −20%, vital capacity unchanged; progesterone drives the extra ventilation.
3. Gas: pH 7.40–7.47, PaCO₂ ~30, HCO₃ 18–22: a compensated respiratory alkalosis. A PaCO₂ of 40 in a breathless woman means she is tiring, and the low bicarbonate leaves less buffer.
4. Blood: plasma rises more than red cells → dilutional Hb ~10.5–11 with normal MCV and ferritin. Fibrinogen 4–6 g/L: hypercoagulable, so ≤2 g/L is already DIC.
5. Kidney: GFR +50%, creatinine 0.4–0.8 → a creatinine of 1.0 is abnormal.
6. Gut: lax lower oesophageal sphincter and slow transit → aspiration risk.
7. Aortocaval compression from ~20 weeks: lying flat, the uterus squeezes the IVC (less preload) and aorta → hypotension and poor placental flow. Fix: left uterine displacement, a wedge under the right hip; manual displacement in arrest.
8. Heart sounds: S3, loud split S1 and a soft ejection murmur are normal; any diastolic murmur, fixed split S2 or S4 is disease.

### Traps tab (in screen order)
1. [OBS-03] Wrong option: “Cardiac output in labour rises 30–50%” → Why: That is the antenatal figure on the card. DocT keys 60–80% for labour (only 41% right); most chose the antenatal range. Standard texts put about +50% in the second stage and the 60–80% peak immediately after delivery, so when a stem says labour or just delivered, pick the highest range offered.
2. [OBS-03] Wrong option: “Loud S1” as the heart sound of pregnancy → Why: A loud, widely split S1 does occur, but the keyed answer is the third heart sound, from rapid filling of a volume-loaded ventricle (50% right). The two options that are never normal are a diastolic murmur and a fixed split S2.
3. [OBS-03] Wrong option: “Only progesterone lowers vascular resistance” → Why: Progesterone, relaxin and nitric oxide all contribute; ‘all of the above’ is keyed (82%).
4. [OBS-03] Wrong option: “Blood pressure rises in pregnancy because volume rises 45%” → Why: The volume fills a dilated circuit; pressure falls in mid-pregnancy and only returns to baseline at term. Of cardiac output, stroke volume, heart rate and blood pressure, only blood pressure decreases (84%).
5. [OBS-03] Wrong option: “The diastolic dip is due to increased cardiac output or heart rate” → Why: A higher output would raise pressure. Pressure falls because resistance falls more than flow rises (92% right).

## OBS01 · Concept 3 — Recognising deterioration with shifted thresholds
NAV: on HOME tap the tile under OBS01 labelled "Concept 3: Recognising deterioration with shifted thresholds". Tabs: Mimics 3 · Traps 5 · Cards 0 · Asked 4.
Summary line (italic): Because her baseline has moved, the obstetric early warning chart and the obstetric qSOFA use their own cut-offs: systolic 90 or less, respiratory rate 25 or more, anything other than alert.

### Core (numbered boxes)
1. Her baseline has moved (lower BP, faster breathing), so adult thresholds miss her.
2. MEOWS is a colour-band chart: 1 red or 2 yellow = urgent review; nothing is added up (unlike NEWS).
3. MEOWS red triggers: RR <10 or >30, SBP <90 or >180, HR <40 or >120, temperature <35 or >38, responds to pain only.
4. Obstetric qSOFA (sepsis screen): SBP ≤90, RR ≥25, anything other than alert.
5. Standard qSOFA is SBP ≤100, RR ≥22, GCS <15: in pregnancy the BP cut-off moves down and the RR cut-off moves up.

## OBS01 · Concept 4 — Small reserve, difficult airway
NAV: on HOME tap the tile under OBS01 labelled "Concept 4: Small reserve, difficult airway". Tabs: Mimics 3 · Traps 5 · Cards 2 · Asked 6.
Summary line (italic): A swollen, friable airway, a small oxygen store, a high oxygen use and a full stomach make intubation harder and desaturation faster, so the plan limits attempts and puts oxygenation ahead of the tube.

### Core (numbered boxes)
1. The view is harder: airway oedema, mucosal engorgement, friable capillaries (the GT answer), big breasts, weight → intubation 2.5–3× harder.
2. The time is shorter: FRC −20% (small store) + O₂ use +30–40% (fast drain) → she desaturates 'despite adequate pre-oxygenation'.
3. The stomach is full: lax sphincter, slow transit → rapid sequence induction.
4. Prepare: tube 6.5–7.0, short-handled laryngoscope, ramped position, full pre-oxygenation.
5. Plan (OAA/DAS): mask ventilation allowed at <20 cmH₂O; 2 attempts at intubation (+1 by an expert); then declare failed intubation.
6. Failed intubation → oxygenate: supraglottic airway (max 2 attempts) or mask → then decide proceed or wake.
7. Cannot oxygenate → CICO: 100% O₂, full paralysis, front-of-neck access through the cricothyroid membrane.

Red box "Key vs guideline":
- NEET 2025 recall keys emergency cricothyrotomy after two failed attempts with difficult mask ventilation. Guideline (OAA/DAS): difficult-but-possible ventilation → supraglottic airway; front of neck only when oxygenation fails (CICO). Pick the key if it is the only CICO-type option.

## OBS01 · Concept 5 — Supporting the mother, protecting the fetus
NAV: on HOME tap the tile under OBS01 labelled "Concept 5: Supporting the mother, protecting the fetus". Tabs: Mimics 4 · Traps 5 · Cards 3 · Asked 11.
Summary line (italic): Organ support is the same as in any adult with a few fetal adjustments: uterus off the vena cava, saturation 95% or more, no permissive hypercapnia, lines above the diaphragm, steroids for the fetal lung, and no necessary scan withheld.

### Core (numbered boxes)
1. Organ support is the same as any adult, with a few changes for the fetus.
2. Ventilation: Vt 6 mL/kg PBW, plateau <30; SpO₂ ≥95% (PaO₂ >70) because the fetus sits on the steep part of its curve.
3. No permissive hypercapnia: keep PaCO₂ near 30–32, since maternal CO₂ acidifies the fetus. Prone is allowed; refractory → VV-ECMO; delivery may help the mother.
4. Circulation: two large-bore cannulae, lines above the diaphragm (avoid femoral), uterus displaced; shock gets noradrenaline as in any adult.
5. Fetal lung: betamethasone 12 mg IM × 2, 24 h apart (before 34 weeks).
6. Imaging is never withheld: chest X-ray <0.1 mGy, CTPA 0.01–0.66 mGy, abdominal/pelvic CT 10–50 mGy; no fetal harm expected below 50 mGy.
7. Asthma (top-up): treat exactly as outside pregnancy (SABA, ICS, steroids, IV Mg); hypoxia harms the fetus more than any drug.

Red box "Key vs guideline":
- DocT GT keys tidal volume 8–9 mL/kg for ARDS in pregnancy (3% right). Correct practice: 6 mL/kg PBW, plateau <30; the real pregnancy change is no permissive hypercapnia and SpO₂ ≥95%.
- Phenylephrine is the bare answer to 'vasopressor of choice in pregnancy' (spinal hypotension at caesarean). In septic shock, noradrenaline first, as in any adult.

# OBS02A · Hypertensive disorders, eclampsia, HELLP and fatty liver
Before you start (fold on HOME): Normal pregnancy lowers blood pressure in the first half, because systemic vascular resistance falls, and raises plasma volume by almost half, so a pressure of 140/90 in the second half is abnormal for a pregnant woman even though it looks mild. The spiral arteries that feed the placenta are normally remodelled into wide, low-resistance channels by about 20 weeks, which is why 20 weeks is the dividing line in every definition in this deck. The placenta has no autoregulation, so its flow follows maternal pressure; this is why blood pressure is lowered in a controlled way and never to normal. Doses and cut-offs here follow ACOG’s 2020 bulletin, which the examiners’ keys use, with ISSHP and NICE where they differ.

## OBS02A · Concept 1 — Naming the hypertensive disorder
NAV: on HOME tap the tile under OBS02A labelled "Concept 1: Naming the hypertensive disorder". Tabs: Mimics 3 · Traps 5 · Cards 2 · Asked 7.
Summary line (italic): The 20-week line separates chronic from pregnancy-induced hypertension, and proteinuria or any organ dysfunction turns gestational hypertension into pre-eclampsia, with 160/110 or organ injury marking severe disease.

### Core (numbered boxes)
1. Hypertension = ≥140/90 on two readings 4 h apart.
2. Found before 20 weeks (or still there >12 weeks after delivery) → chronic.
3. New after 20 weeks, no proteinuria, no organ dysfunction → gestational.
4. After 20 weeks + proteinuria ≥300 mg/24 h OR any organ feature → pre-eclampsia. Organ features: platelets <100,000, creatinine >1.1 or doubled, transaminases ×2, pulmonary oedema, cerebral or visual symptoms. Proteinuria is no longer required.
5. Chronic hypertension that gains proteinuria or severe features → superimposed pre-eclampsia.
6. Severe = ≥160/110 or organ injury; the amount of proteinuria never grades severity.
7. Pre-eclampsia + seizure → eclampsia.

## OBS02A · Concept 2 — A sick placenta injures endothelium
NAV: on HOME tap the tile under OBS02A labelled "Concept 2: A sick placenta injures endothelium". Tabs: Mimics 1 · Traps 4 · Cards 1 · Asked 4.
Summary line (italic): Shallow placentation leaves an ischaemic placenta that releases anti-angiogenic factors, and the resulting endothelial dysfunction explains the hypertension, the proteinuria, the leak and the low platelets.

### Core (numbered boxes)
1. Shallow trophoblast invasion → the spiral arteries stay narrow and muscular.
2. The under-perfused placenta becomes ischaemic.
3. It releases sFlt-1 and soluble endoglin, which are anti-angiogenic.
4. These cause systemic endothelial dysfunction, the root of every sign.
5. Kidney: glomerular endotheliosis → proteinuria. Liver: microcirculatory failure → HELLP. Brain: oedema → headache, PRES, stroke. Vessels: high SVR, angiotensin-sensitive, contracted plasma volume.
6. Risk: primigravida, diabetes, chronic hypertension, BMI >30, cardiovascular disease. Prevention: low-dose aspirin, calcium.

## OBS02A · Concept 3 — Lower pressure, restrict fluid, deliver
NAV: on HOME tap the tile under OBS02A labelled "Concept 3: Lower pressure, restrict fluid, deliver". Tabs: Mimics 4 · Traps 5 · Cards 6 · Asked 18.
Summary line (italic): Delivery is the only cure, so until then severe pressure is lowered to prevent stroke, fluid is held to 80 mL/hour, and the timing of delivery is set by gestation and by which organ is failing.

### Core (numbered boxes)
1. The only cure is delivery. Until then there are two separate jobs: the antihypertensive prevents stroke, magnesium prevents seizures.
2. Treat ≥160/110 within 30–60 min: IV labetalol 10–20 mg, then 20–80 mg every 10–30 min (max 300 mg).
3. Asthma, bradycardia or heart block → hydralazine 5 mg IV, or oral nifedipine 10–20 mg. No ACE inhibitors/ARBs; avoid atenolol; nitroprusside last.

## OBS02A · Concept 4 — Eclampsia and magnesium
NAV: on HOME tap the tile under OBS02A labelled "Concept 4: Eclampsia and magnesium". Tabs: Mimics 4 · Traps 5 · Cards 3 · Asked 13.
Summary line (italic): A seizure in pregnancy is eclampsia until proven otherwise and is treated and prevented with magnesium, whose toxicity is tracked at the bedside by the knee jerk, the respiratory rate and the urine output.

### Core (numbered boxes)
1. A seizure in pregnancy is eclampsia until proven otherwise; 20% had no prior pre-eclampsia. Antepartum 53%, intrapartum 19%, postpartum 28%.
2. The fit lasts 60–90 s; fetal bradycardia usually recovers, so stabilise the mother first.
3. Airway, left lateral, prevent injury, then MgSO₄ 4–6 g IV over 15–20 min, then 1–2 g/h until 24 h after delivery or the last fit.
4. Another fit on magnesium → 2 g more magnesium, not a benzodiazepine first.
5. Toxicity ladder (mg/dL): therapeutic 5–9 → >9 knee jerk lost → >12 breathing stops → >30 cardiac arrest. Antidote: calcium gluconate 1 g IV.
6. Bedside monitoring: knee jerk, RR, urine output. Renal impairment: same load, lower maintenance.
7. How it works: cerebral vasodilatation, NMDA block, less ACh release (so it potentiates NMBs). It is not an antihypertensive.
8. Evidence: Magpie (~10,000, prevention): eclampsia −58%. Collaborative Eclampsia Trial (treatment): magnesium beat diazepam and phenytoin.

# OBS02B · Haemorrhage, AFE, sepsis, heart, arrest, OHSS
Before you start (fold on HOME): By term the mother’s blood volume has risen by about 45% and her cardiac output by 30–50%, and the uterus receives roughly 700 mL of blood a minute, so obstetric bleeding is fast but her pulse and pressure hide it for a long time. Pregnancy is also a clotting state: fibrinogen doubles to 4–6 g/L, so a ‘normal’ adult value is low for her. Lying flat after 20 weeks, the uterus presses on the vena cava and aorta and cuts venous return, which matters in every collapse. OBS02A covered the physiology and hypertensive disease; this deck covers the emergencies in which she bleeds, collapses or arrests.

Summary line (italic): A term mother has about 45% more blood and a fibrinogen of 4–6 g/L, so her pressure holds until 1–1.5 L has gone and a fibrinogen under 2 g/L is already a warning.

## OBS02B · Concept 4 — Sudden collapse: embolism and sepsis
NAV: on HOME tap the tile under OBS02B labelled "Concept 4: Sudden collapse: embolism and sepsis". Tabs: Mimics 3 · Traps 5 · Cards 3 · Asked 12.
Summary line (italic): Collapse with hypoxia in labour followed by bleeding from every puncture site is amniotic fluid embolism, an inflammatory reaction to fetal material that is treated by support, delivery and blood products.

### Core (numbered boxes)
1. Amniotic fluid embolism: in labour, at delivery or within 30 min: sudden hypoxia → hypotension/arrest → seizures → DIC (bleeding from IV sites).
2. Incidence 2–6 per 100,000; mortality ~20% now (older series up to 85%).
3. Mechanism (the exam answer): a systemic inflammatory, anaphylactoid response to fetal material; not mechanical, not IgE. Phase 1: pulmonary vasospasm, RV failure, hypoxia. Phase 2: LV failure, DIC.
4. Diagnosis is clinical, by exclusion. Treat: multi-organ support, massive transfusion + TXA, RV support, emergency delivery; arrest → resuscitative delivery by 5 min; VA-ECMO as rescue.
5. Sepsis sources specific to pregnancy: chorioamnionitis, pyelonephritis (progesterone dilates the ureters), endometritis, pneumonia.

Summary line (italic): Peripartum cardiomyopathy is new heart failure with an ejection fraction under 45% around delivery, and a pregnant arrest is run as standard ACLS plus uterine displacement, access above the diaphragm and delivery by 5 minutes.

### Core (numbered boxes)
1. Peripartum cardiomyopathy: heart failure from the last month of pregnancy to 5 months postpartum, no other cause, EF <45%; about 1 in 3,500.
2. Before delivery: β-blocker, diuretic, hydralazine + nitrates, digoxin; no ACE inhibitor/ARB (allowed after delivery). Very low EF: bromocriptine and anticoagulation.
3. Persistent LV dysfunction = poor prognosis; it recurs, so counsel on contraception.
4. Arrest: standard ACLS (compressions, energy, drug doses unchanged) + continuous manual left uterine displacement, IV above the diaphragm, expert airway, 1 breath every 6 s.
5. On magnesium? Stop it, give calcium. Detach fetal monitors, call the neonatal team.
6. Fundus at or above the umbilicus → resuscitative delivery by 5 min, done for the mother. Causes A–H: Anaesthetic, Bleeding, Cardiovascular, Drugs, Embolic, Fever, General, Hypertension.
